TypeSafe's manifesto and ARCS agree on how to build. They disagree on one thing, and the gap is where privacy lives.

TypeSafe argues that today's models are smart enough, that AI should be a judgment primitive inside ordinary code, and that safety means parts you can inspect, test and constrain. ARCS already runs on those rules. The one place they part is the human: TypeSafe wants AI that runs with no one present, and ARCS will not let a machine overrule a person or touch their data without a declared door. This page maps the match, names the split, and lists the privacy carve-outs that any use of TypeSafe inside ARCS has to respect.

split

Who is in the loop

TypeSafe wants the human out of the loop. ARCS keeps the human in charge of their own preferences and of every irreversible door.

The two fit once the line is drawn by reversibility. Judgment that can be undone and touches only our own or public text can run in the background with no one watching: routing a message, typing a blocker, checking a claim against its source. Anything that decides for a person, acts on their behalf, or moves their data waits for a declared human decision.

If we borrow TypeSafe's framing without stating this line, ARCS reads as agreeing that the human should leave. It does not.

What a privacy-preserving ARCS keeps out of TypeSafe's reach

Each line below is a rule we already have, or one the October proposal recommended. TypeSafe's customer agreement, as summarized, lets it keep customer data indefinitely to derive telemetry and use that telemetry to improve its products. Zero data retention is offered only to enterprise customers through sales. Those terms decide most of this list.

C1

Hosted calls take public text only

Published posts and public pages may go. Canon, ADRs, proposals, source code, lore drafts, blameless entries and anything naming a counterpart may not.

Anonymous sessions stay serverless

Anonymous use stores nothing server-side. A third-party call that keeps the text would break that promise even if ARCS itself stored nothing.

C4

Send the sentence, not the document

When a judgment call is allowed, send only the span being judged. TypeSafe's own notes say large inputs with irrelevant detail hurt accuracy, so the privacy rule and the quality rule agree.

C5

Counterpart names and their details are replaced before text leaves the machine. Text whose subject is a person stays local even after redaction.

No ambient watching

Background judgment runs only over text a person or the repo brought in on purpose. ARCS has no system watching calendars, messages or behavior for a decision forming, and TypeSafe in the background would not change that.

Primitives and state, mapped to our evals

TypeSafe's API has four question types. Choice picks one of your options, Score places the input on your ordered levels, Noul asks a yes or no question, and Box locates something in an image. Every answer comes only from the options or levels you declared, which is our pre-registration rule enforced by the API. State is the content being judged, sent whole with each request as a string, a named-field object, an array or images; the docs describe no session, cache or reuse between requests.

Score and Choice return a probability spread and a confidence value. That is the missing input for earned fidelity: a spread-out judgment renders at GRAIN and a confident one at SHARP, measured rather than self-reported. Noul returns no confidence, so a value near 0.5 means the model cannot tell, never a middle grade.

The state model divides the work cleanly. TypeSafe holds no conversation, so lineage and receipt history stay in ARCS: they judge, we remember. Holding nothing between requests is still not retention, though. The customer agreement governs what they keep, so state is exactly the text that leaves the machine, and C1 to C9 apply to every field of it, images included.

Their advice to keep related facts together pulls against C4. The answer is the smallest state that still allows the comparison: for a claim check, the claim and the cited excerpt, never the whole post and source. One more catch: slop_eval grades pages before they ship, and unshipped text is not public, so it stays on a local judge until it is published.

P1

A state projector makes the carve-outs mechanical

One function builds every TypeSafe state: it takes an allowlist of named fields, refuses any field not on it, swaps counterpart names for pseudonyms, and rejects text not tagged as already public. It is the same projection idea as recoil_brief.py --audience external, and it turns the D4 send rule from prose into a check that blocks. Filed as backlog row typesafe-state-projector.

Before, during and after

Before

  • You answer D1 to D4 on the October proposal.
  • If D2 is yes, I draft the questions for TypeSafe: zero data retention, a written confidentiality commitment for inputs, the subprocessor list, an MCP server. You send them.
  • If D4 is yes, add the public-text-only send rule.

During

  • The pilot classifies public text only, against your 20 labels, with an 80% agreement bar.
  • Option order is reversed and a self-arguing claim is planted, so the known weak spots are tested.
  • Results become a decision receipt.

After

  • Private and personal text stays local whatever the pilot shows.
  • Any move toward user content starts with a canon amendment, a /privacy update and a privacy-review pass, before code.
