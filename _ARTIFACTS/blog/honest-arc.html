03 The three twists

1

The fact-check overruled the crowd

Three reviewers wanted to feature one line as the centerpiece. The one reviewer who checked the actual code proved it false. Sounding right lost to being right.

2

The same lie, one sentence over

The fix for the first false claim quietly re-anchored on the very next sentence of the same source — also false. A later, more skeptical pass un-verified an earlier "✓ verified." The document caught its own patch.

3

A stranger broke the headline

Handed the finished files cold, a fresh AI showed the grand claim ("a different kind of thing than big AI") rested on a false premise. It collapsed to something smaller and true — catching a mistake an earlier fix had introduced.

The honest ending

The claim didn't win. It got smaller until it was true.

What survived: your judgment is never overruled by a shortcut, and the results stay as readable files. Not the grand version — "a fundamentally different kind of thing" — which a cold test proved false. A method that can prove its own headline wrong is the opposite of a sales pitch. That is why this reads as honest and not as a fairy tale: the arc was allowed to end where the evidence stopped, not where the story wanted to.
