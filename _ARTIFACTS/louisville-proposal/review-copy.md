**Review copy.** This is the proposal as an editable document. Change anything: scope, dates, people, terms. Mark it up in comments or suggestions, or print it and mark it by hand, then send it back and I'll fold your changes in. Paragraphs are numbered so you can point to them.

¶1. You asked where you are on the arc of your AI build, where it's least efficient, and which hypotheses are failing. I'd answer that by auditing the build through two real decisions, one technical and one business, each traced from its inputs to how it turns out, before the next 20,000-bag MOQ rides on it.

¶2. **Analyzing your own stuff from inside is hard:** Confirmation and recency bias. An outside read checks the work you and your team have built; it doesn't replace it.

## If you want the outside read

¶8. I'd read your build through what it decides. Every decision gets the same three things: What it relies on, what it can't see, and the date you'll know.

## 02 The two decisions

¶9. You pick them. Here is what I'd suggest.

¶10. **Technical.** A choice in how the build works, such as how the warehouse and MCP feed your criteria, or what stays on-prem. I follow it from the data in to the answer out, and mark where it held, slowed or broke.

¶11. **Business.** A call the build informed, such as Filipino Adobo, a partner check before a collab, or the stop number before an MOQ.

## 03 What $5,000 gets you

¶12. Every deliverable, and the time it takes.

1. Both decisions traced in writing: What each relies on, what it can't see, and the date you'll know. If one has played out, it sits next to what actually happened.
2. One page: Where the build stands, what's least efficient, and which hypotheses aren't holding, as the two traces show them.
3. Read against the six things you said you want: Accuracy, efficiency, speed, cost, on-prem, and a single source of truth that isn't growing data bloat and tech debt.
4. Next steps, each with a number for success and a date to check it.
5. Honest marks: Anything two decisions can't show is marked as not checked, anything I'm unsure of is marked as unsure, and your pushback goes on the page.

¶13. A two-week sprint. About three and a half hours of your team's time, a little over four with the optional session.

| When | What you give | What you get |

| Week 2 | The readout, 60 minutes, and your pushback on anything that's wrong. | The one page, the read against your six, and next steps, walked through together. |
