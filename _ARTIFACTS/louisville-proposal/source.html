An outside read on your AI build

You asked where you are on the arc of your AI build, where it's least efficient, and which hypotheses are failing. I'd answer that by auditing the build through two real decisions, one technical and one business, each traced from its inputs to how it turns out, before the next 20,000-bag MOQ rides on it.

Analyzing your own stuff from inside is hard: Confirmation and recency bias. An outside read checks the work you and your team have built; it doesn't replace it.

Questions worth putting to your team

Useful whether or not I'm involved. Each comes from something you raised when we spoke, as I understood it.
What you raised
Worth asking
Volume-based indicators can miss a signal that's still rising, and every day without a call costs time to market.
Your criteria already reads whether a flavor has peaked or is still rising. On the calls it made, how often was that read right, and what would have told you it was wrong before the MOQ?
You've built early-alarm signals for partner risk by hand before, and an analysis before a partnership is signed is critical.
Before the next collab is signed, what analysis runs on the partner, and would it catch a problem as it starts to bubble up after?
When a simulation misses, either the data was bad or the hypothesis was wrong.
For the last miss, which was it, and can your setup tell the two apart next time?
You don't want a 20,000-bag MOQ placed without an early indicator.
Which flavor calls carry a number that says stop, agreed before the order?

If you want the outside read

I'd read your build through what it decides. Every decision gets the same three things: What it relies on, what it can't see, and the date you'll know.

The two decisions

You pick them. Here is what I'd suggest.
Technical
A choice in how the build works, such as how the warehouse and MCP feed your criteria, or what stays on-prem. I follow it from the data in to the answer out, and mark where it held, slowed or broke.
Business
A call the build informed, such as Filipino Adobo, a partner check before a collab, or the stop number before an MOQ.

What $5,000 gets you

Every deliverable, and the time it takes.
  1. 01Both decisions traced in writing: What each relies on, what it can't see, and the date you'll know. If one has played out, it sits next to what actually happened.
  2. 02One page: Where the build stands, what's least efficient, and which hypotheses aren't holding, as the two traces show them.
  3. 03Read against the five things you said you want: Accuracy, efficiency, speed, cost, and a single source of truth that isn't growing data bloat and tech debt.
  4. 04Next steps, each with a number for success and a date to check it.
  5. 05Honest marks: Anything two decisions can't show is marked as not checked, anything I'm unsure of is marked as unsure, and your pushback goes on the page.
