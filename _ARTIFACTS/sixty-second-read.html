The 60-Second Read

A chronograph that teaches a dense recoil report how to read itself. Not a countdown that rushes Ahmed. An instrument that keeps a promise: three reads, twenty seconds each, and you have it.

Fixes: "lots of information, hard to follow" Refinement, not redesign One batched deploy

Animatic · working prototype

The instrument, alive

Press start. The hand sweeps one wedge at a time, lighting the step it belongs to. It reflects where the reader is, it never pressures where they should be. This is the real behavior, at spec.

00–20s

What you're looking at

A conversation turned into two belief-lines moving over time. Same shape as your Activity Schema, pointed at a decision.

20–40s

How to read the chart

A line rises when evidence backs a belief, falls when it doesn't. The drop at S4 is me being wrong, kept on purpose.

40–60s

What to do with it

The same read, pointed at one of your customers' call timelines. That is the pilot.

Does not auto-run on load. Honors prefers-reduced-motion: the hand jumps between segments, no sweep.

Creative direction

Tonal register

The reference is a mechanical chronograph on a dark cockpit panel, not a stopwatch app. Brushed-steel bezel, tritium-green needle glowing against near-black, machined tick marks. It belongs to the same world as the recoil chart it introduces: both are time instruments, both read left to right, both are calm under load.

The feeling is an instrument handed to a skilled operator, the WORLD.md register, never SaaS onboarding. No confetti, no bounce, no "great job." The 60 is a promise the page keeps, not a threat it makes.

Do

  • Analog dial, machined ticks, single glowing needle
  • Progress reads as position, advanced by the reader
  • Tritium green as the one accent; everything else quiet
  • Silence by default; sound only if the reader opts in

Don't

  • A live countdown that ticks down and rushes him
  • Auto-play on load (that is the pressure version by accident)
  • Red "time's up" states or urgency color
  • Any second accent competing with the needle

Product requirements

What it must do

Functional

  • Sits first, after the hero, above the report
  • Static and legible with zero interaction (the fix can't require a click to become useful)
  • Each step is an anchor link into the section that proves it, reusing the existing scroll-spy sub-nav (no second observer)
  • "Start the read" is opt-in enhancement, not the only path
  • Legend annotation anchored to the first chart so it decodes at point of contact

Non-functional

  • Mobile-first, holds at 375px (his flagged failure case)
  • prefers-reduced-motion: dial jumps, no sweep
  • Keyboard focus visible on both buttons; dial has an aria-label
  • Inline styles only, no dependency, no em-dashes, no ARCS jargon
  • Air-gap intact: no NCI / conviction / kinematics leak

Sixty seconds, shot by shot

If you ever record the Loom you offered Ahmed, this is the storyboard. The on-page version is the same beats without the voiceover. Sound is optional and opt-in on the page; on the Loom it is the bed.

0:00COLD OPEN

Dial at rest, reading 60

Full frame on the unlit chronograph. Ahmed's own line fades in beneath: "targeting a very similar set of customers." Nothing moves yet.

Single low sine swell, a room tone. No pulse.
0:00–0:20WEDGE 1

Hand sweeps the first third

Step one lights: "what you're looking at." The recoil chart slides in behind, dimmed, waiting.

Soft mechanical tick per major mark. Warm, felted, not sharp.
0:20–0:40WEDGE 2

Hand crosses to the S4 drop

Step two lights: "how to read the chart." The cyan line's fall at segment 4 pulses once. The honest part gets the emphasis.

One sustained cello note under the pulse. The correction is the melody.
0:40–0:60WEDGE 3

Hand completes; pilot resolves

Step three lights: "what to do with it." The view settles on the one-customer pilot ask. Hand rests at 60.

Notes resolve to a major chord, quiet. Landing, not fanfare.
0:60HOLD

Rest state

Dial holds. One line remains: "Send me the customer you want to start with." No replay prompt shouting.

Return to room tone. Silence does the closing.
Music brief: think Jóhann Jóhannsson or Max Richter, a single instrument and a lot of air. Ambient, ~60bpm to match one tick per second, no drums, no build to a drop. The track exists to make silence feel intentional, not to hype. Licensing-safe route: a calm ambient bed from a royalty-free library (Uppbeat, Epidemic) filtered under the tick layer. Sound is muted by default on the page and only plays if the reader turns it on.
