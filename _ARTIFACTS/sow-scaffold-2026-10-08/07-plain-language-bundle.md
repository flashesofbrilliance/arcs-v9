## Exec overview

Your product already does what you set out to show: the site works and the scaffolding is in place. What comes next is the part that will matter most to families: Alice, the AI that helps parents find their way. Before more gets built, the most valuable thing I can do is help make sure everyone, including the budget, the lawyers and the build, is describing the same product.

Having the documents is not the same as everyone understanding them the same way. Think of a budget that Finance reads one way and Marketing reads another. Each document is fine. The surprise comes later. My job is to close that gap early, while it is cheap, so what gets built keeps matching what the plan says it will cost.

My approach has four stages. Each one ends with a checkpoint set in advance. I aim not to spend ahead of a decision that should come first. And you can stop after any stage and keep everything I have made.

Think of building a house. First I look at the ground and the plans with you. Then we agree the route together. Then the floor gets poured. Only then do you decide how big the house gets. If you ever want to stop, you leave with your binder.

Until I have seen your code and build plan, the technical points on this page are my best reading, not confirmed facts. I will tell you which is which as I learn more.

## What is in scope, and what the audit could change

I cannot know exactly what stage 1 will show. So here are three general cases, so you can see which steps are in scope in each. They are not predictions, and nothing here is priced.

- **Best case:** the audit finds the basics already in place. Changes are tracked, testing is kept apart from the real thing, and each kind of information already has an agreed home.
- **Base case:** the audit finds some gaps. For example, changes are not tracked, testing and the real thing are mixed, or I need to make a chart of where each kind of information may live.
- **Worst case:** the audit finds that a service is not a suitable home for some kind of information. A decision, with costs, comes before anything else is built.

## Questions to settle with your lawyers first

Kelly named the hard problem: making Alice warm without ever giving health advice. I would turn that into something testable. With your lawyers, write down three lists:
- what Alice may always do;
- what she may do only with a check;
- what she must never do.

Then I would test her against a fixed set of sample questions, and we agree in advance what a pass looks like. I am not a lawyer and none of this is legal advice. Your counsel should confirm anything about privacy law, children's information, vendor agreements or contracts. I can help organise their questions so the ones that block building come first.

To keep legal time and cost contained, I would suggest a different order from the usual one. Rather than asking your lawyers to start from a blank page, your team brings them a short memo: what information the app holds, who uses it and at what age, where each kind of information goes, what Alice may and may not do, and which rules you *think* apply. Your lawyers then confirm, correct or object, within a time and fee limit agreed up front. Just as useful: ask them for a plain list of what any AI or hosting provider must have, and must not do. That list turns choosing a provider into a quick check rather than an open debate. Your lawyers still decide which rules apply; the memo only gives them something concrete to react to.

## How we will handle feedback

Feedback is welcome at every step. So it helps rather than piles up, I suggest a few simple rules, agreed now:

- **One thread.** All feedback goes to one email thread. After each call I write up what we said in that thread, so nothing lives only in a conversation.
- **One voice.** You name one person who gathers the team's comments and settles any disagreements before sending them to me.
- **One round per stage.** Feedback on a stage's work comes in one batch, within about three working days of its checkpoint. Anything later rolls into the next stage.
- **Two revisions included.** Each piece of work includes two rounds of changes. More rounds come out of office hours, so the cost is always visible.
- **Settled stays settled.** Reopening a decision already made needs that decision's owner, and it goes in the decision record with the reason.
