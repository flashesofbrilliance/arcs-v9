# My Ally LD: a plain-language plan

For Kelly and Chelsea, from Zach at manana

manana is Zach's consulting practice. He has built a set of tools called ARCS to support this kind of work, and this plan says plainly which of them he would use at each stage and which he would not.

## The short version

You have built a product that works on the surface. The part still ahead is the part everyone is watching: Alice, the AI that helps parents find their way. Before more gets built, the most valuable thing we can do is make sure everyone, including the budget, the lawyers and the build, is describing the same product.

Having the documents is not the same as everyone understanding them the same way. Think of a budget that Finance reads one way and Marketing reads another. Each document is fine. The surprise comes later. Our job is to close that gap early, while it is cheap, so what gets built keeps matching what the plan says it will cost.

The plan has four stages. Each one ends with a checkpoint you can check for yourselves. Nothing gets spent ahead of a decision that should come first. And you can stop after any stage with a pack of everything we have made, in your hands, so you lose nothing.

## The whole idea, simply

You are building a helpful guide for families, and her name is Alice. Think of building a house for her. First we look at the ground and the plans. Then we agree the route together. Then we pour a strong floor. Only then do we decide how big the house gets. At every step we say out loud what "done" looks like, so nobody is surprised. If you ever want to stop, you take your binder and walk away with everything.

Any word that sounds technical is explained in the translation codex that comes with this note (`09-translation-codex.md`).

## Our current view on Alice

We would hold off connecting Alice until the early work is done. Wiring in the AI is the most expensive thing to redo, and several answers it depends on are not settled yet: which AI provider, what your lawyers allow, how private documents are handled, and who sees what.

If it would help to show something sooner, there is a cheaper option. A small, time-limited trial, using made-up family information only, would show what a conversation with Alice really costs and how she sounds. It would give your cost model real numbers without committing you to the full build.

## Questions to settle with your lawyers first

Kelly named the hard problem: making Alice warm without ever giving health advice. We would turn that into something testable. With your lawyers, write down three lists:
- what Alice may always do;
- what she may do only with a check;
- what she must never do.

Then we test her against a fixed set of sample questions and agree in advance what a pass looks like. We are not lawyers and none of this is legal advice. Your counsel should confirm anything about privacy law, children's information, vendor agreements or contracts. We would help organise their questions so the ones that block building come first.

## How we handle "will this work?"

Before any important piece of work, the person who owns it writes down:
1. how confident they are that it can be done in the time and budget;
2. a cap on time and money, so a miss never hurts the whole plan;
3. what "it worked" looks like, and what "it did not" looks like;
4. what a miss would cost in time, pace, focus, money and morale, and who would carry it.

It works like a test budget in a marketing plan. You cap the test so one miss never sinks the campaign, and you decide in advance what result tells you to stop.

## The cost, in three buckets

We will fill these in together, using your usage model, current price pages with their dates, and our rates once they are agreed. Each bucket gets a best case, a likely case and a worst case, with the cases defined before anyone fills in a number.

1. **Fixed:** one-time and set-up work, subscriptions, and your lawyers' time.
2. **Grows with use:** the AI usage meter (more families and longer documents mean a bigger bill), search and file handling.
3. **Hidden:** rework when a legal answer arrives late, the time families spend reviewing what gets hidden from the AI, switching providers later, and your own four people's time.
