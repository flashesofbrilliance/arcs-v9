# My Ally LD: a plain-language plan

For Kelly and Chelsea, from Zach at manana

manana is my consulting practice. I have built a set of tools called ARCS to support this kind of work, and this plan says plainly which of them I would use at each stage and which I would not.

## The short version

You have built a product that works on the surface. The part still ahead is the part everyone is watching: Alice, the AI that helps parents find their way. Before more gets built, the most valuable thing I can do is help make sure everyone, including the budget, the lawyers and the build, is describing the same product.

Having the documents is not the same as everyone understanding them the same way. Think of a budget that Finance reads one way and Marketing reads another. Each document is fine. The surprise comes later. My job is to close that gap early, while it is cheap, so what gets built keeps matching what the plan says it will cost.

My approach has four stages. Each one ends with a checkpoint you can check for yourselves. Nothing gets spent ahead of a decision that should come first. And you can stop after any stage with a pack of everything I have made, in your hands, so you lose nothing.

## The whole idea, simply

You are building a helpful guide for families, and her name is Alice. Think of building a house for her. First I look at the ground and the plans with you. Then we agree the route together. Then we pour a strong floor. Only then do you decide how big the house gets. At every step I say out loud what "done" looks like, so nobody is surprised. If you ever want to stop, you take your binder and walk away with everything.

Any word that sounds technical is explained in the translation codex that comes with this note (`09-translation-codex.md`).

## My view on Alice

I would hold off connecting Alice until the early work is done. Wiring in the AI is the most expensive thing to redo, and several answers it depends on are not settled yet: which AI provider, what your lawyers allow, how private documents are handled, and who sees what.

If it would help to show something sooner, there is a cheaper option. A small, time-limited trial, using made-up family information only, would show what a conversation with Alice really costs and how she sounds. It would give your cost model real numbers without committing you to the full build.

## Questions to settle with your lawyers first

Kelly named the hard problem: making Alice warm without ever giving health advice. I would turn that into something testable. With your lawyers, write down three lists:
- what Alice may always do;
- what she may do only with a check;
- what she must never do.

Then I would test her against a fixed set of sample questions, and we agree in advance what a pass looks like. I am not a lawyer and none of this is legal advice. Your counsel should confirm anything about privacy law, children's information, vendor agreements or contracts. I can help organise their questions so the ones that block building come first.

## How I would approach "will this work?"

Before any important piece of work, the person who owns it writes down:
1. how confident they are that it can be done in the time and budget;
2. a cap on time and money, so a miss never hurts the whole plan;
3. what "it worked" looks like, and what "it did not" looks like;
4. what a miss would cost in time, pace, focus, money and morale, and who would carry it.

When the work is mine, I write mine down too. It works like a test budget in a marketing plan. You cap the test so one miss never sinks the campaign, and you decide in advance what result tells you to stop.

## A possibility I want to name early: setup that depends on what I find

Some of the groundwork cannot be scoped until I have looked. I would rather tell you now than surprise you later. Depending on the audit, a layer of careful set-up may be needed, and it would be priced only if the audit shows it is needed. Here is what would switch each part on:

| If I find this | I would add this |
|---|---|
| The app's changes are not tracked, or anyone can change the live version | Proper change tracking: every change recorded, reviewed before it goes live, and easy to undo |
| Testing and the real thing are mixed, or real documents are used while building | Separate places for rehearsal and for real use, with made-up information only in rehearsal |
| Some kind of information has no agreed place to live | A simple chart of what kind of information may live in which service, for how long, and who can see it, made with your lawyers |
| No tested backup, no diary of who did what, or no alarms | A backup that has been practised, an activity diary and alarms |
| A lawyer, partner or funder asks me to show the safeguards | A record showing where the proof of each safeguard lives |
| A service turns out not to be a suitable home for some kind of information | A decision, with costs, about moving that part, made before anything else is built |

I would do the cheap protective parts first (change tracking, separate rehearsal and real), the rest before the first outside family uses the app, and the evidence record only when someone asks. The aim is simple: every statement about where your families' information lives is backed by an agreement or a decision by your lawyers. It is not to turn a four-person team into a big compliance department.

## The cost, in three buckets

We will fill these in together, using your usage model, current price pages with their dates, and my rates once they are agreed. Each bucket gets a best case, a likely case and a worst case, with the cases defined before anyone fills in a number.

1. **Fixed:** one-time and set-up work, subscriptions, and your lawyers' time.
2. **Grows with use:** the AI usage meter (more families and longer documents mean a bigger bill), search and file handling.
3. **Hidden:** the cost of moving information or parts of the app if a service turns out not to be the right home for some kind of information; rework when a legal answer arrives late, the time families spend reviewing what gets hidden from the AI, switching providers later, and your own four people's time.
