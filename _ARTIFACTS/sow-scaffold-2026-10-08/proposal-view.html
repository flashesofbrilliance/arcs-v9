Before more gets built, the most valuable thing I can do is help make sure everyone, including the budget, the lawyers and the build, is describing the same product.

My approach has four stages. Each one ends with a checkpoint set in advance. I aim not to spend ahead of a decision that should come first. And you can stop after any stage and keep everything I have made.

The whole idea, simply

Build it like a house: look at the ground first, agree the route, pour the floor, then decide how big it gets.

Think of building a house. First I look at the ground and the plans with you. Then we agree the route together. Then the floor gets poured. Only then do you decide how big the house gets. At every step I say out loud what "done" looks like, so nobody is surprised. If you ever want to stop, you leave with your binder.

How I would approach "will this work?"

Before important work, the owner writes down confidence, a cap on time and money, what worked and failed look like, and what a miss would cost.

Before any important piece of work, the person who owns it writes down: 1. how confident they are that it can be done in the time and budget; 2. a cap on time and money, so a miss never hurts the whole plan; 3. what "it worked" looks like, and what "it did not" looks like; 4. what a miss would cost in time, pace, focus, money and morale, and who would carry it.

When the work is mine, I write mine down too. It works like a test budget in a marketing plan. You cap the test so one miss never sinks the campaign, and you decide in advance what result tells you to stop.
