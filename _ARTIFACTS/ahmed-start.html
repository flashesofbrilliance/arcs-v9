What you are looking at A scorekeeper for meetings, shown on our own July 22 call. Before we spoke I wrote down what I expected you to say. After, I checked each line against the recording.

Why it exists Written down before, it is a bet. Written down after, it is a story. Only a bet can be scored. Over a series of calls, the scores are a track record of how well someone reads a customer.

How to read it Everything is rows, left to right: what was expected, what was said, how confidence moved, what to do about it. If a number went down, the row is red. The red rows are the useful ones.

The offer, in three sentences

It is for a sales leader at one of your customers who already records their calls.

They get one page per deal: where the buyer's confidence rose, where it fell, and the line that turned it. Rolled up, which deals to step into this week and the one objection that keeps costing the team.

Your customer hands over an export of the calls. An automated pass scores them. One person reviews the output and sends it. Nothing to install.
