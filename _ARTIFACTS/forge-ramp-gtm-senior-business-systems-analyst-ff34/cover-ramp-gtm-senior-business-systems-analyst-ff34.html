I run the system, then I build the tool that runs it. That is the short version of why this role fits.

Here is the longer one. Most GTM systems reqs are really a question about who owns the seams: between the CRM and the data warehouse, between partner attribution and payout, between what the process says and what the reps actually do. I have spent two decades on those seams. At Grayscale I led go-to-market for ten SEC/FINRA-regulated products and owned the data contracts, field-mapping logic, and integration architecture that kept systems telling the same story across a regulated release lifecycle. At Children's Tumor Foundation I built the enterprise data layer from nothing, CDP evaluation through Salesforce Lightning modernization, and net-new revenue moved twenty percent because the segmentation finally matched the lifecycle.

A few things I noticed that made me want to write.

Ramp's channel motion is exactly the kind of problem where the seams compound fast. Partner attribution from first touch to payout is not one system problem, it is four: referral capture, deal registration, partner-to-opportunity linkage, and monthly payout reconciliation, each with its own failure mode and its own stakeholder who will notice when it breaks. I have built the data contracts and integration architecture that hold that kind of multi-system chain together in regulated environments. The discipline transfers directly.

I have sequenced multi-month projects across engineering, sales, and finance stakeholders with no PM attached, held scope, and cleared blockers before they landed. That is the operating mode I default to.
