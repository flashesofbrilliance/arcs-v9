Antifragile core & the lossless test

The single most valuable thing this call produced was a wrong prediction. The receipt filed beforehand committed to a specific move, ask the recruiter directly for the CEO's background, and committed to what a pass would look like: a named, textured answer about a person. That answer never came. Joe said he had not done the deep dive on the co-founders and offered a values-based account of the client relationship in its place. Had the receipt not been filed in advance, the honest version of this is the one that gets lost: a call where the company name landed, a loop got named, and the artifact got praised feels like a good call, and the missing half quietly becomes a detail. Because the prediction was on the record with a falsifier attached, the miss is now the most load-bearing line in the file. It produced a correction that generalizes well beyond this opportunity: a third-party recruiter is a high-quality channel for process, bar and timing, and a near-zero channel for the business and the principals, so principal-level diligence runs independently and it runs before the call rather than after. The proof that the correction is real and not cosmetic is that the independent pass closed the exact gate Joe could not, naming Aaron Langley as a real co-founder and the first interviewer, in a fraction of the time the call took. Being wrong did not cost the position, it relocated the work to where it actually gets done.

Lossless test: Autopilot exists because people would rather copy a disclosed position than trust a confident opinion. You do not buy what someone says they believe, you look at what they actually put money into, on the record, before the outcome was known, and then you check later whether it worked. That is the entire method in this file, turned on my own judgment instead of on a portfolio. Before the call, I wrote down what I expected to happen and what would prove me wrong. After the call, I scored it against what was actually said, and one of my two predictions was simply wrong, so it got marked wrong with a number attached rather than reworded into a partial success. A signal that scores 0.15 against a coin flip does not get argued with, it gets retired, and the belief that a recruiter could tell me who the founders really are scored about that well. The recruiting firm says it keeps ninety-one percent of the people it places, which is a claim about a process, and a claim about a process only means something if somebody is also counting the times it did not work. This is me counting mine. Nothing in the paragraph above needed a single term you would not hear on a trading desk, which is the test.
