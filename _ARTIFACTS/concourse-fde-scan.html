Where I expect to add the most value

Concourse configures ready-made modules to each agency's rules and delivers in months. As the number of deployments grows, I expect the slow part to be what repeats around the build: discovery, getting data access, and assembling compliance evidence.

That is where I would aim first: intake templates, a standard data-access request, reusable compliance evidence, and checks that catch configuration drift between agencies. The build gets faster when the scaffolding around it stops starting from zero.

If deployments already reuse nearly all of that, this prediction is wrong and I would focus on build speed instead.

What I would bring to a kickoff: a one-glance engagement card

One page the client, the FDPM and the FDE all read at kickoff and update every week. It sets the policy, the controls and the gates for the whole 90 days, so progress is visible and nobody is surprised at handoff. The example below is invented, for a made-up county permitting deployment; it shows the shape, not Concourse's process.

The questionCan residents get a residential permit decision in 10 business days instead of 45?
Who decidesPermitting director signs off; IT security approves data access; county counsel reviews the records policy.
Window90 days, three 30-day phases, each ending at a gate.
Days 1 to 30 · DiscoverMap today's process with the clerks who run it. Get data access in writing. Gate: director agrees on the one measure of success.
Days 31 to 60 · BuildConfigure the modules to the county's rules. Weekly demo to real users. Gate: a clerk completes a real permit in the new system.
Days 61 to 90 · Hand offTraining, runbook, who to call when it breaks. Gate: county staff run it for two weeks without us.
Controls from day oneAccess by named role only. Every change reviewed and logged. Accessibility checked before each demo, not at the end.
Progress, every weekOne line each: measure vs target, gate status, top risk, decision needed and from whom.
Stop ruleIf data access is not granted by day 20, escalate to the director that week instead of building on sample data.
