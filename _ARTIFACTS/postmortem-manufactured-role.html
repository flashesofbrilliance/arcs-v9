§ Root cause · separated A sufficient alone · B raised the price of a yes
A · req realityOut of Zach's control. Sufficient on its own. Chris's post was, per CJ, a probe to "see what type of response he would get." The forming role was a growth-operator exec; the pitched role was "a new function that doesn't exist yet." A recruiter cannot mint a VP function on a candidate's behalf. Chris asked in writing for a Chase meeting, then went silent. No execution change turns "JD being defined" into a hire inside a Talent screen.
B · executionIn Zach's control. Did not cause the no; raised the price of a yes. Wrong axis (CJ conceded the AI axis in minute eight; the unconceded axis was the mandate). Negotiating before there was a counterparty. Self-authoring the req and the rubric for the person whose job is to write them. Maximal ask with no scoped fallback; the Day-60 pilot was in the plan as a reward, never offered as the entry. Stop-loss unexecuted. Warmth mis-scored as fit. Rule unbanked for 60 days.
VerdictManufactured role. Not a fit loss, not a warmth loss. A render that ignored a level-1 answer to a question that was asked.
