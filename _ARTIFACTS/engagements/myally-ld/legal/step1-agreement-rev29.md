# MyAllyLD Step 1 agreement

**At a glance.** I review your app's code and plans and tell you, in plain words, what is solid, what to fix
first and roughly what the next stages cost. It is a fixed $1,500, takes about a week once we start, and you
decide on any next step afterwards. You see each piece as it's ready, and we review your comments together in
short scheduled calls. The numbered sections below are the agreement. The notes and glossary at the end only
explain them; if a note and a section ever seem to differ, the section counts.

## 1. Who this is between

Access Consulting, LLC, a Connecticut limited liability company, for its MyAllyLD project ("you"), and Zach
Harris, signing as an individual ("I"). The person signing for Access Consulting confirms they can bind it.
This agreement takes effect on the date of the last signature.

We already have a mutual non-disclosure agreement (the "NDA"), effective October 8, 2026, which stays in
place. This agreement is the separate services agreement the NDA calls for in section 5.1. (Note 1)

## 2. What I'll do and what you get

I read your app's code in Lovable, its database rules and your integration spec. You get, in your name:

1. **A code review:** the code sorted into three piles (ready to build on, keep with some tidying, better
   rebuilt), with the reasons.
2. **A data separation check:** whether each family's information is kept apart from every other family's in
   the current code.
3. **A spec map:** what your spec describes, set against what is built, half-built or missing.
4. **A data governance plan:** the controls and approvals your pilot schools and families are likely to ask
   for, options to look at for each, what to buy or rent instead of build, and the questions to take to your
   counsel and accountant.
5. **Rough ranges,** each with its assumptions written out: a build ready to test with up to 100 families; the
   path from there to a public launch; and monthly running costs for AI, hosting and security checks over two
   years. These are my planning estimates, not quotes. (Note 2)
6. **A go or stop memo,** with a ranked list of the problems to fix first.
7. **A 45-minute readout call** to walk through all of it.

Items 1 to 6, in writing, are "the deliverables".

## 3. What is not included

Fixing anything, a full security test, firm estimates, and setup work such as GitHub, version control,
automatic checks and deploys, or test environments. Those belong to Steps 2 and 3. Anything not listed in
section 2 is extra work, added only by a change order under section 8.

## 4. Price and payment

- **Outside costs are yours and paid directly by you:** the accounts your app runs on, such as Lovable,

  (Note 3)

## 5. When it starts and how long it takes

The work starts on the working day after the last of these is in:

1. this agreement, signed by both of us, and the invoice paid;
2. an invite for zach.harris@gmail.com to a copy (a "remix") of your Lovable project with no real family
   information in it;
3. screenshots of your last one or two monthly bills and usage screens for Lovable, Supabase and your AI
   provider (crop out anything private).

**The readout is within five working days of the start.** If something on your side arrives later, the dates
move by the same amount. If the readout comes more than 10 working days after the start for reasons on my
side, you may end this agreement and get a full refund. A date moved by a change order you approved (section
8) is not a delay on my side. A "working day" is Monday to Friday, not counting US federal holidays.

## 6. Along the way: a punchlist with batched reviews

- **The Step 1 tab is a punchlist.** As each deliverable is ready, I mark it "ready for review" and let you

- **Comment whenever suits you,** on any item marked ready, in one shared place. I don't answer comments one
  at a time; I collect them.
- **We go through them together** in a 30-minute review call midweek, and again at the readout. Short,
  batched calls keep the work inside its 12 hours and keep your time free in between. These review calls are
  part of Step 1 and don't use your office hours. (Note 4)
- **Office hours stay exactly as I offered on October 29:** 4 hours a month at no charge, in 30-minute slots,
  to use however you like, booked through my calendar link. They don't count toward the 12 hours, and because

## 7. Delivery and your sign-off

I deliver the final written deliverables no later than the readout. Comments from the review calls are folded

## 8. Extra work and change orders

**No surprises, in either direction: I never bill for work you didn't approve in writing first, and I tell
you a request is extra before I start it, never after.** (Note 6)

- **Any change to the scope is agreed in writing first.** That covers adding, removing or changing a
  deliverable, and anything that changes what I'm working from. The order is always the same: I give an
  estimate, you approve it in writing, then the change starts. An estimate may be a range for planning, but
  what you approve is a single most-it-can-cost number. **An email "yes" from you is your written OK for a
  change order.**
- **New or changed material from you:** if you send something new during Step 1, such as a new version of
  your spec, I tell you within 2 working days whether it fits inside Step 1 or needs a change order. Until
  you decide, I keep working from what we started with.
- **Dropping something:** if you'd rather drop a deliverable, we agree in writing whether the price changes.
- **Office hours or extra work?** Questions, advice and talking things through are office hours (section 6),
  at no charge. Outside the work in sections 2, 6 and 7, anything that needs me to make something, such as
  writing, research, reviewing new material or building, is extra work.
- **If Step 1 itself needs more time:** at 12 hours I stop and ask. Extra time is in 2-hour blocks at $125 an
  hour, approved and paid like a change order before the work is done. A redo under section 13 is free and
  does not count toward the 12 hours.
- **How extra work gets added (a change order):** you ask, by email or on a call. Within 2 working days I
  send a short change order, by email, saying:
  1. what I'll do and what you get;
  2. the most it can cost, in 2-hour blocks at $125 an hour;
  3. when it starts and when it's done;
  4. whether it moves the readout or any other date.
- **When the clock starts on extra work:** the working day after you reply "yes" to the change order by email
  and pay its invoice. Nothing starts before both.
- **The quoted amount is a ceiling.** If I finish early, unused blocks are refunded or, if you prefer, kept as
  credit for later work. If it looks like needing more, I stop and send a new change order first.
- **Extra work leaves Step 1 alone** unless the change order says it moves a date.
- The readout always shows the hours actually spent, on Step 1 and on each change order.

## Notes

These notes explain the sections in everyday terms and give the legal name for each idea, for your counsel.
They are not part of the agreement.

1. **Section 1.** NDA section 5.1 allows access to your code or systems only under a separate signed services
   agreement with its own security, data and ownership terms. Sections 10 to 12 are those terms.
2. **Section 2, item 5.** Lawyers call this a non-reliance or estimate disclaimer: the ranges are planning
   numbers built on stated assumptions, not a fixed-price bid.
3. **Section 4.** This is an expenses clause. It keeps your platform and AI bills in your name and stops me
   from running up costs on your behalf.
4. **Section 6.** This is progressive delivery with batched review: work is shown as it's ready, and feedback
   is handled in scheduled, time-boxed sessions rather than one message at a time.
5. **Section 7.** This is acceptance with "deemed acceptance": silence for 7 days counts as a yes, so the
   step can close. It does not remove the 30-day redo-or-refund promise in section 13.
6. **Section 8.** This is a change-order (or change work order) process: extra work is written up, priced
   as a not-to-exceed amount and approved before it starts.
7. **Section 10.** The 72-hour notice matches NDA section 5.2. Working only on a remix with no real family
   information is the main way this agreement keeps children's data out of my hands.
8. **Section 11.** NDA section 4.1 allows AI tools on your material only under terms where the provider does
   not train on it and keeps it confidential. Naming the tools makes that checkable.
9. **Section 12.** This is an assignment of the deliverables to you, effective on payment, with a licence (a
   permission to use) for any of my pre-existing material inside them. NDA section 9.4 expects an assignment.
10. **Section 13.** This is a disclaimer of warranties and of professional advice, plus an allocation of
    roles: I flag, you and your advisers decide.
11. **Section 14.** This is a limitation of liability, with a higher "super-cap" for confidentiality and data
    claims, carve-outs for fraud, intentional misconduct and deliberate disclosure, and a shortened time limit
    for bringing claims. Injunctions under the NDA are unaffected.
12. **Section 15.** This is a narrow intellectual-property indemnity: I stand behind the originality of what I
    deliver.

## Glossary

- **AI usage (tokens):** what AI providers charge for; each request and answer is metered in small units
  called tokens.
- **Business terms / no-training terms:** a provider's contract under which it does not use your content to
  improve its AI models.
- **Change order:** a short email from me describing a piece of extra work, the most it can cost, and its
  dates. It starts only after your "yes" by email and payment.
- **CI/CD:** automatic checks and deploys; software that tests each change and publishes it safely.
- **COPPA, FERPA:** United States laws on children's online privacy (COPPA) and on student education records
  (FERPA). Whether and how they apply to you is a question for your counsel.
- **Data separation:** making sure one family can never see another family's information.
- **Deliverables:** the written items 1 to 6 in section 2.
- **Indemnity:** a promise to defend the other side and cover its costs if a third party sues over something.
- **Injunction:** a court order telling someone to stop doing something, such as sharing confidential
  information.
- **Lovable, Supabase:** the tools your app is built with (Lovable) and where its data is stored (Supabase).
- **NDA:** our mutual non-disclosure agreement, effective October 8, 2026.
- **Punchlist:** the list of Step 1 items on the Step 1 tab, each marked as it moves to "ready for review".
- **Readout:** the 45-minute call where I walk you through the deliverables.
- **Remix:** Lovable's name for a copy of a project. Changes to the copy don't touch the original.
- **Review call:** a 30-minute call where we go through your batched comments together.
- **Spec:** your written description of what the app should do (your integration spec).
- **Super-cap:** a higher limit for one kind of claim, here confidentiality and data.
- **Version control (GitHub):** a saved history of every change to the code, so nothing is lost and any
  change can be undone.
- **Working day:** see section 5.
