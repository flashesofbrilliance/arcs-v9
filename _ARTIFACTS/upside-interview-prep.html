Questions to expect

Walk me through something you built from zero.

ARCS: the problem, the architecture choices, what you'd change. Be clear about what's live and what isn't.

How do you make AI output reliable?

Every model response is checked for the right shape and for truncation before it's saved. A fixed test set catches regressions. People approve decisions. Offer to say where it didn't work.

What would your first 90 days look like?

Weeks 1 to 4: sit with the teams and find the costliest work. Weeks 4 to 8: ship one narrow tool that saves real hours. Then grow from that first win. No big platform promise on day one.

When do you use AI, and when plain code?

Fixed rules stay in plain code. AI handles judgment and reading text. People approve anything that can't be undone.

You haven't worked in commerce. Why you?

Say it first: I learn a domain by sitting with the people doing the work, as I did in health, finance and sports. That's this job.

Why Upside?

Building from scratch, real users in the next room, results you can measure, and a team that wants the change.

Signal threads

  • Do they name a specific team or workflow? That means the first project is real.
  • Do they talk about outcomes or headcount? Outcomes means the forward-deployed framing is sincere.
  • Do they mention budget for tools or a second hire? That means it's a funded program, not an experiment.

Risk flags

  • Staff-level depth: about 8 years of engineering asked. Frame yours as owning systems end to end for 20 years, not titles.
  • Commerce domain is new. Raise it yourself, early, with the learn-by-embedding answer.
  • The ARCS codebase is private. A demo is available on request. Never call it public.
  • No accuracy figure for ARCS decision scoring. Not enough outcomes have closed.

Stop-loss

If it turns into a pure engineering-titles screen, don't stretch claims to match. Answer straight, ask what the next round tests, and close cleanly.
