# Fit check: the RFP engine against fund documents (2026-11-04)

Internal. Scopes whether the public RFP engine (github.com/flashesofbrilliance/rfp-engine, page at
good.arcs.care/rfp-engine) can back a fixed-fee "trace every number in the IC memo or board deck to its
clause" offer for fund and family-office buyers.

**Verdict:** the rules carry over; the readers do not. The guardrails that matter to an investment committee
(cite a real clause, figures match word for word, amended text wins, plain "not covered", no verdicts) are
already enforced in code and pass their checks today. What a fund engagement needs and the engine does not
have: a reader for LPA, side letter and resolution formats (it parses a real LPA-style excerpt to 0 clauses),
PDF ingest, a memo-to-claims step (the engine answers questions; this job checks claims), and side-letter
precedence, which is not "latest wins". That is build work, so it belongs inside the fixed fee as a
per-document-set setup, not as a promise that it works out of the box.

## Rules that carry over

| Engine rule (where in code) | Carries to fund documents? | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Every answer cites a clause that exists (`answer.py`, golden S1 to S3; Merkle proof per citation in `provenance.py`) | **Yes, strongest asset** | An IC member clicks from the number to the clause. The tamper tests mean a cited clause cannot be quietly edited after the fact |
| Every $, % and date matches the cited clause word for word, or the summary is withheld (`FIGURE_RE`, `has_figure`) | **Yes, with a fix** | Probe 3 shows it catches the wrong number. It also flags `2%` vs `2.0%` and would flag "$250 million" vs "$250,000,000". Needs a value normaliser for funds, or a reviewer pass on every flag. Budget it |
| Latest amendment wins; quoting the superseded value alone fails (`resolve.py`, golden S1, S2) | **Partly** | Fits LPA amendments and restated resolutions. The amendment reader only knows the RFP wording ("Section 7.1 (...) is revised"); LPA amendments use other phrasing and need new rules. Side letters are a different shape: they override the LPA **for one investor**, and MFN elections spread terms to some others. That is precedence by investor, not "latest wins". New logic, and the riskiest piece |
| Plain "not covered" beats a guess (golden S7, S8; `not_addressed`) | **Yes** | This is the product for an IC: "the memo says X; no clause in the set supports it". Note the engine's limit stated on its own page: "not covered" means the search did not find it, not that the document is silent |
| No drafting, no verdicts, refused in code (`DRAFT`, `VERDICT`, `DRAFTED` regexes) | **Yes, retune** | The verdict pattern is bid-shaped ("should we bid"). For funds it must refuse "should we invest", "is this a good deal", "is this term market". Cheap regex change plus golden cases |
| No model reachable means `unavailable`, no answer | **Yes** | Good default for a confidential setting |

## Limits that bite

0. **Right number, wrong clause (found in the end-to-end run, `2026-11-04-e2e-qa.md`).** A memo's wrong "100%" fee
   offset matched the "100% catch-up" in the carry clause and came back traced. The fee offset clause says 80%.
   A figure match must also share a subject word with the memo sentence. That rule demoted the false match in this
   one case. It is untested beyond that.
1. **Numbers, not meaning.** The check proves a figure appears in the cited clause. It does not prove the clause
   means what the memo says (a 2% fee on commitments vs on invested capital, a hurdle that is compounded vs simple,
   "net" vs "gross"). The offer must say this in plain words. Every flag and every pass is for a person to read.
2. **No legal review.** It is not a lawyer reading the LPA. It never says a term is enforceable, market or
   acceptable. Fund counsel still owns that.
3. **Derived numbers.** Most IC memo numbers are computed (fee drag in dollars, net IRR, carry at a given exit).
   The engine never states a computed figure (its own rule), so these come back "not covered" with the input
   clauses shown. Correct, but the report must sort them into "traced", "inputs traced, math not checked" and
   "not covered", or the IC drowns in flags.
4. **Readers.** Plain text in one numbering style today. LPA style parsed to 0 clauses (probe 1). Needs a reader
   per format: LPA articles and sub-clauses like 8.3(b)(ii), defined terms in Article I, side letter paragraphs,
   board resolutions ("RESOLVED, that ..."), term sheets that are often tables. Plus PDF to text, which the engine
   does not do (stated on the public page and in the README's not-tested table).
5. **Untested on LPAs and side letters.** Zero runs on any real fund document. The real-model record so far is on
   RFP questions: 24 questions, 11 correct, 12 withheld, 1 wrong (target was 0 wrong; missed). Any fund claim
   must start from that, not from the mock 5/5.
6. **Keyword search.** No embeddings. An LPA says "Management Fee"; a memo may say "fee load" or "annual charge".
   The glossary helps; a fund glossary has to be built per engagement.
7. **Memo direction is new.** The engine answers questions about documents. This offer checks claims in a memo
   against documents. Needs a front end that pulls each number and its sentence from the memo, then asks the engine
   for the clause. Not built.
8. **Confidential documents and the no-model path.** LPAs and side letters are under NDA, and side letters are
   often confidential even from other LPs. Options, in order of safety:
   - **No model at all:** the figure match and the clause search run with no model and no network. A no-model
     memo check is possible (extract figures, search clauses, match), but outside the four fast-path questions
     the engine gives clauses, not a summary. For a memo check that is close to enough.
   - **Local model** (`--provider local`, Ollama): nothing leaves the machine. Untested on fund text; slower; the
     local model's accuracy is unmeasured.
   - **Claude API:** sends the question and a few clauses to Anthropic. Likely not acceptable to a family office
     without its counsel's sign-off. Do not offer it as the default.
   - **The engine's own default is Claude** (`provider.py:111`, `MODEL_PROVIDER` defaults to `claude`). Any question
     that misses the fast path calls the API unless the setup sets it otherwise. The offer promises no model by
     default, so the setup must lock that setting and test that no call goes out.
9. **Side letters are invisible today.** In the end-to-end run, Investor A's 1.50% side letter fee was never raised
   against a memo line quoting the LPA fee.
10. **Unclassified amendments are noisy.** LPA wording ("amended and restated") is correctly flagged for review, but
   the note shows up on every memo line whose search touches that clause, not only the line about it.
11. **File formats (tested, `2026-11-04-format-tests.md`).** The loader opens only `.txt` and skips `.docx`, `.pdf`
   and `.md` silently. With an LPA in text and its amendment in Word, the engine answered the superseded 2.0% fee as
   current, with no warning. Converters accept tracked changes without saying so, can paste comment text into
   clauses, move footnotes to the wrong clause, and lose Word's automatic clause numbers. Page headers end up inside
   PDF clauses. Scans and term sheet tables are refused. All of this needs a reader built per format, plus a gate
   that stops at live tracked changes.

## What the fixed fee has to cover (for Zach to price)

- Setup per document set: readers for the client's LPA, amendments, side letters, resolution and term sheet formats; PDF to text; a fund glossary.
- The memo front end: pull every number and the sentence it sits in.
- Fund retune: verdict refusals for investing, value normaliser for figures, the same-subject rule for a traced figure, side-letter precedence for the named investor, golden cases for each.
- A locked no-model setting, with a test that no call leaves the machine.
- Load every file or refuse (no silent skips). A Word reader that keeps automatic clause numbers and stops at live
  tracked changes, comments and manual redlines. A PDF cleaner for headers and page numbers. OCR, or a refusal, for scans.
- One dry run on a past, already-decided memo the client picks, before any live one.
- The report: one row per number, with traced, inputs traced but math not checked, or not covered, and the clause link.
