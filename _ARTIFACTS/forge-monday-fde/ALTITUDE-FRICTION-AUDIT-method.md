# Altitude and friction audit: a reusable discovery method
Use it when the problem space is new or undefined, and you need to plan, prioritize and find friction without trusting one point of view. Source: the chorus gauntlet built for the monday.com take-home, generalized. Contains no personal data. In a real organization, replace hypothesized personas with interviews.

## Core idea
Every initiative is read at five altitudes by people with different decisions and dealbreakers. Friction shows up where the same initiative looks different at different altitudes, or where detail gets lost between them. Tracing consequences three orders deep tells you which friction to fix first.

## Steps
1. **Map stakeholders by altitude.** Executive, strategic, operational, tactical, surgical. Add department. Mark who decides, who influences, who reviews, and who can veto on process or policy.
2. **For each altitude, record:** the decision they own, their read budget (how long they will give it), and the fidelity that satisfies them.
3. **Elicit dealbreakers.** Ask each persona (or real person): "What would make you say no? What would make you not look further?" Record the exact words.
4. **Trace three orders.** For each dealbreaker: first order (what happens directly), second order (what that causes), third order (what that causes after). Fix the first-order cause when the third-order result is severe.
5. **Build the claim ladder.** Each top-level claim, then the argument under it, then the plan, then the steps, then the evidence. Mark any rung with no support.
6. **Find friction hotspots:** conflicts between altitudes (executives want brevity, surgical readers want depth), handoffs that lose detail, claims that change between levels, and numbers that disagree.
7. **Find the consonance spine:** what many altitudes want first. Lead with it.
8. **Prioritize** each friction item on: severity at the third order, how many altitudes it touches, reversibility (is it a one-way door?), cost to fix, and strength of evidence.
9. **Design cheap tests.** For each top item, state a falsifier: what observation would show you are wrong.
10. **Log decisions with dates and sources** so the trail is real-time, not reconstructed.

## Discovery questions by altitude
| Altitude | Ask |
| --- | --- |
| Executive | What outcome would make this worth your attention? What would make you stop it? What number do you watch? |
| Strategic | What does this need to be consistent with? What are you sequencing it against? What has to be true for it to scale? |
| Operational | What does running this cost in time and people? Where do handoffs break? What do you need to estimate and staff it? |
| Tactical | What does a user do first? Where do people get stuck? What would you need to teach it? |
| Surgical | What could be wrong or unsafe? Which edge cases matter? How would we know it is working? |

## For an undefined problem space, add
- **Quiet signals:** what people avoid saying, what they route around, what they check twice.
- **Abductive prompts:** "What is the best explanation for this friction?" Then state a test that would kill the explanation.
- **Inliers and false positives:** list friction that looks real but is explained by something simpler, and test it before acting.
- **One-way doors:** flag decisions that are hard to reverse and require an explicit sign-off first.

## Outputs
A stakeholder-by-altitude map, a ranked friction list with three-order paths, a consonance spine, a short list of cheap tests, and a dated decision log.

## Where it is useful
Organizational planning and prioritization, a friction audit of a process or rollout, a discovery phase for a customer deployment, and the take-home's discovery section.
