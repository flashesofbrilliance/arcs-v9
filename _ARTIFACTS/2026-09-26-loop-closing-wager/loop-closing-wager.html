Why the first test doesn't settle it

Czar's test measures adoption, but his claim is about desire. People could adopt agents in huge numbers to pass time or for company. That would support his "eat, poop, sleep" view while his own test scores him as wrong.

Six's claim has no deadline. "Will take time to diffuse" can explain any shortfall, so as written it can never fail.

Definitions

Agreed up front so nobody wins on a technicality.

Personal agent
Software that takes an action in an outside system on the user's behalf: sends, books, pays, cancels, or files. A chatbot that only answers questions does not count.
Mainstream
16% of US adults use one at least weekly. This is the "early majority" line from Rogers' diffusion-of-innovations model, the point where a technology stops belonging to early adopters.
Proactive loop-closing
The agent notices an open item (unanswered message, unpaid bill, unbooked appointment) and handles it or proposes handling it, without the user starting the task.
Resolution date
2028-09-26, czar's own two years.
The crux
One question separates the two positions When closing a loop costs almost no effort, do people keep the help on?

Czar says people would rather not even think about their open loops. An agent's job is to remove the thinking. So the test: among people whose agent had proactive loop-closing switched on by default, is it still on after 90 days?

> 50% still on → six < 50% muted or off → czar

Every claim and its kill condition

Each side's position splits into smaller claims. Each one names what would prove it wrong.

IDClaimWrong if, by 2028-09-26…
SIX-1Agents close loops at scaleThe median active user delegates fewer than about 3 real actions a week, or most delegated tasks need manual rework.
SIX-2Closing loops reduces stressAgent users show no drop in perceived stress (PSS-10 scale) compared with similar non-users. Includes the rebound case, where cheaper loop-closing leads people to open more loops.
SIX-3Freed attention goes to what mattersTime-use data shows most reclaimed time goes to passive consumption instead of the priorities users named themselves.
SIX-4Diffusion is slow but comingMainstream (16%) is not reached by six to set.
CZAR-1People don't want to close loops, even with helpMore than 50% keep default-on proactive loop-closing after 90 days.
CZAR-2People would rather not think about their loopsWhen shown their open loops, most users act on them instead of dismissing or muting the feature.
CZAR-3People default to passing timeFreed time goes mostly to self-named priorities. This is the same measurement as SIX-3, read from the other side.

Ground rules
