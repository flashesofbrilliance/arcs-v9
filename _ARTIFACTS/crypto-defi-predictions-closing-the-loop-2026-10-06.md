## What the pattern says about the method

1. Strongest signal: the regulatory direction. Banks won the lobbying on stablecoin yield (GENIUS Act passed; yield fight now centers on the Section 404 compromise). Deposit-token plumbing is being built by banks on the timeline implied.
2. Weakest signal: magnitude and timing. A dated cascade needs a trigger, and the trigger (Jan 28 statement) was misread in two pieces. Both the T-bill "hit" and the wstETH cascade rest on that one document.
3. Self-grading is not a receipt. The "S-TIER, 99.8% confidence" gauntlet marks sit on pieces whose central premise did not survive a read of the primary text. Treat in-house grades as draft status only.
4. The Collateral Wars call was published five days after gold's all-time high. It named the winning asset near the top and its own falsifier then fired.
5. What worked: stated falsifiers. Rows 2a and 5 resolved cleanly because the piece named the exact condition in advance.

## Borrowers who levered at the high: what lenders do with staked collateral (2026-10-06, search-sourced)

- Liquid staking tokens (wstETH) can be liquidated on-chain; native staked ETH cannot (locked, exit-queue limited, about 57,600 ETH a day can exit as of October 2026). Lenders with native-staked collateral rely on margin calls, haircuts, top-ups or negotiated unwinds. The Ethereum validator exit queue had fallen to zero by January 2026, so there was no backlog of stakers trying to leave around the January and February drawdown (secondary).

- Notional rebalancing and USDC: margin top-ups create USDC demand, and collateral sales convert ETH into USDC that stays in circulation. Supply only falls if proceeds are redeemed with the issuer, which the collateral-backed stablecoin data above do not show.
