Verdict on the pre-registered test

These are six milestone points chosen as the first at each pixel count, a frontier staircase. A fit to them describes the frontier, not what most people used, and six points cannot show that the pace itself is accelerating. "Compounding innovation" is a story told about the curve; the data here are only pixel counts.

Rows, bit depth, launch price and sources

Rings on the chart (not in the table): the era dial's documented minimum modes, 512 x 342 (1984), 640 x 480 (1992 and 1995) and 800 x 600 (2001). The 1992 and 1995 rings share one point on the vertical axis because the two minimums are the same mode. They sit below the first-product points by design: a minimum is not a frontier.

What is and is not claimed

Dates and modes come from press and archival pages found by web search on 2026-10-16, not from primary documents, and "first" is the maker's claim for the 1080p and 8K rows while sources disagree on who was first at 4K. Bit depth is documented for the first two rows only, so the axis is pixels, not bits. Prices exist for four rows across three kinds of product (a whole computer, a laptop, two televisions), so they are listed as notes and never turned into a cost-per-pixel curve: nothing here shows that cost falls.
