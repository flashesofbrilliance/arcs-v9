Candidate positioning brief · for RecruitHaus

Zach Harris

For the Blacksmith Staff Product Engineer search · a one-page brief to carry to the team, and to price the offer.

The short version: you are not putting forward a specialist. You are putting forward the connective tissue, one hire who does the work of three.

Two hires in one. A forward-deployed engineer, an applied-AI engineer, and a product engineer, in one person. That is the whole pitch, and it is checkable.

What that means, in plain terms

Ships with your customersforward-deployed engineer
Sits with the customer or founder, co-designs the thing, and ships it in the field, not from behind a spec.
≈ normally a dedicated hire
Builds your AIapplied-AI engineer
Builds the AI-native features and stands up the internal agent workflows the rest of the team then runs on.
≈ normally a scarce, expensive specialist
Owns the productproduct engineer
Owns the product end to end, not just the code, makes the hard call under the surface instead of routing it.
≈ normally a product manager plus an engineer

One senior hire covers all three. That is rare, and it is the exact shape this role is asking for.

Why this beats a deeper specialist, for this role

A single-threaded specialist

Goes very deep in one lane (say, pure infrastructure, or pure machine learning). Excellent, but needs two more people around them to cover the product and the customer, plus the coordination between all three.

Zach, multi-threaded

Fluent across product, AI, and customer at once. For a company productizing coding agents, the job is the seam between those three, so the cross-lane person is the fit, not the compromise. A high-signal sleeper by design: small-batch craft over big-company scale, so the signal is in the work, not the résumé brand.

The part that compounds

He does not just close his own tickets. He brings the agent-native way of working (delegating to coding agents at scale, with the guardrail discipline to keep it safe), the same capability Blacksmith is building for its customers. In the first 90 days he sets the pattern the team compounds on and accelerates their internal applied-AI effort. A force multiplier, not a seat filled.

How to price it

The comp logic, for the offer conversation

If one hire covers what would otherwise be two specialized roles, then a premium on a single salary is a discount, against two loaded salaries plus the coordination tax between them. Price him at the top of the band, not the middle. The ROI math is on one-covers-two, and every claim behind it is verifiable before the offer.

Why this is a low-risk bet

This was our shared read on the call: bet on the right person and the upside compounds; bet wrong and, at this stage, it can spiral. Zach is the low-variance, high-upside version of that bet, because you can verify it before you place it: a live commit trail, an evidence-graded résumé, and a build that shows exactly what it refused to claim.

When they push back: the three lenses

The team reads through three lenses. Each raises a different objection; each has a first answer, and a deeper one if they keep pushing. These are inferred from public profiles, not from the room, use them as filters, not scripts.

Lens 1 · Product + business (the CEO seat)“Is this real product instinct, or a nice personal project? Where's the market pull?”
→ He took an ambiguous, no-spec problem to a running, opinionated system, solo. That is the 0-to-1 instinct the seat needs, and he leads with market humility: ask what he'd cut from his own build if a user showed up tomorrow. He has the list.
2nd order If they push: “but there are no external users yet.”
→ The zero-to-revenue rep is in the client and advisory work; the personal build proves he takes vague to shipped with no spec to hide behind. Both, not one.
3rd order Still skeptical: “will he build what the market wants, not what he likes?”
→ He reads his own work adversarially, that is what the evidence-graded résumé and the cut-list are. He designs for the arriving user, not his own taste.
Lens 2 · Skeptical systems (co-CTO)“Has he actually written systems code? Isn't agent-driven speed a corruption risk here?”
→ Concede the systems ramp is real, weeks not zero. But agent-safety is his edge, not his risk: a year as the adversarial power-user of coding agents, the exact surface you're productizing, with the reflex to never merge on a plausible green.
2nd order If they push: “concretely, will HE ship a corruption bug moving fast?”
→ His velocity is fenced by mechanical gates, lint, build, leak-checks, tests, auto-rollback, all in CI on every change. He treats agent output as false until a test draws blood. The failure mode they fear is the one he's spent a year instrumenting against.
3rd order Still skeptical: “prove the depth.”
→ Don't take it on faith, test it live: hand him the gnarliest recent incident in the first screen and watch him reason, not bluff. He closes depth gaps fast and never fakes understanding.
Lens 3 · Rigor + gates (co-CTO)“Can a solo builder hold a bar on a team? Will quality survive his speed?”
→ His instinct is to encode the standard so it enforces itself, the same pattern as a good check suite. Speed and rigor aren't opposed for him; the gates are what let him move fast safely.
2nd order If they push: “solo isn't team leadership.”
→ The highest technical leadership is the guardrail that keeps everyone safe without you in the room. He came up standing up practices in regulated teams, that is the rep.
3rd order Still skeptical: “where does he actually fit?”
→ Near your platform and applied-AI work, where the standard he encodes accelerates everyone. His verification discipline is your culture, not a foreign body.

Best / base / worst case

Best case

The connective tissue within a quarter: ships product fast, accelerates the internal applied-AI effort, and lifts the whole team's agent-workflow discipline. The force-multiplier outcome.

Base case

A strong senior product engineer who ships in days, ramps on systems depth over the first months, and quietly raises the bar on how the team uses coding agents. Clear net-positive.

Worst case

The systems-depth ramp is slower than hoped, or the breadth doesn't convert to the one depth they most need. Contained, because the guardrails below make it recoverable.

De-risk it: ARCS circuit breakers

◆
Scoped paid trial first. A two-to-four-week real project before a full commit. Verify the bet with work, not interviews.
◆
30/60/90 milestone gate. Pre-agreed, shipped-artifact success criteria at each mark. No vibes, checkable.
◆
Week-one shipped PR. His ship-speed makes a real first-week artifact cheap; you see the pattern immediately.
◆
Incident pairing in the loop. Hand him a live incident in a screen; the ramp risk is visible before the offer, not after.
◆
Public reference-checkability. The commit trail, graded résumé, and the Forge mean the downside is bounded by evidence you can read today.

Each turns the worst case from a loss into a lesson you catch early. That is the ARCS move, and the same instinct he'd bring to your infra decisions: never optimize grip strength on a no-rope route, add the rope.
