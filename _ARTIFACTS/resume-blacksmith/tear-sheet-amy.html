Candidate positioning brief · for RecruitHaus

Zach Harris

For the Blacksmith Staff Product Engineer search · a one-page brief to carry to the team, and to price the offer.

The short version: you are not putting forward a specialist. You are putting forward the connective tissue — one hire who does the work of three.

Two hires in one. A forward-deployed engineer, an applied-AI engineer, and a product engineer, in one person. That is the whole pitch, and it is checkable.

What that means, in plain terms

Ships with your customersforward-deployed engineer
Sits with the customer or founder, co-designs the thing, and ships it in the field — not from behind a spec. Usually a dedicated hire.
Builds your AIapplied-AI engineer
Builds the AI-native features and stands up the internal agent workflows the rest of the team then runs on. Usually a scarce, expensive specialist.
Owns the productproduct engineer
Owns the product end to end, not just the code — makes the hard call under the surface instead of routing it. Usually a product manager plus an engineer.

One senior hire covers all three. That is rare, and it is the exact shape this role is asking for.

Why this beats a deeper specialist — for this role

A single-threaded specialist

Goes very deep in one lane (say, pure infrastructure, or pure machine learning). Excellent — but needs two more people around them to cover the product and the customer, plus the coordination between all three.

Zach, multi-threaded

Fluent across product, AI, and customer at once. For a company productizing coding agents, the job is the seam between those three — so the cross-lane person is the fit, not the compromise.

The part that compounds

He does not just close his own tickets. He brings the agent-native way of working — delegating to coding agents at scale, with the guardrail discipline to keep it safe — which is exactly the capability Blacksmith is building for its own customers.

So the first 90 days are not one person's output. He sets the pattern the team compounds on and jumpstarts their internal applied-AI effort. You are buying a force multiplier, not a seat filled.

How to price it

The comp logic, for the offer conversation

If one hire covers what would otherwise be two specialized roles, then a premium on a single salary is a discount — against two loaded salaries plus the coordination tax between them. Price him at the top of the band, not the middle. The ROI math is on one-covers-two, and every claim behind it is verifiable before the offer.

Why this is a low-risk bet

You said it best on our call: bet on the right person and the upside compounds; bet wrong and it can trigger a spiral. Zach is the low-variance, high-upside version of that bet — because you can verify it before you place it. The work is public: a live commit trail, an evidence-graded résumé, and a build that shows exactly how it was made and what it refused to claim.
