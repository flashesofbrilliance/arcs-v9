Questions & landmines

Ask before pitching. The first ten minutes should be his problems, not my deck: he has already read the deck.

  • Open with his words: "Joe said you think I could help with some current problems. Which ones are on fire right now?" · look for: two or three named, specific problems (a system, a customer, a deadline), not "scaling" in the abstract
  • The seat probe (req_reality gate question): "Does this land in an existing function, or is it something you're still designing?" · look for: an answer in his own words. Bespoke-and-still-being-designed is the Fanatics/HypeLab shape: keep the next step to a written read on one problem, not a role negotiation
  • Where does revenue come from in the next 12 months? Bloomberg and private-market clients, or government pilots? · look for: one motion he is betting on, with a reason. Public sector cycles run long; the private side is the faster proof
  • Who builds today? Engineering headcount, who ships the forecasts, how the open VP of Engineering role relates to this one · look for: a real number and a clear reporting line
  • His CTO question, from the deck: how far down can a doubting analyst open the path from sources to a single forecast number? And how does the system notice its own calibration drifting after a shock? · look for: whether he lights up (this is his domain) or deflects
  • Runway, asked plainly: "What does the next 12 to 18 months look like on funding?" · look for: a straight answer. A pre-seed company with a $300K seat needs a reason
  • Close: offer to come back within a few days with a short written read on one of his named problems; confirm the Anthony call · look for: he names the problem to write about
  • Landmine: comp. Do not raise it. Joe owns the fee and the number. If Lyndon raises it: flexible, people and problem first, Joe has the range. Zach (2026-09-30): "I'm not going to negotiate against myself." If he cites a number Joe gave him: do not confirm or restate it. "I'd rather we figure out what the seat is first; Joe and I can sort the number after."
  • Landmine: pitching ARCS as a product. He builds explainable AI forecasting; ARCS reads as a neighboring product. Use it only as proof of how I work (source-graded, receipts, backtest-style scoring), never as a thing I want to bring with me
  • Landmine: the Solution Architect hat. Do not agree to forward-deployed, DC-heavy travel by default. Zach's limit (2026-09-30): not "traveling 25 to 50% of the time"
  • Landmine: clearances. Do not overclaim government access. Ask what the DOD work requires instead
  • Landmine: seniority. Do not argue with the young-gun framing. Frame senior as de-risking, the line Zach already gave Joe: "you're not paying somebody to learn on your dime"
