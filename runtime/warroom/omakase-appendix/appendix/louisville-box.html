A sashimono box holds with no nails — every joint cut inside the wood, invisible, load-bearing. A himitsu-bako opens only in sequence — each slide earned by the one before. This thread, formalized, is that box.

Omotenashi, the governing doctrine: what is hidden from the guest is hidden for the guest. The mizuya — the preparation room — stays out of sight not to deceive the tea guest but so the experience arrives whole: sublime, harmonious, uncluttered by mechanism. The fence said this in carpenter's terms; the flash said it in light; the box says it in wood, and adds the final property — the joints are what make it beautiful, and they are exactly the parts you cannot see.
