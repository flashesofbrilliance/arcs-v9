## 1. Canonical Archetypes: The Atomic Family in Full Resolution

### 1.1 The Seven, As Originally Defined

The canonical Book of ARCS defines seven “Atomic Family” energies that live within and between people:[1]

- Dreamer (IPLR) – child who takes things apart; Inventor/Pattern‑Seeker/Learner/Researcher.
- Guardian (CEL) – parent who watches for danger; Catalyst/Executor/Leader.
- Elder (LRV) – grandparent who remembers; Ledger/Recorder/Validator.
- Shapeshifter (BFQ) – river that finds new paths; Balanced/Flexible/Quick.
- Bridge (CVI) – weaver who connects worlds; Cross‑domain/Varied/Integrator.
- Heart (SBW) – one who holds space; Spaceholder/Bilateral/Witness.
- Sage (FPER) – jeweler who sees truth; Filter/Precision/Eliminator/Refiner.

Each archetype has:[1]

- A Genesis parable (origin myth).
- A Lesson (gift, curse, primary question).
- A Therapeutic reflection (reframes pathologized traits as design features).
- A Meditation + Intention (somatic anchoring + language upgrade).
- Energy tags (activation energy, sustained energy, ROI, timeline).

### 1.2 Dreamer (IPLR) — Exploration as Oxygen

Core question: “What if?”[1]

Gift: Innovation, pattern recognition, possibility generation.
Curse: Restlessness, inability to finish, scattered energy.
Shadow (typical): Elder – fear that commitment kills curiosity.

Genesis compression:

Child who takes everything apart (including relationships) to see how they work.

World calls them “unfocused” and “too much”; they keep going because curiosity = breath.[1]

Rebuilds father’s radio “better than before,” leaves note: “I had to see how it worked. I’m not sorry I learned.” Father weeps in recognition of his exiled self.[1]

Therapeutic reframe: You are not flaky; you are a scout, not a settler. Settling without scouting is misalignment; scouting without any settling is chaos. You need Guardians (urgency), Elders (memory), Sages (filters) as convergence rails, not as jailers.[1]

Somatic test: Hands twitch when imagining taking something apart; body leans forward in curiosity during Somatic Scan.[1]

### 1.3 Guardian (CEL) — Stakes as Oxygen

Core question: “What’s at stake?”[1]

Gift: Execution, focus, decisiveness.
Curse: Chronic stress, control, burnout.
Shadow (typical): Dreamer – fear that exploration wastes precious time.

Genesis compression:

Shepherd who always watches horizon; “anxious” to others, but flocks survive because of vigilance.[1]

“You call it anxiety. I call it survival.” But privately weeps; cannot ever truly rest.[1]

Therapeutic reframe: You’re not controlling; you’re over‑fitting to environments where no one else watched. The move from “everything is urgent” to “urgency with receipts” breaks the adrenal cage.[1]

Protocol link: Foundational Shift #2 (Receipts) is explicitly designed to show Guardians that 40–60% of “emergencies” were never real.[1]

### 1.4 Elder (LRV) — Memory as Shield and Cage

Core question: “Can we trust this?”[1]

Gift: Trust, validation, wisdom, pattern‑based prediction.
Curse: Rigidity, inability to forgive, living in past pain.
Shadows (typical): Dreamer, Shapeshifter – novelty == disguised repeat betrayal.

Genesis compression:

Village elder remembers every promise and betrayal; charismatic stranger offers prosperity, elder demands receipts: “Promises are cheap. Receipts are truth. Show me proof of what you have done.”[1]

Stranger fails, later ruins another village; Elder’s memory saves her people, but she is trapped in never‑forget mode.[1]

Therapeutic reframe: You are not “negative”; you are the checksum of the system. Without you, communities fall for the same con twice. With you unchecked, no new pattern can enter.[1]

Upgrade: Elder transforms when memory is coupled to update rules: new receipts can overwrite old priors instead of hard‑locking them.[1]

### 1.5 Shapeshifter (BFQ) — Adaptation as First Principle

Core question: “What’s possible now?”[1]

Gift: Flexibility, resilience, non‑attachment.
Curse: No anchor, “flaky,” identity diffusion.
Shadow (typical): Elder/Guardian – terror of being stuck.

Genesis compression:

River whose path is blocked by earthquake; villagers panic, river simply finds a new path.[1]

Years later old channel reopens; some demand river “return to true path,” but river stays with lower‑energy new channel: loyalty to reality, not nostalgia.[1]

Therapeutic reframe: You are loyal to reality, not to plans. Western culture mislabels that as disloyalty. Your work is not to stop flowing; it’s to identify your water (the invariant essence beneath endless adaptation).[1]

### 1.6 Bridge (CVI) — Belonging Across Difference

Core question: “What if we connected these worlds?”[1]

Gift: Bridge‑building, perspective‑taking, synthesis.
Curse: No home, translator exhaustion, “I don’t fit anywhere.”
Shadow (typical): Sage/Elder – narrowing feels like death.

Genesis compression:

Weaver travels mountains, deserts, seas; each insists its way is the only true one.[1]

She weaves their threads into a tapestry; villagers disapprove, travelers weep in recognition of themselves in the shared cloth.[1]

Therapeutic reframe: Your lane is synthesis. The missing move is a single Elder anchor: one place where you are not translating, you are simply known.[1]

### 1.7 Heart (SBW) — The Translator of Pain

Core question: “What are you really saying?”[1]

Gift: Deep listening, facilitation, bilateral witnessing.
Curse: Exhaustion, resentment, “I’m everyone’s therapist and no one asks how I am.”
Shadow (typical): Sage/Guardian – fear of saying “no” and enforcing limits.[1]

Genesis compression:

Child in multi‑lingual village hears in stone, wind, and waves the same underlying longing.[1]

Becomes the Heart who resolves fights, but privately never speaks her own language.[1]

Therapeutic reframe: You are not a doormat; you are infrastructure. Healthy infrastructure has capacity limits and maintenance windows. Boundaries are not aggression; they’re uptime protection.[1]

### 1.8 Sage (FPER) — Signal in a Sea of Glass

Core question: “Is this signal or noise?”[1]

Gift: Clarity, precision, quality control.
Curse: Isolation, “too critical,” fear of false positives.
Shadow (typical): Dreamer/Shapeshifter – terror of letting in garbage.

Genesis compression:

Jeweler rejects 9/10 “diamonds” as flawed; merchants hate him, but the one genuine stone he validates turns out to be priceless.[1]

“I do not fear saying NO nine times to say YES once.” Yet people stop bringing him stones—loneliness as side effect of rigor.[1]

Therapeutic reframe: You’re not impossible; you’re a false‑positive minimizer. The hinge is separating “good enough to try” from “good enough to commit.” That distinction maintains standards while allowing experimentation.[1]

## 2. INIT: The Four‑Channel Diagnostic at Full Detail

The original book already defines a multi‑channel INIT diagnostic; here it’s expanded explicitly as a multi‑modal measurement instrument.[1]

### 2.1 Sacred Heptagram — Visual Channel

This yields a vector from center → primary and a counter‑vector from center → shadow.

### 2.2 Somatic Scan — Body Channel

The Somatic Scan is not a vibe check; it’s a structured interoceptive assay:[1]

For each archetype, the script gives a vivid scenario (garage at midnight, 24‑hour deadline, charismatic stranger, earthquake river, multi‑lingual room, conflict mediation, 10 idea pitch).[1]

Subject notes for each: open vs. contract, forward vs. backward lean, breath deepening vs. shallow.

The body verdict is recorded:

Energy that produced maximum openness = candidate primary. Energy that produced maximum contraction = candidate shadow.[1]

### 2.3 Mirror Test — Relational Channel

Exercises interrogate who makes you feel “same species”:[1]

List 3 people you feel most alive with.

Mark archetypal traits (explore, urgency, memory, adapt, connect, hold space, filter noise).

Insight: Shared energy is either primary (energizing) or shadow (longing).[1]

This detects relational projection patterns and cluster biases (e.g., 3 Dreamers → Dreamer tribe).[1]

### 2.4 Receipt Inventory — Analytical Channel

You construct a micro‑dataset of your last 5 major decisions:[1]

Jobs, cities, relationships, projects.

For each: what attracted you, what made you stay, what made you leave.

Heuristics map patterns to archetypes:

- Leave at routine → Dreamer.
- Leave at constant pressure → Dreamer/Shapeshifter.
- Leave when expectations unmet → Guardian/Elder.
- Leave when isolated → Bridge/Heart.
- Leave when standards low → Elder/Sage.[1]

### 2.5 Atomic Signature — Multi‑Channel Consensus

All four channels are synthesized:[1]

- Geometry: primary / shadow.
- Somatic: primary / shadow.
- Mirror: primary / shadow.
- Receipts: primary / shadow.

You then compute consensus primary and consensus shadow across channels, and explicitly note secondary as the bridging archetype used to cope.[1]

This signature is the routing input for where to start reading, which parables to prioritize, and which Foundational Shifts matter most.[1]

## 4. Foundational Shifts: Full, Clinic‑Grade Protocols

### 4.1 Shadow Integration — Exiled Parts Negotiation

The Installment 1 text already contains a nearly complete IFS‑style protocol; here it is reframed as a 4‑phase intervention.[1]

Phase 1 – Identification (Weeks 1–2): Trigger Map: last 3 strong negative reactions → map to archetypes → candidate shadow. Cross‑check with INIT diagnostic to ensure coherence.[1]

Phase 2 – Bilateral Letters (Weeks 3–4): Letter to shadow: reason for exile, inherited narratives, past harms. Letter from shadow: its protective intent and fear model. This surfaces the underlying safety contracts that currently lock you in a lose–lose pattern.[1]

Phase 3 – Mediated Dialogue (Weeks 5–8): You hold the Observer seat. Primary and Shadow both articulate needs; Observer proposes testable compromises. Example: Dreamer explores for 2 weeks; Elder demands 3 receipts before committing 3 months.[1]

Phase 4 – Daily 2‑min Integration (Weeks 9+): Check‑in: “What does my primary need today? What does my shadow need?” Design micro‑actions that honor both.

Outcome markers: internal sabotage events (starting then quitting; over‑promising then ghosting; panic at commitment) should reduce in frequency and amplitude over 3–6 months.[1]

### 4.2 Receipts Culture — Turning Life into a Dataset

Receipts transform story‑based memory into data‑backed models.[1] Schema: Context → Prediction → Why → Actual → Learning.

Can be solo (career, money, health) or relational (co‑founders, couples, families).[1]

Examples in canon: job decisions, co‑founder tension over launch timing, client boundary fears.[1]

Weekly practice (15 minutes): Close last week’s open receipts with actuals. Compute subjective “I over/under‑estimated X” notes. Extract one updated rule per week (e.g., “My urgency intuition overshoots by 40% without Elder validation”).[1]

Scaling: at 10 receipts you see hints; 30 receipts yield a usable self‑model; 100 receipts create a personal priors library that outperforms intuition alone.[1]

### 4.3 Triadic Repair — Third‑Force Introduction

Canonical text gives a structured trio‑based repair protocol.[1] Use when: Dyad is locked in blame–resentment–silence. Same argument recurs with only nouns swapped.

Third force types: Person (therapist, advisor, friend embodying missing archetype). Protocol (shared metrics/receipts that both agree to trust more than ego). Temporal catalyst (external deadline that reframes stakes).[1]

Mechanics: weekly 1‑hour triadic session; both parties speak; third force reflects and extracts next small experiment; each meeting generates a joint receipt.[1]

Over 3–6 months, the third force’s function is internalized; the pair can then self‑mediate using the same structure.[1]
