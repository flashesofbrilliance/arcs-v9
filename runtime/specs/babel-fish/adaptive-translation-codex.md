## STATED vs. REVEALED — THE RECALIBRATION DIAGNOSTIC

*Before translating to any native register, run this diagnostic. The delta between what a persona states about themselves and what their behavior reveals is the calibration signal for NCI accuracy.*

**The epistemological constraint:**
Conscious processing runs at approximately 50 bits/second. Humans receive ~11 million bits/second through their senses. What a person can *state* — about their preferences, fears, risk appetite, confidence — is bounded by their conscious bottleneck. Their *revealed* behavior leaks from the rest.

**Why this matters for translation:**
A persona's stated register is what they say they respond to. Their revealed register is what actually moves them. The delta between the two is where overconfidence, underconfidence, and biased framing live.

| Diagnostic | What it surfaces | ARCS recalibration action |
|---|---|---|
| **Stated confidence > Revealed behavior** | Overconfidence — asserting certainty while avoiding commitment | Lower NCI; probe the gap; don't match their stated confidence level |
| **Stated confidence < Revealed behavior** | Underconfidence — understating capability, revealing competence under pressure | Raise NCI; give the person the credit they won't claim |
| **Stated risk appetite ≠ Revealed choices** | Risk framing mismatch — they say they want bold bets, they reveal conservative anchors | Translate to their revealed risk register, not their stated one |
| **Stated priorities ≠ Revealed attention** | POV skew — they say they care about X, their energy goes to Y | Lead with Y; use X as validation |

**The DEWY receipt as revealed-layer artifact:**
Every DEWY decision receipt is a snapshot of the revealed layer — what was actually happening in the room, not what the stated agenda said was happening. The receipt externalizes the 50-bit bottleneck: it captures the structural pattern that the conscious mind wasn't fully processing in real time. The gap between what you *thought* was happening (stated) and what the receipt shows (revealed) is the recalibration data.

**Per-persona application (read before translating):**
Before selecting a translation register, ask: what is this persona's *stated* self-description vs. what does their actual behavior reveal about how they process? The stated/revealed gap narrows the right entry point.

## THE 50 BITS / SEC CONSTRAINT — Why Stated ≠ Revealed

*The deepest explanation for why adaptive translation exists as a discipline.*

Human consciousness processes approximately 50 bits/second of the ~11 million bits/second arriving through the senses. What a person can *state* about their preferences, fears, and risk appetite is bounded by this bottleneck. Their *revealed* behavior — what they react to, what makes them lean forward, what makes them disengage — leaks from the rest.

**For the ARCS practitioner:** You have access to more signal than the persona does about themselves. Body language, energy shifts, the question they started and stopped, the word they used twice — these are revealed-layer data. Your job is to translate to the frequency that reaches through the bottleneck, not just the frequency they consciously report.

**The stated/revealed delta narrows:**
- Overconfidence and underconfidence (skewed self-assessment)
- Risk appetite mismatch (stated boldness, revealed conservatism — or the reverse)
- POV distortion (conscious framing doesn't match actual priority structure)

**The DEWY receipt externalizes this:** Every receipt captures what was structurally happening in the conversation — the momentum map, the recoil zones, the CLUE patterns — regardless of what either party consciously stated was happening. The receipt is the revealed layer made legible and learnable.

*The gap between what you said the conversation was and what the receipt shows it was — that's your recalibration data.*
