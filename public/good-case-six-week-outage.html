The Case of the Six-Week Outage

A change shipped with every checkmark green. Then one person asked the smallest question in software, how do I know this actually works, and the answer was a core feature that had been dead in production for six weeks without a single complaint. You have the evidence. You do not get to guess. You get to falsify.

  1. Each theory states its kill condition up front: the evidence that would prove it false.
  2. Pick a theory, then present the evidence you think kills it.
  3. The right evidence strikes it out. The wrong evidence costs a test.
  4. The true theory cannot be killed. Evidence against it only comes back consistent.
  5. When one theory is left standing, the question closes.

The hole in the middle

You closed all three questions from the record alone. Now look at what the record never said.

The evidence shows the production database sat outside the step that applies migrations on deploy. It does not show when that was decided, by whom, or why. That missing decision is the donut hole: the one part of this case nobody can reconstruct, because it was never written down.

A receipt at that moment would have closed it. That is the entire argument for receipts, in one case.

What changed after this case: a health check that compares the live database to what the code expects, a screen that looks for the saved record before it claims failure, and migrations that run on every deploy.
