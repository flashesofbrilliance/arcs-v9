This is the instrument, run on my own tape. Everything scored on this tab is a read of my own performance and predictions, not yours. The method crosses freely, the subject stays me. You get the whole machine, pointed at myself.

The idea is simple: two people on a call are either tracking each other or they are not, and you can hear which on the tape. ARCS listens to a recorded call and maps that, beat by beat, into four states, and files a falsifiable bet before the call so it can be scored honestly after.

The checks that keep a read honest

Reading energy off a transcript is easy to fake, so the read has to survive a few evals before it ships. These cross the fence; the recipe underneath does not.

  • It is traceable. Every beat traces to a real line in the transcript. If it is an inference, it is labelled one. Your words are in quotes; my read is labelled mine.
  • It files a bet first. The ex-ante receipt is written before the call, so the read is scored against reality afterward, not rationalized. If it cannot fail, it is not a receipt.
  • It meets you at your level. It checks the register each person was speaking at. Meeting you as the practitioner who built this, not explaining the value to the person who invented it.
  • It admits mistakes. The belief I flagged as most likely wrong is the one it scored as a miss, in writing, and moved the wedge. The instrument corrects the person who filed it.

The pre-meeting briefing, made real

Run forward instead of backward, the same instrument is the exec brief you already build by hand. Before a call: here is the person, the register to meet them on, the altitude to open at. After: here is where it actually landed, and the honest diff against what you predicted.

For your world that is the exec brief, closed and scored: first and third party signal stitched into a read, then a receipt that says whether the read held. It reads recordings that already exist, and it runs model-agnostic on Gemini, so nothing leaves Google.
