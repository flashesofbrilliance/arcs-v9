What you are looking at

This is the instrument, run on my own tape. Everything scored on this tab is a read of my performance on our two calls, not yours. That is deliberate. The method crosses freely, the subject stays me. You get the whole machine, pointed at myself.

The idea is simple: a conversation is a pair of waves. When two people are on the same wavelength the signal builds, when they are not it fades. ARCS listens to a recorded call and maps that, beat by beat, into four states.

The four states it hears

In sync

You finish each other's thoughts, the idea builds. Confidence grows.

Human

Humor and human warmth. The thing that carries a call even when the content is thin.

Useful pushback

You push back and it sharpens the other person. Disagreement that adds something, not noise.

Out of sync

Paragraphs in, one word out. The place a briefing is supposed to catch before it happens.

Tab 1 is that map, drawn on our two calls. The gold and green beats are where we were aligned. The red beats are my mid-curves. Same four states, run on any call.

The checks that keep a read honest

Reading energy off a transcript is easy to fake, so the read has to survive a few evals before it ships. These cross the fence, the recipe underneath does not.

  • It is traceable. Every beat traces to a real line in the transcript. If it is an inference, it is labelled one.
  • It keeps the why. The read keeps the why of a moment, not just what was said.
  • It meets you at your level. It checks the register each person was speaking at, and flags where one went over the other. That is literally what caught my mid-curves.
  • It admits mistakes. When the first read was wrong it says so. On our call two I first credited you with a "whoa" of enthusiasm. On the re-listen, that "whoa" was mine, about your coffee maker. The instrument corrected me, in writing.

The pre-meeting briefing, made real

Run forward instead of backward, the same instrument is the thing you said nobody has perfected. Before a call: here is the person, here is the wavelength to meet them on, here is the altitude to open at. After: here is where it actually landed, and the honest diff against what you expected.

For your world that is a read across a GTM team's calls: which reps land, which moments go lossy, where a deal quietly falls out of phase before anyone names it. Nothing to wire up to start. It reads recordings that already exist.
