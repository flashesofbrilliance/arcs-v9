02 · Narrative Atoms

Each big choice becomes a reusable atom: one decision, one trade‑off, one tripwire, one review gate.

Atoms for this Parlance‑style brief

Demo set
Deck Structure · Cohesive vs Modular
Decision: Modular proof‑point library for Series B. Trade‑off: memorable narrative vs precision. Tripwire: if investor questions show loss of thread by pitch 5, switch to cohesive.
Positioning Tone · Aspirational vs Evidence‑Based
Decision: Evidence‑based, leading with unit economics and clinical outcomes. Trade‑off: less "fashion" energy, more investor trust. Tripwire: if consumer tests show drop in brand affinity, add aspirational layer in v2.
Brand Governance · Green / Yellow / Red
Decision: Every new asset tagged Green, Yellow, or Red against the new narrative. Tripwire: 3 consecutive Yellow or Red tags from the same team triggers a narrative review.

04 · Stress‑Test Strip

Every big idea runs Strawman → Red Team → Steelman → Blue Team → Purple Pill before it ships.

Stress‑Testing the new investor story

Five passes
Strawman

Weak version: "We’re now a longevity brand." No numbers, no proof. Easy to dismiss.

Red Team

Attack: "Unit economics don’t support the claim; clinical evidence thin; brand sprint is lipstick."

Steelman

Strong: "We’re building the infrastructure layer for longevity benefits, proven by X outcomes and Y unit economics."

Blue Team

Defend with investor POV: "We de‑risk the story by logging every choice as a narrative atom and showing tripwires."

Purple Pill

Integrate tension: keep fashion‑forward surface for consumers, evidence‑based core for investors.

06 · Operating Manual After 5 Engagements

The PDF’s last section becomes an explicit go‑faster / stop‑loss panel and pattern library.

Go‑Faster & Stop‑Loss Rules

From narrative to operating system

Go‑faster rules

  • Say yes immediately to positioning work where founder + CFO are pre‑aligned.
  • Say yes to Series B narratives where product roadmap is locked.
  • Say yes to rebrand requests tied to acquisition or raise timelines.

Stop‑loss rules

  • Fix first any "rebrand" ask without exec alignment.
  • Avoid positioning work before product‑market fit.
  • Defer brand governance for companies under a certain ARR threshold.

Patterns worth repeating

"Series B investors care about unit economics before narrative" · "Rebrand usually means 'we don't know our customer'" — explicitly surfaced here as the unfair advantage ARCS gives Parlance.
