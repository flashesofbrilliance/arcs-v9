Receipts or it didn't happen

When an agent marks a task done, the record is the only witness. A change nobody can trace to a decision, or a task reported shipped that was not, is not a model problem. It is an evidence problem, and evidence problems can be measured. We hold ourselves to the same rule.

Every entry names the belief, the test that broke it, and what replaced it. That number is written from the ledger itself, not typed by hand, so this page cannot claim more honesty than the log can show. Read the belief log

Our merges and publishes are logged as decision receipts in a ledger that refuses edits and deletes: a correction is a new row that names the one it replaces. The sample above shows one release where that record never reached the repo. We found it the same way you would.

How the audit works

  1. Gather. We collect the committed record for a window you pick: commits, artifacts, receipts. No chat transcripts, by design.
  2. Reconstruct cold. A fresh agent with zero context rebuilds what happened, and must cite the record for every claim or mark it unverified. It cannot narrate from memory because it has none.
  3. Map the gaps. Everything it could not rebuild becomes the gap map: your observability gap, section by section, with how well the record backs each one.
  4. Retrofit receipts. We add a lightweight receipt at the points that matter, especially before irreversible or outbound actions, so the next reconstruction closes those gaps.

What the first run found

The first cold run rebuilt 90% of the commits and artifacts in the incident's range from the committed record alone; the misses were index pages, with no decision riding on them (the report). A four-run follow-up, changing one thing at a time, compared cold and warm readers: warmth did not corrupt what a reader grounded, it changed whether the reader grounded at all (the control and the experiment).

What it will not do

  • It makes your agents' work explainable after the fact. It does not explain how a model thinks.
  • It is only as good as what you commit. Work that never reaches the record is invisible to a cold reader. That is the finding, not a failure of the audit.
  • It is not a compliance certification. It measures whether the record can rebuild the work; it does not sign off on regulation.
  • It does not claim a patent or a new science. The pieces (event logs, blameless post-mortems, cite-or-abstain review) are old. The framing is the useful part.
  • It never rates your people. It reads the record, not the team.
