Zach Harris · Candidate Brief

Zach Harris · Product Manager, Patient Experience

Conduit Health · via HappyPath

A hands-on product manager who owns the whole loop: set the roadmap, talk to users, read the funnel, prototype, work with engineering, test, launch, and iterate on behavior. I still ship production code weekly and built my own eval gates. Your two asks, as I heard them: a builder who can also carry go-to-market, and a patient experience that is sticky but engaging for older patients.

Actively interested in this role. New York metro; three days a week in Union Square works. Twenty years in product across regulated health, finance, and direct-to-consumer.

What you'll own, one to one
Retention and LTV
Done before: 73 percent program adherence at Ideal Protein, a PE-backed medically supervised diet program, where I was Head of Product Innovation on the turnaround team; lifecycle segmentation that grew net-new revenue 20 percent at the Children's Tumor Foundation. Where I'd start: a reorder cohort view (month 1, 3, 6, 12) by product line and health plan, aimed at the biggest drop.
Go-to-market
Done before: a two-step lead qualification flow at Ideal Protein that grew qualified leads 40 percent; took a direct-to-consumer brand from zero to profitability and more than $2M in revenue in 24 months. Where I'd start: first orders by health plan and referral source, read against month-3 reorders, so acquisition is judged on patients who stay.
End-to-end patient experience
Done before: owned the roadmap and customer-experience rebuild at Ideal Protein (patients, clinicians, and 3,500+ clinics, under HIPAA), with time-to-interaction as the conversion KPI; booking success rose 63 percent for patients within five miles of a clinic. Where I'd start: instrument order, visit, authorization, delivery, and refill as one journey, so the worst hand-off shows up in one view.
AI-powered support
Done before: built routing in my own AI product between fixed rules, the model, and a human reviewer. An 80-year-old and their adult child should not get the same journey, and for many patients SMS or a voice call beats an app. Where I'd start: a map of which moments AI runs (refill reminders, delivery status), which it assists (coverage questions), and which go to a person (clinical concerns, a caregiver in distress).
LLM evaluation and quality
Done before: rubrics, regression gates, and failure diagnosis on my own AI product (text, one operator, not patient traffic). Where I'd start: pull a few hundred real refill SMS and voice threads, label accuracy, tone for older patients, and handoff misses, and publish that baseline before changing anything.
Experiments and metrics
Done before: split-tested calls to action at Ideal Protein (clinic-finder leads and clinician form fills up 30 percent); built the KPI and efficacy scorecards that decided which of three AI product lines at Twill Health moved forward. Where I'd start: a ranked backlog of reorder experiments, each with a metric and a kill rule, read against seasonality and payer mix before anyone calls a win.
Honest gaps
  • No production Voice AI for patients yet. My eval work is on text. First move: the labeled baseline above, from real Conduit conversations.
  • No DME or Medicare supply background. First two weeks: sit with intake, authorization, and reorder operations, listen to reorder calls, and map where refills lapse before touching the roadmap.
Questions I would bring to Rocky
  • From the outside, my guess is that LTV on the consumables (incontinence, CGM supplies) is set at the reorder confirmation and the doctor's-order and authorization renewal, not the first order. Is that right, and how does it differ by product line?
  • Where do SMS and Voice AI run end to end today, and how is quality measured now?
  • How does this role work with the other product hires and with Clinical Operations?
