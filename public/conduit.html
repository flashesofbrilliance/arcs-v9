A product builder who owns the whole loop, pointed at the problem at the center of this role. Two decades in regulated, high-trust domains, including HIPAA digital health and a direct-to-consumer brand I took from zero to profit. I reach for AI before headcount, and I design for the patient in front of the screen, including the one for whom a screen is the wrong channel.

Conduit, as I read it

Conduit gets Medicare and Medicaid patients their insurance-covered supplies (incontinence, mobility, home safety, and diabetes monitoring) with a telehealth visit, automated insurance authorization, and home delivery in about 7 days instead of 50+. More than 50,000 patients served, a payer network of 100+ health plans covering nearly 90 million lives, and $22M raised including a $17M Series A led by Drive Capital. The retention problem is a refill problem: most of these products are consumed monthly, so LTV is decided by whether the second, sixth, and twelfth delivery happen, and whether the prescription and authorization renew before they lapse. That is where I would point the SMS and Voice AI first.

What you'll own, one to one
Retention and LTV
Done before: lifecycle segmentation that grew net-new revenue 20 percent (CTF); a D2C brand to profitability in 24 months. At Conduit: a refill-continuity cohort view (month 1, 3, 6, 12 by product line and payer) and a roadmap aimed at the biggest drop.
End-to-end patient experience
Done before: owned roadmap and CX overhaul for a medically supervised program serving consumers, clinicians, and providers. At Conduit: treat order, telehealth visit, authorization, delivery, and refill as one journey with one set of failure points, not separate screens.
AI-powered support
Done before: routing between fixed rules, model reasoning, and a human reviewer on ARCS. At Conduit: an explicit map of which patient moments AI runs (refill reminders, delivery status), which it assists (coverage questions), and which go to a person (clinical concerns, a caregiver in distress).
LLM evaluation and quality
Done before: rubrics, regression gates, and failure diagnosis on my own AI product. At Conduit: an eval set drawn from real SMS and voice conversations, scored for accuracy, tone for older patients, and correct handoff, run before every change ships.
Experiment velocity
Done before: hypothesis tests that validated two AI product lines and stopped a third before engineering time (Twill). At Conduit: a ranked backlog of refill and reactivation experiments, each with a stated metric and a kill rule.
Metrics that prove it
Done before: calibration withheld per cohort until the sample can carry it. At Conduit: two scorecards, retention and AI quality, defined up front so a lift is real and not a seasonal or payer-mix artifact.
Four reasons it fits
  • LLM evaluation and quality is my recent work. On ARCS I built the evaluation layer: rubrics, gates that stop a regression before it ships, diagnosis of failed runs, and routing between fixed rules, model reasoning, and a human reviewer. The AI structures and scores; it never issues the final verdict.
  • Retention and lifecycle, measured. Grew net-new revenue 20 percent at the Children's Tumor Foundation through lifecycle segmentation; ran a before, during, and after-event fan lifecycle loop at the WTA; built a direct-to-consumer brand to more than $2M in 24 months.
  • Regulated health with many kinds of customers. Head of Product Innovation for a $350M ARR, PE-backed medically supervised diet program in 64 countries, serving consumers, clinicians, and provider partners under HIPAA. AI-enabled digital therapeutics at Twill Health.
  • Designing for older patients. An 80-year-old and their adult child should not get the same journey, and for many patients SMS or a voice call beats an app. That is exactly why owning SMS and Voice AI quality is the job.
