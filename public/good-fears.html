Nineteen fears, one question

A founder shared nineteen things about AI that keep them up at night. The list spread because every item is easy to argue with. We did not argue. We asked each item one question: what would prove this wrong, and by when? We did not judge whether any fear is right. Guess first, then flip the card to see our read.

How we read each fear
  • Checkable: as written, some observation could count against it.
  • One number short: checkable once it names a metric or a date. The card shows the sharpened version.
  • Fog: the load-bearing word has nothing observable behind it, or a may or might means no outcome can count against it.
  • Value: a statement of what is good, not a claim about the world. Receipts do not apply to preferences.

Our reasoning is on every card, and it is contestable. That is the point: a read you can argue with line by line.

Bring your own position
