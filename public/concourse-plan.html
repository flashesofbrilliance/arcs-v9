0 · Spec: what this plan rests on

Inputs, assumptions and the tests that would prove me wrong, stated before the plan so you can argue with them first.

Problem
Residential permit decisions take 45 business days; target 10, measured from complete application to decision, on the county's own records.
Non-goals
Commercial permits, inspections scheduling, replacing the county's payment processor, a public chatbot. Each is a phase-two candidate, not a day-one promise.
What I know
From two conversations, not from inside: pods are one FDE and one FDPM on roughly 90 days; each person carries several deployments; operations clears access before kickoff; a shared integrations library exists; TypeScript, AWS and Postgres are the defaults; FDEs platformize during hypercare.
Constraints
Contract-mandated vendors stay. Records retention set by county counsel. Accessibility is a launch criterion. Access by named role only.

Dependencies
Written data access (IT security), the signed measure (director), the records policy (counsel), sandbox credentials for each vendor system (operations).
Blind spots
Informal workarounds the clerks never wrote down; seasonal volume; whether the county counts a decision the way the measure does.
Headwinds
Procurement or security review that runs on its own calendar; a sponsor change mid-deployment; my own lack of large-scale on-call, covered by the team's runbook and alerting norms.

1 · 30, 60, 90 days

Three phases, each ending at a gate someone on the county side signs.

  1. Days 1 to 30Discover. Map today's process with the clerks. Data access in writing. Gate: the director agrees on the one measure of success. Stop rule: no access by day 20, escalate that week.
  2. Days 31 to 60Build. Configure shared modules and integrations to the county's rules. Weekly demo to real users. Gate: a clerk completes a real permit end to end.
  3. Days 61 to 90Hand off. Training, runbook, alerts, who to call. Gate: county staff run it two weeks without us. Then hypercare, and anything one-off goes back into the shared library.

3 · Pre-mortem: it is day 90 and it failed

Four likely causes, each followed three steps downstream, with the cheapest early test.

Data access arrives late
First orderBuilt on sample dataSecond orderBreaks on real records at go-liveThird orderThe county stops trusting the next project

De-risk: written access is the phase-one gate; test one real record by day 20. Owner: FDPM, with operations before kickoff.

The success measure was never agreed
First orderEach stakeholder grades a different outcomeSecond orderScope creeps to please all of themThird orderLaunch slips and nobody can call it done

De-risk: one measure, signed in phase one, repeated in every weekly line. Owner: FDPM, FDE drafts it.

A one-off integration breaks after launch
First orderPayments or records stop syncingSecond orderClerks fall back to paperThird orderEvery later deployment inherits the same fragile piece

De-risk: build it to the shared library's boundary, add an alert and a runbook line before go-live. Owner: FDE.

Staff never adopt it
First orderClerks work around the systemSecond orderData in it goes staleThird orderThe 10-day target is measured on bad data

De-risk: weekly demos with the clerks who will use it, and the phase-two gate is a clerk finishing a real permit. Owner: FDE and FDPM.

4 · Critical path

The earliest step in our court is written data access; everything after it waits on it.

  1. Day 0 to 20Written data access, plus the signed measure. If either misses, the worst case starts here.
  2. Day 20 to 45Integrations: reuse from the library first; anything new is built to be reused.
  3. Day 45 to 60Clerk completes a real permit. Accessibility checked before each demo.
  4. Day 60 to 90County runs it alone for two weeks. Then hypercare, and the learnings go back into the platform.

5 · Guardrails agreed at kickoff

Written with the client on day one, so scope and pace are decided by rules, not by whoever asks last.

RuleWhat it covers
AlwaysEvery new request is written down with its cost in days. The signed measure and the 90-day date only move by the director's written decision. Every change is reviewed and logged.
SometimesA small request (under a day, inside the measure) goes in the current phase. Anything bigger trades against something already planned, or waits for phase two.
NeverBuild on data without written access. Add scope without naming what it replaces. Let the weekly line go out without the budget and days remaining.

Check-in pace changes on triggers, not on mood.

Speed up to twice weekly
If days used run more than 15 percent ahead of plan, a gate misses its date, or a new request would move the measure. Stays fast until two check-ins in a row are on plan.
Slow to every other week
In hypercare, when alerts are quiet and the measure holds for two weeks. Any new incident returns it to weekly.
Stop and decide
If committed days or budget would be exceeded before the next gate, work pauses on that item until the director picks: cut scope, add time, or fund phase two.
