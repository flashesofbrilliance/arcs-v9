Notes from the Trenches // Ancestors, unlabeled

The Instinct Before It Had a Name

Two pieces, six years apart, no shared vocabulary, and the same cognitive reflex both times.

Two LinkedIn posts, 2019 and 2025 · read as a matched pair, neither edited to agree with the other

I · No shared vocabulary

Six years sit between the two posts, and nothing carries over on the surface. The first was written in Los Altos in February 2019, about a Disney process for imagining new products. The second was written in Fairfield County in September 2025, about the state of my own laptop. Neither one says NCI. Neither one says DEWY, or Y gate, or receipt. The framework that would eventually give those words their meaning did not exist yet when the first post went up, and had not been applied to either subject when the second one did. And yet, read side by side, they are running the identical move: they take something that looks like undifferentiated mess and read it as a compressed signal about the system producing it.

II · 2019, the foam before the wave

The 2019 post is called Imagineering, and it opens on a moment of pure indeterminacy. "First nothing. An idea, no light. Before any substance or anything real." Then a flicker, and the claim that this flicker, not the finished product, is where the interesting information lives: "this flickering is the foam cap on a wave of technology that's just about to break. It's the surface tension on top of the ocean, before the swell you can see."

That is not a metaphor about excitement. It is a claim about where the signal is. The foam is not noise sitting on top of a wave that will tell you what it is anyway once it arrives. The foam is the earliest legible form the wave takes, and reading it correctly, before the swell is visible, is the entire value of the exercise. The post's other move is the same shape: the difference between a successful product journey and an unsuccessful one is "the distinction between the first few steps you could go, and those that you should go," and Imagineering, in Disney's own use of the word, is what a team does when it decides "to create a new universe with new rules of gravity" because the inherited rules stopped making sense of what they were looking at. The post closes on two questions and four hashtags: agile, design thinking, Etienne Jules Marey, playtime. No system underneath any of that has a name yet. But the instinct producing the post already has a target: read the diffuse, pre-formal state for what it is telling you about the shape of the thing arriving.

III · 2025, the desktop as mirror

The 2025 post opens on a literal desktop, not a metaphorical one. "After years in the marketing trenches, my laptop desktop looks like a digital episode of Hoarders. Files scattered everywhere, zero organization, pure chaos." The post's own turn, its plot twist, is to refuse the obvious reading of that mess as merely a personal failing to be cleaned up, and to read it instead as evidence about a system much larger than one laptop: "This visual nightmare is the perfect metaphor for online advertising in 2024."

Everything that follows is the same operation run against the ad-tech market: clickbait, algorithms optimizing for page views instead of outcomes, a push model built for a world where everyone wants the same thing at the same time, advertisers trapped paying fees that eat the working media, a programmatic system the post calls a prison. None of that is presented as a list of unrelated complaints. It is presented as symptoms of one underlying failure, which the post states directly at the end: "The desktop chaos isn't just a personal problem, it's a system failure." The literal mess on the screen and the systemic mess in the market are offered as the same object, read at two different scales.

The same move, two vocabularies apart

2019 surfacea flicker before any idea has substance: "the foam cap on a wave... before the swell you can see." 2019 systema coming shift in what a product could be, read off that flicker before it had form. 2025 surfacea cluttered laptop desktop: "files scattered everywhere, zero organization, pure chaos." 2025 systema broken attention economy, read off that clutter as "the perfect metaphor." Shared movetreat the visible mess as a compressed signal about the system underneath it, not as noise to clear before the real analysis starts.

Neither post uses the word signal. Both posts are doing signal-reading.

IV · What the framework would later call it

Name the move and it becomes recognizable as something this publication now has vocabulary for: reading a noisy surface for the coherent structure underneath, rather than discarding the noise or waiting for a cleaner signal to arrive. That is a habit of mind, not a technique that gets taught. It showed up in 2019 pointed at a corporate process with a Disney trademark on it, and it showed up again in 2025 pointed at a pile of files on a personal laptop, and it showed up in both cases before there was a name for it, a grade for it, or a product built around it.

The mess is the message. What the instinct was reaching for both times was the same claim: the chaos in front of you is a compressed signal about the system underneath it.

This is the useful test for whether a capability is native or learned. A learned technique shows up after you are taught it, applied to the kinds of problems the teaching covered. A native instinct shows up before the technique exists, applied to whatever happens to be in front of you at the time, a Disney essay one decade and a messy desktop the next, with no throughline connecting the two except the person doing the reading. The framework did not install this instinct. It found it already running, in two pieces of writing that predate the framework's own name by years, and gave it a name so it could be pointed at other things on purpose.

V · Why the ancestor is the proof

The temptation, looking back, is to read the 2019 post as an early sketch of the framework and the 2025 post as its more mature application, a straight line from naive to expert. That is not quite what the record shows. There is no ARCS reference in either piece, no borrowed vocabulary, no attempt in the 2025 post to sound like a system that did not exist yet. What the record shows instead is two independent instances of the same reflex, separated by six years, a different subject, a different register (one earnest and forward-looking, the other self-deprecating and quick with a joke), and no citation of the other. That independence is the whole of the evidence. A technique you were taught would carry some trace of the teaching. A reflex that shows up unprompted, twice, in unrelated material, was there before either post was written.

That is why the ancestors matter more than a clean origin story would. A framework built to formalize a technique risks formalizing something borrowed. A framework built to name an instinct that was already firing, unprompted, at a Disney process and at a laptop desktop, gets to make a narrower and more defensible claim: not that it taught this way of seeing, but that it found a name for something that was native all along, and made it legible enough to hand to someone else.
