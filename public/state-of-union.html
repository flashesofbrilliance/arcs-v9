Evals, falsifiability & where to get it validated

The papers make sharp, numeric claims. That's a gift: each is falsifiable. Below, every headline claim gets a metric, a pre-registered test design, and an explicit kill criterion — the result that would refute it. This is the same evidence discipline the product sells; publishing it turns marketing numbers into defensible science and de-risks both the patents and the raise.

Falsifiability matrix — claim → test → kill criterion

Claim (source)Headline metricPre-registered testKill criterion
Abductive accuracy scales with receiptsYP §2.4 / §3.2 68% → 87% CI-hit Time-ordered backtest on held-out receipts at DB sizes 1k/10k/100k; predicted CIs vs actuals. CI-hit rate flat or <80% at 100k, or no monotonic gain with N
Calibration is honest, not false precisionYP §2.4 χ² p < 0.01 Reliability diagram + Brier score; bin predicted-confidence vs observed frequency. Reliability curve off the diagonal; Brier no better than climatology
Beats human expertsYP §2.4 76% of cases Blinded head-to-head: analysts vs engine on identical unseen briefs; pre-set win metric. Win rate ≤ 50% (coin-flip) under blinding
2nd-derivative predicts viral cascades earlyWP §2.1 78% @ 24h lead Labeled backtest on 10k historical events; precision/recall + lead-time distribution vs velocity-only baseline. No lift over first-derivative baseline; lead time ≤ 0
Phase-cancellation lifts multi-campaign ROASWP §2.1 +35% ROAS Randomized A/B: optimized spacing vs naïve calendar across matched campaign pairs. CI on ROAS delta crosses zero
Rhetorical resonance beats baseline contentWP §2.2 +65% engagement Pre-registered RCT; resonance-optimized vs human-copy, single primary endpoint, multiplicity-corrected. Effect vanishes after correction / regresses on replication
Network effect: accuracy A(N) is logarithmicYP §6.2 λ ≈ 1.2e-5 Fit A(N) across real cohort sizes; out-of-sample forecast of a held-out cohort's accuracy. Fit fails to predict held-out cohort within stated CI
Make the evals credible before you publish Pre-register every design on OSF or AsPredicted (hypothesis, primary endpoint, stopping rule, kill criterion) before looking at outcomes. Report a single primary endpoint per claim with multiplicity correction; release the backtest harness + a de-identified receipt sample so results replicate. Pre-registration is also the cleanest antidote to the "stated vs revealed preference" critique the Yellow Paper itself raises.
