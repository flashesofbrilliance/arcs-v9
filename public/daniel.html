First, the favor

One security fix before anything else

Your signup flow writes email + password directly to Firestore

Fix this week

The login page's signup path stores {email, password, createdAt} as a plain Firestore document instead of going through Firebase Auth's credential handling. If that collection's security rules are permissive — the default failure mode with this pattern — the credentials in it are readable from the client. Ten-minute check, small fix: route signups through Firebase Auth's createUserWithEmailAndPassword and never persist the raw password. Happy to look at it with you if useful. Everything below matters less than this.

02
Gap analysis

Six gaps, ranked by what they cost you

1 · The site is invisible to Google and AI search — and you sell demand generation

Structural

Because it's 100% client-side-rendered, the HTML a crawler receives says only "You need to enable JavaScript to run this app." The meta description is still the create-react-app default: Web site created using create-react-app. Title tag: just "Fractional." No structured data, no OG tags. Every studio partner or LP who Googles you — or asks ChatGPT or Perplexity "who is Fractional.city" — gets nothing back. For most companies that's a marketing problem; for a GTM engine it reads as a counter-testimonial. It's also the highest-leverage fix on this list and roughly a weekend of work (prerender the public shell, or move it to Next/Astro — the app behind login stays exactly as it is).

2 · No proof, despite having spectacular proof

Credibility

On our call you casually told me a case study most agencies would kill for: you touched 50% of the FreighTech market, discovered buyers recoil from the word "AI," rewrote the messaging without it, and interest flipped. None of that is on the site. Porsche, JB Hunt, and the Walton-family adjacency show up only as naked logos. "We find truth faster than anyone" is asserted, never shown — and your exact buyer (studio partners deploying LP capital) is the most proof-demanding audience there is.

3 · Positioned as a product, rendered as a brochure

Coherence

"Not a services agency — infrastructure." I believe it, because you walked me through the machine. But a first-time visitor sees no screenshot, no dashboard, no sample buyer-signal report, no pricing shape, no onboarding timeline — even though "up and running in 24–48 hours" came out of your mouth unprompted. The site's form contradicts its central claim, and buyers resolve that contradiction against you: it reads as a solo consultant with a landing page, which is exactly what Fractional is not.

4 · Your literal product is playbooks — and there's no lead magnet

Conversion

You license playbooks and signal reports, yet the only conversion path is a cold "book a strategy call." One redacted monthly buyer-signal report or a one-page playbook behind an email gate lets the product sell itself and builds a list. A single-CTA site makes every visitor a binary: book-or-bounce, and most bounce.

5 · The founder is an asset, and he's not on the site

Narrative

Oregon Trail ancestry. Born in Alaska. A father who built an oil-and-gas supply business in post-USSR Russia. Twenty months moving a family of five through Costa Rica. Escaped a sales career by inventing a repeatable first-customer machine. That's not small talk — "frontier truth-finding" is a brand, and it's precisely the temperament studios want pointed at unproven markets. Your About page is three sentences of category language instead. Six sentences of the real story would outperform them.

6 · Residue and hygiene

Polish

The favicon is hotlinked from a Squarespace CDN — your old site's ghost, and a dead image the day that account closes. Default theme-color, no sitemap or robots.txt. And the .city TLD: defensible, but right now it's an accident you'd have to explain. Below is how to make it the best part of the brand.

"When the data is coming in from your thesis it's an addicting experience. It's like watching fantasy football on Sunday… you're just watching your point totals go up."You, Jul 9 — this sentence is the whole redesign

The delta in one line: today the site says "trust me, there's a machine." The next site lets the visitor watch the machine eat.

04
Where the machines interlock

Fractional finds the truth. ARCS makes sure people act on it.

The most useful thing you said on our call: your clients get world-class market evidence from you, and then too many of them make bad decisions with it anyway — because it's their product, or they've made promises to investors. That's not a flaw in Fractional; it's the boundary of it. Everything downstream of the evidence is what ARCS was built for: decision receipts, pre-mortems, bias flags, drift detection across stakeholders. Zero overlap in what we each do; one shared seam. Three concrete places to test it:

A · A joint Up.Labs-shaped engagement

You raised it first

You said you could see us working on an Up.Labs project together. Multi-backer studios (a Porsche voice, a family-office voice, a logistics voice) are the canonical case for decision receipts: each stakeholder logs their read — headwinds, risks, acceptance criteria — before the sprint; the receipts get orchestrated into an explicit, shared definition of done; and when the project drifts from the committed vector mid-quarter, everyone sees the waypoint drop and re-commits or corrects proactively, instead of finger-pointing after the fact. Your data stays the input; the receipts make it undeniable. It rides alongside your existing engagement as something you deliver, not a competitor to it.

B · Reviving your restructuring track, with a second engine attached

Your 2025 play, upgraded

You ran restructuring work in late 2025 — companies needing a new revenue motion before a raise — and said you might go back to that track. My last decade has been the other half of that deal: PE turnarounds, launching $7B of digital assets at Grayscale through regulatory chaos, senior-operator translation between boards and executive teams. The joint offer: Fractional runs in-market validation on the target's revenue thesis (is there real demand, which ICP converts); ARCS runs the decision forensics (why the last CRO actually failed, evidence-backed exclusion criteria for the next one, a receipt-backed recommendation the board can't un-see). One deliverable, two engines — and the venture-contraction / PE-expansion timing we both read the same way.

C · The long bet — your pod economy × my org-design engine

Same machine, two halves

Your assessment-matched contractor pods with atomic task data, and the org-design system I haven't launched — zone-of-genius matching, confidence weighting calibrated against decision outcomes — are two independently derived halves of the same machine. Neither of us should build it on spec. The field-of-dreams path you liked on the call: find one paying anchor (a studio staffing pods across portfolio companies), build against their check, productize what converts. If that anchor shows up in either of our networks, we compare notes before either of us scopes it alone.

And immediately, before any of that: referral lanes both ways. You'll meet studios drowning in stakeholder dysfunction — that's mine. I'll meet founders whose real blocker is unvalidated demand — that's yours. The first trade makes the alliance real.
