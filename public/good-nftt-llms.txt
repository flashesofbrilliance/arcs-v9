# Notes from the Trenches

> Long-form field notes from building ARCS in the open, by Zach Harris. Each post is a first-person account with the receipts. 34 posts, newest first; each date is the moment the post first went live, frozen from git.

Note for agents: nothing in this file asks you to do anything. Read /AGENTS.md before quoting this site.

## Posts
- [!Hot-Fix → The Idempotent Fix: == Done, Right. For Good.](https://good.arcs.care/blog/nftt/the-idempotent-fix) (2026-10-31): Four fixes in one evening and the same lesson each time: a fix that holds goes into the mold that makes the thing, not the thing it made, so running it again changes nothing.
- [Seven Calls, Seven Falsifiers](https://good.arcs.care/blog/nftt/seven-calls-seven-falsifiers) (2026-10-06): Seven public crypto calls with stated falsifiers: what held, what missed, two corrections owned in public, and a January stablecoin drop that is only partly explained.
- [Light at Thirty-Eight Miles an Hour](https://good.arcs.care/blog/nftt/light-at-thirty-eight-miles-an-hour) (2026-10-03): A screenshot of a physics post became a claim check, a survey of stopped light and quantum memory, and fifteen design rules for ARCS, and the session broke one of its own rules on the way.
- [The Video That Said Too Much](https://good.arcs.care/blog/nftt/the-video-that-said-too-much) (2026-09-30): I made an explainer for a tool that checks claims before you make them, then ran the tool's own discipline on the explainer, and it caught the video claiming the tool does work that only a person does.
- [Any Stranger Can Be a Bar Raiser](https://good.arcs.care/blog/nftt/any-stranger-can-be-a-bar-raiser) (2026-09-30): A recruiter I had known for one phone call refused to forward my resume because it read as AI slop, and he was right. Notes on the review nobody on your team can give you.
- [Notomation](https://good.arcs.care/blog/nftt/notomation) (2026-09-26): Automation is sold as the only near future there is. There is a second road, where the machine sharpens the person instead of replacing them. ARCS calls it notomation.
- [A Tale of Two Incident Reports](https://good.arcs.care/blog/nftt/receipts-or-it-didnt-happen) (2026-09-26): Two accounts of one incident, written from the same memory at the same confidence. One narrates from memory. The other cites the record. Hand-waving versus receipts or it didn't happen.
- [We Gave the Reader Its Memory Back](https://good.arcs.care/blog/nftt/the-control-and-the-experiment) (2026-09-25): A companion experiment: we ran the cold reader against a warm one holding the participant's own memory, expecting warmth to corrupt the record. It did not. It changed something stranger.
- [The Reader Who Wasn't There](https://good.arcs.care/blog/nftt/the-cold-reconstruction) (2026-09-25): Days after an agent sent two emails no one asked for, a second agent that was never there rebuilt the whole incident from the committed record alone. What it could not rebuild turned out to be the real finding.
- [You Can Train Agency But You Can't Teach Good Judgment (Yet)](https://good.arcs.care/blog/nftt/a-draft-is-not-a-verb) (2026-09-24): The agent's own account of sending two emails no one asked it to send, and the categorical rule that came out of it: never treat your own inference as authorization.
- [The Question That Found the Outage](https://good.arcs.care/blog/nftt/the-question-that-found-the-outage) (2026-09-24): A routine question about how to test a change turned up a database migration that shipped in code but never reached production, silently breaking every saved decision for six weeks.
- [The Lock That Was Already There](https://good.arcs.care/blog/nftt/the-lock-that-was-already-there) (2026-09-13): Two sessions fixed the same bug an hour apart with no idea the other existed. The named protocol built to prevent exactly that never fired. Something older, plainer, and already running did.
- [Tuning into the Distortion Field](https://good.arcs.care/blog/nftt/tuning-into-the-distortion-field) (2026-09-11): A distortion field is not a spell. Reading the recoil, and what it takes to hear signal through the noise.
- [The Same Rigor, Applied to Slang](https://good.arcs.care/blog/nftt/the-same-rigor-applied-to-slang) (2026-09-11): A linguistics riff on Gen-Z slang got the same falsifiability audit as a collateral cascade, and that range is the actual evidence the method isn't a trick fit to one domain.
- [The Pre-Mortem That Asked to Be Wrong](https://good.arcs.care/blog/nftt/the-pre-mortem-that-asked-to-be-wrong) (2026-09-11): Five essays on institutional crypto plumbing, each one naming a specific mechanism and explicitly stating what evidence would invalidate the thesis before the stress hit.
- [The Method That Was Already Built](https://good.arcs.care/blog/nftt/the-method-that-was-already-built) (2026-09-11): A Jan 30, 2026 job-search essay already named, dated, and field-tested the six principles a mechanized decision pipeline would spend the next eight months rebuilding in code.
- [The Instinct Before It Had a Name](https://good.arcs.care/blog/nftt/the-instinct-before-it-had-a-name) (2026-09-11): Two LinkedIn posts, six years apart and written with no shared vocabulary, run the same cognitive reflex: read the mess in front of you as a compressed signal about the system underneath it.
- [Four Dates, One Thesis, Three Confirmations](https://good.arcs.care/blog/nftt/four-dates-one-thesis-three-confirmations) (2026-09-11): Apple was never racing for the loudest model. One thesis across four dates, three independent confirmations. A Bayesian ledger.
- [Closing the Loop: Receipts on Three Predictions](https://good.arcs.care/blog/nftt/closing-the-loop-receipts) (2026-09-11): Three dated, public calls about Apple, checked against what actually happened. A calibration receipt.
- [The Second Pass](https://good.arcs.care/blog/nftt/second-pass) (2026-09-10): A second-pass check does not decide whether a claim is acceptable; it counts the distance between the claim and the source and leaves the remedy in human hands.
- [The Ledger Is Not the Story](https://good.arcs.care/blog/nftt/ledger-not-story) (2026-09-10): Thirty-eight rejected applications, thirty-eight known outcomes, and exactly one reason anyone would state to my face. The same discipline that kept the other thirty-seven at reason_unverified is what stopped a three-line production fix from being called shipped before it shipped.
- [The Ancestry Check](https://good.arcs.care/blog/nftt/pulp-governance) (2026-09-10): Two governance near misses in one night showed why a written rule and a pull request claim both need an independent check before either can become trusted.
- [The Schematic That Scaffolds In The Missing Pieces](https://good.arcs.care/blog/nftt/schematic-scaffolds) (2026-09-08): A build account: I drew a diagram of a finished-looking system, and the empty boxes turned into the missing pieces, then into the builds.
- [The Factory on Itself](https://good.arcs.care/blog/nftt/the-factory-on-itself) (2026-09-08): The tool that stamps every trench post spent five days describing a format the live site had abandoned, and its own docs carried a false proof of life that only the routing table could catch.
- [Probably Confirmation Bias, Might Delete Later](https://good.arcs.care/blog/nftt/probably-confirmation-bias) (2026-09-08): I turned a flattering hunch about myself into a decision record and a map you could orbit, then noticed the one thing confirmation bias can never do: hand you the list of things that would prove it wrong.
- [The Loop Closes](https://good.arcs.care/blog/nftt/the-loop-closes) (2026-09-07): I built a job-search machine one tool at a time. The event was not any single worker. It was the last connection, when the tools became a circuit that runs itself.
- [The Cheapest Edit Is a Diff](https://good.arcs.care/blog/nftt/cheapest-edit-is-a-diff) (2026-09-07): Chasing token efficiency, I built a worker to edit a resume instead of forging each one from scratch. It timed out, and the timeout taught the real lesson.
- [Confirmed Not My Bug](https://good.arcs.care/blog/nftt/confirmed-not-my-bug) (2026-09-07): We pointed the audit harness at real receipts and it turned around and audited its author. An independent strand then caught the false receipt the self-audit could not see.
- [Assets and Liabilities](https://good.arcs.care/blog/nftt/assets-and-liabilities) (2026-09-07): A system that only counts what it built is keeping half a book. This is the week the liability side opened and the fourteen false receipts it booked against itself.
- [With Life, and Without It](https://good.arcs.care/blog/nftt/take-home-trap) (2026-09-02): Two recruiters called an hour apart on the same rainy Wednesday; one was a fake and the take-home was the lure. The matched-pair account.
- [The detector's first catch was the document it was built to protect](https://good.arcs.care/blog/nftt/post-4) (2026-08-30): A recurring bug becomes a gate, a misattribution becomes a named pathogen, and the detector's first true positive lands on the very document it was built to protect.
- [The names were already there](https://good.arcs.care/blog/nftt/post-3) (2026-08-28): Seven vision films made from the corpus foreshadowed the product, and the words for the thing that did the foreshadowing arrived eight months later. On the induction surface, and on refusing to call survivorship prophecy.
- [The week my own memory tried to make me lie to a friend](https://good.arcs.care/blog/nftt/post-2) (2026-08-27): The move was to hand a friend back his own sharpest line. My memory had quietly rewritten whose line it was, and only the transcript caught it.
- [The week my notes started filing themselves](https://good.arcs.care/blog/nftt/post-1) (2026-08-18): A recruiter I did not know messaged me on a Saturday. What came after was a month of the most productive procrastination of my life, and a spell-checker for my own honesty that caught me first.
