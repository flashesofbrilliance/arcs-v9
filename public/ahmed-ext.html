How to read itEach line is one belief, scored 0 to 1, left to right in call time. It rises when your words backed it, falls when they cut against it. The red dot marks the moment a belief of mine broke.

The cyan line is a prior I walked in with, that your media proposal was the thing you wanted to work on. It held flat, then dropped hard when you corrected it. That one correction carried most of the update. The 0.7 was filed in writing before we spoke; that pre-call receipt is what makes the drop checkable instead of a story. The value is not that the model was right. It is that when I was wrong, the correction became the highest-information event in the log, not something quietly edited out.

What it does

The capabilities, at a high level

Enough to see the shape. The specific model and operators are held back, so this is what it does, not how it is tuned.

  • Each belief gets a starting estimate, an update anchored to a quoted line, and a close. Wrong ones stay in the log.
  • Reads that cannot be traced to a line get cut. The media-pitch prior above is what a surviving correction looks like: kept, dated, and scored against what you actually said.
  • More calls, more answerable questions. Each report gets graded expected versus actual, so the track record is checkable, not claimed.
Two things it surfaced

The signal under the summary

  • Context not content, one layer apart. You standardize the context AI acts on. I standardize the decision a human commits to. You said it yourself on the call: what I do for a person is what you do for the data.
  • The open loop, statefulness. Most orgs never close the gap between a decision made downstream and the context it leaves stale upstream. Both our systems exist because that loop is open.
