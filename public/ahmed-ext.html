The short version

What this is, in your language

You model raw data into one activity table, then query it for any answer. This does the same thing for a conversation. It turns a call into a time-ordered record of what each side believed, and how those beliefs moved as new information landed.

Two things get tracked per read: how likely it is true, and how settled that is. Expected versus actual, joined on time. Your temporal join, applied to a decision instead of a row.

Where our customers overlap

You put it well on the call: the customers who care about revenue and have real data problems are also the ones making expensive, hard-to-reverse decisions. That is exactly who this is for. Wealth advisors, regulated industries, anyone with a high burden of proof on a one-way call.

Your data may be the less interesting part. Your customers' need is the interesting part, and it is a strong fit.

Why being wrong is the point

The useful part is not that the engine was right. It is that when I was wrong, it caught the correction and booked it, instead of quietly editing it out. It gets sharper from being wrong.

That is the same property your one table has. Every activity you append makes it more answerable, not more brittle. This does that for decisions.

The same model, both directions

The reason this maps to how you already think is that it is the same structure, pointed at decisions instead of data.
