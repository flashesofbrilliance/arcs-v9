Behind the scenes — the engine, in your register

1. The data model

A decision is an entity. Every belief-update about it is a timestamped activity. One table, queried for any bearing. It is your activity schema with a different subject.

receipt_activity decision_id uuid -- the entity (this call) t timestamp -- segment time claim text -- "media proposal is his focus" prior float[0,1] -- credence before this segment posterior float[0,1] -- credence after conviction enum -- low | med | high (stability of the estimate) trigger text -- the statement that moved it evidence enum -- explicit | implicit | external

No foreign keys. Joins are temporal, on decision_id + t, exactly the way you join yours.

2. How a belief moves

Each claim is tracked as its own estimate over time. A new statement is evidence, and the credence updates toward it in proportion to how diagnostic the statement is. Standard Bayesian posture, applied per claim.

  • Confidence is the posterior: how likely the claim is true, on 0 to 1.
  • Conviction is the precision of that posterior: the inverse of how much a reasonable next data point would move it. You can hold high conviction that a claim is false.
  • The recoil is a reverse pass. Read the call end to start, take the final state of each claim, and attribute every shift back to the segment that caused it. It is closer to backprop over a conversation than to a summary.
  • Where the learning concentrates: a falsified prior carries high surprisal, so it moves the model the most. The ledger is built to put the most weight exactly where I was most wrong, not where I was already right.

3. Belief kinematics

Once confidence is a time series, you can differentiate it. That is where most call-analytics tools stop flat and this one does not.

  • First derivative is how fast a belief is moving inside the call. A sharp slope means a segment did real work.
  • Second derivative tells you whether it is converging toward a settled value or diverging and destabilizing. Convergent beliefs are safe to act on. Divergent ones are not yet.
  • Across a book of calls, this flags which beliefs about a prospect are stable enough to commit to, and which are still in motion, per rep and per stage.

4. The two passes and the sampling

A
Reverse listening reads end to start. It takes where the call actually landed, then flags divergence between where it seemed to be heading and where it went.
B
Deep recall reads start to end, scored against the pre-call expectation, so you get an honest diff instead of a tidy narrative.
C
Segment count scales sub-linearly with call length, so a longer call gets a finer read without over-slicing a short one. Ours ran about 25 minutes, so roughly five segments.
D
Semantic layer on top: hedging and commitment language are detected, so a shift in how something is said registers as signal, not just what is said.
E
Output is a topography that gains resolution as the log grows, not a flat transcript summary. More calls make it sharper, the same way more activities make your table more answerable.

5. A worked example

Two claims tracked across the five segments of our call. One of them is a belief of mine that collapsed, which is the honest part. The engine caught the correction instead of hiding it.

"our customers overlap"  .60 → .90 "media pitch is his focus"  .70 → .25

The cyan line is a prior I walked in with, that your media proposal was the thing you wanted to work on. It held flat, then dropped hard at segment 4 when you said it was a side project and Polyform was the main event. High surprisal, so that one segment carried most of the update, and the claim closed at low confidence with high conviction. The green line is the overlap between us, which started as a guess and rose to a high-confidence read on your own framing.

6. Why being wrong is the point

The value is not that the model was right. It is that a falsified prior is the highest-information event in the whole log, and the system is built to capture it rather than smooth it over. It gets sharper from being wrong.

That is the same property your one table has. Every activity you append makes it more answerable, not more brittle. This does that for decisions, and it is why the honest diff is worth more than a flattering summary.
