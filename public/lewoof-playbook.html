The debate

Three voices argue about whether you can sell premium dog shampoo while people tighten their spending. Listen to all three, then decide.

The Believer

  • Pet spending grew about 3.7% in 2025, through inflation and uncertainty (APPA).
  • Owners cut accessories before care, and a shampoo is care that people buy again.
  • A narrow premium brand needs thousands of buyers, not millions.
  • Hospitality is a tailwind: pet-friendly travel is growing, and hotels need amenities that say care.
  • Your record is in sales and partnerships, which is the skill this plan needs.

The Skeptic

  • A $48 bottle competes with a $9 bottle that works. When people squeeze, "organic, made in America" is a story some stop paying for.
  • The money that holds up best is vet care and professional services. Shampoo is closer to the edge.
  • Online, winning a customer costs about $68 and a first order earns about $43, so ads depend on reorders.
  • Early partner agreements often take a few rounds. This is the one to finish.

The Inverter

  • For the Believer to be wrong, groomers and hotels would prefer what they use now and would not switch for a story.
  • For the Skeptic to be wrong, buyers would pay more for something they can see: a better coat, or a smoother guest experience.
  • So the question is not whether premium sells. It is whether 10 groomers and 10 hotel buyers take a free sample and reorder. That takes about six weeks and about 40 bottles.

The listening script: ten groomers and hotel buyers

Ten minutes each. Your goal is to learn what they do today, so ask about the past and save your story for last.

  1. Walk me through the last time you bought shampoo. What was it, where did you buy it, and what did you pay?
  2. How many dogs a week, and about how much product does a groom use?
  3. What do you wish your current shampoo did better?
  4. When did you last switch products, and what made you switch?
  5. Do clients ever ask what you use? Do any ask for natural or organic?
  6. Who decides what you buy, and what would you need to see before trying something new: a sample, a price, a bigger size?
  7. What cost per groom would be too much for a better product?
  8. If I send two bottles for you to use on five dogs, will you tell me honestly how it went in two weeks?

Hotel or spa: use the separate hotel call script, or replace questions 1 and 2 with "What do you spend per room on amenities, and who signs off on a change?" and question 7 with "What would make you drop a supplier?"

Scoring: kind words do not count. Commitments count: a sample accepted, a call booked, an intro to another buyer, a paid pilot or a deposit. Write down what they use now, what they pay and what would make them switch.
