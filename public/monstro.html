Why this beats a traditional marketer, for this role

A traditional marketing hire

Deep in brand, a content calendar, agencies, a demand-gen playbook. Uses AI as a writing assistant bolted onto an existing process. The JD is unusually blunt that it does not want this: "this is not a traditional marketing role."

Zach, marketing as a system

Approaches marketing the way an engineer approaches a system: builds the machine, not the campaign. Uses AI as infrastructure, not a writing assistant. The JD's own "who we're looking for" reads as my description, almost line for line: builds end to end, learns new tools untrained, measures instead of guessing.

The part that compounds

I do not just produce content, I build the machine that produces it, then rebuilds itself as better tools arrive. The JD's core loop, "why is this manual? can an agent do it? what needs human judgment?", is the exact router I already run on: mechanical work goes to the terminal, judgment stays with the human. In the first months I would set the leverage pattern the whole company compounds on, and raise the internal bar for using AI as infrastructure.

And I speak the buyer's language natively. Monstro sells governed AI to regulated financial institutions; I ran go-to-market for 10 SEC/FINRA-regulated financial products at $7B AUM. A marketing system built by someone who has actually sold inside regulated-finance governance gates lands differently than one built by someone learning that world for the first time.
