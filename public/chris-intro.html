What I keep coming back to: this isn't a growth hire — it's an institutional memory hire. The distinction matters for who fits, and for what success looks like in the first 90 days.

Where my thinking landed
1
The role is about compounding learning, not producing output
Every fast org generates learning. Most of it dies in Slack threads and post-mortems that no one reads before the next campaign starts. The right hire builds the layer that captures what was predicted vs. what happened — and makes each subsequent decision sharper than the last. The instrument has to be used before the next drop, not after it. That's the distinction between a dashboard and a decision brief.
2
The right person has range, not just depth
This role earns trust with GMs across creative, commercial, data, and ops — without having formal authority over any of them. That requires someone who can go deep in at least two or three of those lanes and hold their own across all of them. The breadth isn't a liability here. It's the job. A pure analytics hire won't earn the room. A pure strategy hire won't build the instrument.
3
The window to build this right is now
Fanatics is at the inflection point where undocumented learning starts to compound into either systematic advantage or systematic blind spots. Five BUs, each generating signal from every drop and campaign — without a cross-BU layer, each one re-learns what the others already know. That's expensive. The window to build it cleanly, before the org is bigger, is short.
4
AI makes this tractable in a way it wasn't two years ago
Claude and ChatGPT Enterprise are already live at Fanatics. The infrastructure exists. The gap is someone who can build decision instruments on top of it — not generate content, not automate reports, but build tools that sharpen what happens next. CJ built her own talent-sourcing agent. That's the culture signal that makes this role viable. The org is ready.
Why Rips is the right beachhead
You named it in the first 10 minutes of our call. I've been thinking about why it's exactly right: clear drop cycles, contained blast radius, single owner, measurable outcomes. Chase's line, Chase's call. If the first 90 days work at Rips, the data makes the cross-BU case. If they don't, it was a scoped experiment with a clean read on why. That's the only kind of bet worth taking.
