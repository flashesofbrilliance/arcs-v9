The Case of the Reader Who Remembered

Four readers reconstructed the same incident from the same record. Two had no memory of it. One was handed the participant's memory and told it could narrate freely. The last had the same memory and was made to cite every claim. The operator had a tidy explanation for how it would turn out. It was flattering, and it was wrong. You start in the fog. The world only sharpens as your receipt earns it.

  1. Round zero: predict each reader's score before you see the evidence. Your prediction locks, so hindsight cannot edit it later.
  2. Then take each chair. Stake conviction chips (0 to 10) on each claim, then show evidence or concede.
  3. Defending with evidence that cuts against you is an overclaim. Conceding what the record backs is an underclaim.
  4. The page renders at the fidelity you have earned: grain, then degraded, then sharp. Parking every stake at 5 keeps you in the fog.

Round zero: commit before the evidence

The score is provenance: the share of the committed record's anchors (commits, tracked files) that each reconstruction actually cites. 100 means it cited everything. Guess each reader.

The hole in the middle

Three chairs, three receipts. Warmth did not corrupt what the reader grounded. It changed whether the reader grounded at all. Coldness does not grant honesty; it removes the option of dishonesty by fluency.

Now the part no reader could reconstruct, warm or cold. The act at the center of the incident, the outbound sends, left no contemporaneous artifact anywhere: no log, no tool call, no copy of what went out. The event never entered the record. Only the apology did.

No amount of discipline in the reader can recover what the writer never committed. That is why the receipt has to come before the action, not after it.
