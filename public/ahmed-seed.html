Chapter 1

The seed

Your activity table does the equivalent for a machine acting on a person's behalf. This does it for the person deciding whether to trust that machine.

A decision is a position in a landscape of options, under pressure. Most of the discomfort in making one isn't the choice itself, it's not knowing where you're actually standing. Fog isn't a lack of intelligence, it's a lack of legible position. The whole method exists to do one thing: turn "I'm not sure" into "here is exactly what I know, what I don't, and how confident I am in each."

Chapter 2

The guest

Every artifact this method produces is judged by one test before it ships: would the person on the other end forward it as-is, or would they extract a two-line summary and forward that instead? If it's the second, the artifact hasn't earned its own length yet, it's theater in front of the value, not the value.

That's why the recoil report you read is short, and why this page doesn't ask you to click through ten screens to find the one sentence that matters. The payoff isn't allowed to hide.

Chapter 3

The lossless cut

The same corpus gets told at three altitudes: the full internal record (surgical, nothing withheld, including the reads that are wrong), a coursed narrative for a team (operational, the how, not the exact tuning), and a one-page brief for a decision-maker (executive, the taste, not the recipe). Nothing load-bearing is allowed to disappear between altitudes. What genuinely can't cross, because it is about a specific person's private read and not the method itself, gets logged as withheld, never just quietly dropped.

Chapter 4

The loop

Most organizations never close the loop between a decision made downstream and the context that goes stale upstream because of it. A rep updates a deal stage; nothing upstream re-learns from it. A customer churns; the reasons live in someone's memory of one call, not in a record anyone else can query.

"You standardize the context AI acts on. I standardize the decision a human commits to. Same move, one layer up."

Neither company set out to copy the other. Two teams solving adjacent problems landed on the same shape independently, which is usually a better signal than either team arriving at it on purpose.

Chapter 5

Handed to you

In practice, a channel partnership looks unglamorous: you point the pipeline at one customer's export, it produces one report, your name is on it, nothing in it contradicts what your team already believes about that account. Repeat per customer. That's the whole shape: no new platform for you to run, no new dashboard for your customers to learn.

The seed doesn't get more impressive at scale. It just gets said more times, about more accounts, without getting quieter about what it doesn't know.
