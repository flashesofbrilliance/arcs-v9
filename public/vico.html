Where my work meets VICO's

A note for Lyndon, from someone who has spent the last year building in the same problem space, on top of a decade shipping regulated, government-grade systems. Introduced by Joe Schriner at Oxenham Group.

What VICO does, as I read it

The way I read VICO: you put a number on the odds that a real-world event will actually happen, political, geopolitical, and economic, by reading on the order of 150,000 public sources (news, think-tank reports, government feeds like OFAC and Treasury) and turning them into forecasts a professional can act on, updated continuously, with scenarios and monitoring.

Two things make it more than another AI demo. The forecasts are explainable and backtested, and they beat the prediction markets they sit next to, putting numbers on geopolitical calls like Iran regime-risk and Venezuela risk ahead of the crowd, and published where the users already work, on the Bloomberg Terminal at WSL VICO. Built for investors, hedge funds, insurers, corporations, and government agencies, by a team out of Google and Meta, Bridgewater and Cerberus, and US intelligence.

Where my work overlaps

1

Your exact problem, worked by hand and in public.I published a full analysis of the ZIM / Hapag-Lloyd shipping merger, a deal whose whole outcome hangs on whether the Israeli government uses a golden-share veto to block it, during Red Sea and Iran tension. It plays the situation forward across 30,000 scenarios, names the early signs that would move the odds, rates every source for reliability, and lands on one risk-adjusted answer (the full writeup). That is your problem exactly, worked the way I think.

2

Writing the call down first, then checking it.Under that one analysis sits a habit: before a call, I write down how sure I am and why, then check it against what happened. Same write-it-first, check-it-after discipline behind any backtested forecast, on a public belief ledger where I scored a twelve-part thesis before the lawsuit was filed, then re-scored it against the complaint. It keeps the calls I got wrong, scored down and left on the record, not deleted. And ZIM and this are the same move, a source-graded probability under a structural constraint, run in two different domains. That portability is the bet VICO is making.

3

A record you can trust when money is on the line.I built the investor-facing platform behind a $7 billion regulated launch (SEC / FINRA): access controls, change controls, every decision traceable to why it was made. That same show-me-why demand is what a trader or a government analyst puts on a forecast, and they need to trust it when it is wrong, not only when it is right.

4

AI you can check, with a person in the loop.I run a live AI system on Claude, end to end: it uses tools, I test it against known answers (evals), and a person reviews what it does before it counts. It shows its reasoning so a skeptic can pull it apart, and I have built the unglamorous parts too, like a layer that routes each job to the cheapest model that still passes. Shipping AI that holds up under that scrutiny is what I have spent this year doing.

Why this interests me

The honest reason: for a year I have been building the one thing VICO sells, an AI answer you can actually trust. Every fact traces to its source, sources are graded before they can move a conclusion, the screen refuses to look more certain than the evidence allows, and it keeps a receipt: what it expected, checked against what happened. Explainable and backtested is not your marketing, it is your moat, and it is the machinery I have spent the year building.

The part I would care about most is the hardest one: government customers who cannot act on a black box. That is exactly what Lyndon described, LLMs that don't exist yet, made dependable enough for the highest-stakes users.

How I'd think about the product

1

Does the calibration survive a cascade that moves its own leading indicator?Being accurate in normal conditions is the easy half. The question I would want to live in: what happens when a second-order effect moves the very indicator the forecast was leaning on, the exogenous shock nobody priced? How does the system notice its own calibration has drifted, and re-weight, and how fast?

2

How far does "explainable" actually go?Leading with explainable is the right call: being able to show your work is what makes a forecast survivable when it is wrong. The question is how far down it reaches, from 150,000 sources to a single number, how much of that path can a doubting analyst actually open up and poke at?

A few things about how I work

·

How I build: I use AI tools to move faster, not to cover for weak fundamentals. The code is mine and the commit history is public (status.arcs.care), so the pace is checkable, not just claimed.

·

Leadership, and how I hold it: I lead by staying in the code. I have led cross-functional teams across four time zones, recruited a company's first CTO, and mentor at Cornell Tech and Outlier Ventures, and I move without friction between three altitudes: hands-on building, running a small team, and being the founder's technical partner on strategy. That switching capacity, not a title, is the hands-on VP this role describes.

·

Craft: I care about how it looks and how traceable it is, because the people who rely on a tool like this have to trust what they are seeing.

How I tried to break this

·

"The calibration is a handful of public calls, not a scored track record."True. The sample is small and there is no Brier score yet. It is filed in the open so anyone can check it, and named as a gap, not dressed up as more.

·

"No VP title, and five-nines and SOC 2 are not shipped."True. The scope is there, teams led, $7 billion delivery owned, a first CTO recruited, but the title and those specific bars are a next step, and I say so first.

·

"GCP and production retrieval are real gaps."True. My cloud work is on Vercel and Supabase, and the retrieval write-path is designed but not shipped. Fast to close inside a real system, and not hidden here.

None of these is load-bearing for the work itself. They are the first month's ramp, not the job.
