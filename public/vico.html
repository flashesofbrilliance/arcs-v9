Where my work meets VICO's

A note for Lyndon, from someone who has spent the last year building in the same problem space. Introduced by Joe Schriner at Oxenham Group.

What VICO does, as I read it

The way I read VICO: you calculate probabilities that real events will occur, political, geopolitical, and economic, by mining on the order of 150,000 public sources (news, think-tank reports, government feeds like OFAC and Treasury) into decision-grade foresight: continuous forecasts, scenario simulation, and monitoring. Two things make it more than another AI-forecasting demo. It is explainable and backtested, and it outperforms the prediction markets it sits beside, calling events like the Israel strikes on Iran and the Venezuela strike ahead of Polymarket and Kalshi, published where the users already work, on the Bloomberg Terminal at WSL VICO. Built for investors, hedge funds, insurers, corporations, and government agencies by a team out of Google and Meta, Bridgewater and Cerberus, and US intelligence.

Where my work overlaps

1

Scoring the call, then checking it against reality.The core of what I build (ARCS) is a decision-scoring layer: every call carries a conviction-and-stability score before the fact and a belief-history trail, so it can be checked against what actually happened after. That is the same score-before, check-after discipline behind a calibrated, backtested forecast. To be exact about the gap: your forecasts are backtested and beat the markets they sit beside; mine are not there yet. ARCS produces conviction states, not probability forecasts, and the resolved-outcome set is still small, so there is no Brier or log score to put next to your track record. What I do have is the discipline itself, run in public: dated predictions with the falsifier written down first, then scored and corrected in the open (my calibration receipts). Turning that discipline into real, backtested numbers is the obvious next step, and exactly the muscle your team already has.

2

Provenance under regulation.I built the investor-facing platform behind a $7B, SEC/FINRA-regulated launch: RBAC, change controls, pre-deployment integrity gates, every decision traceable back to why it was made. That "show your work" requirement is the same one traders, analysts, and government users put on a forecast. They need to trust it when it is wrong, not only when it is right.

3

Human-in-the-loop decision surfaces.ARCS scaffolds judgment without overruling it: evidence-gating and structured scoring as a quality layer, never an autonomous verdict. For a product people bet real money and real decisions on, keeping the human in the loop with the reasoning exposed is the difference between a tool they trust and one they quietly turn off.

4

LLM systems that have to be right and checkable.I run a live LLM system on Claude end to end: agentic tool-use, human-in-the-loop review, and evals. Integrating, evaluating, and shipping LLM features that hold up under scrutiny is the part of the AI stack I have spent this year inside.

Why this interests me

I have spent the last year building on one conviction: the valuable thing an AI system produces is not the answer, it is the scored, checkable call behind it, the thing you can hold accountable later. Anthony's own framing, think in probabilities, prepare for multiple possible futures, be a forecaster and not a reactor, is the same instinct I have been building a product around. Most of the market is still selling a single confident answer; VICO is selling the honest version: a probability, explainable, and checked.

And it lands on the failure mode I care about most. Your work on complex systems and exogenous shocks, and Anthony's emphasis on second- and third-order effects, is the honest hard part: a model that is calibrated in-regime is the easy half; what happens to it the week a cascade moves the leading indicator is the real question. Building that inside a team with Anthony's national-security lineage and your background on exactly that problem is the rare version of this I would want to be part of, not the generic AI-startup version.

How I'd think about the product

1

Does the calibration survive a cascade?Backtested accuracy in the 70-to-80 range is strong. The question I would want to live in: how does it hold when a second-order effect moves the very leading indicator the backtest leaned on, the exogenous shock nobody priced? How does the system notice its own calibration has drifted and re-weight, and how fast? In-regime calibration is the easy half.

2

How deep does "explainable" go?Leading with explainable is the right instinct: provenance is what makes a forecast survivable when it is wrong. The question is how far down the chain it reaches, from 150,000 sources to a single probability, how much of that path does a skeptical analyst actually get to open and interrogate?
