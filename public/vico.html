Where my work meets VICO's

A note for Lyndon, from someone who has spent the last year building in the same problem space. Introduced by Joe Schriner at Oxenham Group.

What VICO does, as I read it

VICO turns the hardest questions in finance and national security into live, scored probabilities: real-time event forecasts, scenario simulation, and monitoring, put where the users already work, on the Bloomberg Terminal. What separates it from most of the AI-forecasting field is that the forecasts are measured: a published 0.15 Brier score is a real calibration claim, not a marketing one (0.25 is the score of always guessing 50 percent, so 0.15 is a genuinely good number). Built by a team out of Google and Meta, Bridgewater and Cerberus, and US intelligence, for traders, analysts, and government teams who have to act on the number, not just read it.

Where my work overlaps

1

Scoring the call, then checking it against reality.The core of what I build (ARCS) is a decision-scoring layer: every call carries a conviction-and-stability score before the fact and a belief-history trail, so it can be checked against what actually happened after. That is the same score-before, check-after discipline behind a calibrated forecast. To be exact: I don't have a Brier score to set next to your 0.15. ARCS produces conviction states, not probability forecasts, and the resolved-outcome set is still small. What I do have is the discipline, run in public: dated predictions with the falsifier written down first, then scored and corrected in the open (my calibration receipts). Putting real numbers on that is the obvious next step, and exactly what a team already living at a 0.15 Brier does well.

2

Provenance under regulation.I built the investor-facing platform behind a $7B, SEC/FINRA-regulated launch: RBAC, change controls, pre-deployment integrity gates, every decision traceable back to why it was made. That "show your work" requirement is the same one traders, analysts, and government users put on a forecast. They need to trust it when it is wrong, not only when it is right.

3

Human-in-the-loop decision surfaces.ARCS scaffolds judgment without overruling it: evidence-gating and structured scoring as a quality layer, never an autonomous verdict. For a product people bet real money and real decisions on, keeping the human in the loop with the reasoning exposed is the difference between a tool they trust and one they quietly turn off.

4

LLM systems that have to be right and checkable.I run a live LLM system on Claude end to end: agentic tool-use, human-in-the-loop review, and evals. Integrating, evaluating, and shipping LLM features that hold up under scrutiny is the part of the AI stack I have spent this year inside.

Why this interests me

I have spent the last year building on one conviction: the valuable thing an AI system produces is not the answer, it is the scored, checkable call behind it, the thing you can hold accountable later. Most of the market is still selling the answer. VICO is the first team I have seen putting that exact belief in front of traders and government at a measured calibration bar, on the Terminal.

And it lands on the failure mode I care about most. Your own work on complex systems and exogenous shocks is the honest hard part: calibration that holds all year is the easy half; what happens to it the week the world changes is the real question. Building inside a team with Anthony's national-security lineage and your background on exactly that problem is the rare version of this I would want to be part of, not the generic "AI startup" version.

How I'd think about the product

1

Is the 0.15 stationary?A 0.15 Brier is strong. The question I would want to live in: does it hold across a regime change, the exogenous shock nobody backtested? How does the system notice its own calibration has drifted and needs re-weighting, and how fast can it react? In-regime calibration is the easy half.

2

How much of the "why" reaches the user?Provenance is what makes a forecast survivable when it is wrong. The surface that shows the path to the number, not just the number, is the one a skeptical analyst keeps open. How much of the reasoning behind a probability is exposed today, and where is the line you have drawn on that?
