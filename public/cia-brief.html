The job, as I heard it

Pick the CRM

Salesforce or Microsoft Dynamics, chosen on total cost, licensing (including AI and API usage charges), openness and fit for gift, sponsorship, in-kind and event giving.

Pick the data and integration layer

Snowflake, Databricks or Fabric; MuleSoft, Boomi or Microsoft's integration tools. Governed data that teams can build point solutions on without an enterprise license for everything.

Land it before advancement hires arrive

Have the platform decision and the diligence done so new fundraising staff start on the chosen system, or help shape its build.

Leave a stronger team

Partner with the Director of Enterprise Systems and set up patterns the in-house team can run on its own.

How I would run the CRM decision

A selection like this goes wrong when the decision is argued in a meeting and remembered differently later. I write the decision down before it is made: what we looked at, what we weighed, what we gave up, and what we expect to see after go-live. Then we check the result against that record at 90 days.

Looking out and looking in

Looking out

  • How Salesforce and Dynamics price for nonprofits and education, including AI and API usage charges.
  • What peer culinary schools, colleges and hospitality groups run, and what they would change.
  • The implementation partner market for each platform: who is good, who is available, at what cost.
  • Where each vendor's roadmap is heading on AI features and data access.

Looking in

  • Systems and data: every system that holds a person or organization (student records, donor database, ticketing and reservations, point of sale, consulting contracts, email and marketing), its owner, and how data moves today.
  • Use cases by team: short user stories, such as a gift officer seeing that a donor also took a class and booked an event.
  • The current reporting loop: how numbers are pulled, checked and turned into decisions today, and where the manual work and blind spots are.
  • Team skills: what the in-house systems team can own after go-live.

Trade-offs on the table

Budget

Five-year total cost: licenses, implementation, integration, add-ons, AI and API usage, and the staff time to run it.

Timing

What has to be live for the new advancement team versus what can phase in, and how long each platform realistically takes to stand up.

Rollout resources

Partner hours, in-house hours, training load on business teams, and who carries support after launch.

Risk

Lock-in, migration data quality, adoption by staff, and what we do if a phase slips.

Then the decision

  1. Weights first. Agree criteria and weights with the business owners, using everything above, before any vendor demo.
  2. Scripted demos. Both finalists run the same CIA scenarios: a donor who is also an alumnus, a consulting client and an event buyer.
  3. Reference checks with peers in higher education and nonprofit advancement on both platforms.
  4. A written recommendation with the dissent recorded, sized to an executive read.
  5. Check the call at 90 days against the written record, and adjust.

A plausible first 90 days

Days 1 to 30: map and weigh

  • Inventory systems, data owners and the silos between the four campuses and the business lines.
  • Workshops with fundraising, education, events, consulting and retail leads.
  • Agree selection criteria and weights; issue scripted demo scenarios to both CRM finalists.
  • Target-state architecture draft, one page.

Days 31 to 60: decide the CRM (pace set with you, from wherever the selection stands)

  • Run demos, references and the five-year cost model.
  • Recommendation to you, written so it can go to the leadership team as is.
  • Start the data and integration evaluation on the same framework.
  • Work alongside the Director of Enterprise Systems so the team owns these patterns after I step back.

Days 61 to 90: set up delivery

  • Implementation partner selection and statement of work review.
  • Constituent data model and migration plan.
  • Design review checklist the in-house team runs on vendor designs.
  • Data and integration platform recommendation.

Past 90 days the work is implementation oversight and rollout, which runs well beyond a single quarter. The milestones above are built so each one stands on its own.

What I would want to ask you

A choice like this succeeds or fails on business owners more than on technology. With a leadership team that is still new, I would rather ask than guess.

  1. Where does the Salesforce and Dynamics shortlist stand today, and what has already been ruled in or out?
  2. Who do you want deciding for each area: fundraising, education and alumni, consumer classes and events, consulting and workforce training, retail, and on the finance, compliance and security side?
  3. What does bringing the Director of Enterprise Systems along look like to you?
