Looking out and looking in

Choose on CIA's real systems and users, checked against what peers and the market show.

Looking out

  • How Salesforce and Dynamics price for nonprofits and education, including AI and API usage charges.
  • What peer culinary schools, colleges and hospitality groups run, and what they would change.
  • The implementation partner market for each platform: who is good, who is available, at what cost.

Looking in

  • Systems and data: every system that holds a person or organization (student records, donor database, ticketing and reservations, point of sale, consulting contracts, email and marketing), its owner, and how data moves today.
  • Use cases by team: short user stories, such as a gift officer seeing that a donor also took a class and booked an event.
  • The current reporting loop: how numbers are pulled, checked and turned into decisions today, and where the manual work and blind spots are.
  • Team skills: what the in-house systems team can own after go-live.

Ground rules that keep the project healthy

No signature without the record; changed assumptions are shown, not hidden.
  1. No signature without the record. No contract is signed until the decision record is written and the compliance review has passed.
  2. Changed assumptions are shown, not hidden. When a belief in the plan changes, we log what changed, when and why, and keep the old version visible, so the plan's history can be read.
  3. Re-plan triggers are agreed in advance. If an indicator below crosses its line, we meet within the week to adjust, before it becomes a slip.
  4. Every owner sees their input used. Within a week of a workshop, each owner sees where their requirements landed.
  5. One page of status, every week. The same five or six indicators, in the same order, so changes stand out.
