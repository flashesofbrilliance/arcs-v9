April Dunford's five components, applied in her mandatory sequence.
1Competitive alternatives
Sean names two explicitly (DIY finance apps: Quicken/Mint/Monarch; human advisors: "James") and dismisses a third implicitly (generalist chatbots). The essay treats these as solved-and-abandoned rather than as the live alternatives a buyer is actually choosing between today.

3Value / proof — this is the crack
Three of four attributes rest on anecdote (the James story) rather than data; only the SEC registration itself is independently verifiable.
4Target customer
The essay never resolves whether the primary buyer is the mass-affluent consumer or the enterprise advisory firm; it reads as B2B2C distribution wrapped in B2C mission language.
5Market category
"Financial Superintelligence" is a high-risk category-creation move: it requires educating the market from zero and shares vocabulary with AGI discourse Wing doesn't control.
Naming verdict: "Your Money, Alive" over "Live Your Money™"
"Live Your Money" grammatically commands the user to act — which contradicts the product's real promise (the system acts so the user doesn't have to). "Your Money, Alive" inverts the grammar correctly: the money becomes the active subject, matching the "silence reassures you" ideal Sean himself describes. The invariant truth under both: Wing's actual promise is vigilance-as-a-service — "you no longer have to be the one watching."

LEGEND
▼
The plain meaning of every industry term used above.
Agentic RIA
A registered investment adviser whose advice is generated and delivered by AI systems that act continuously — not a chatbot that answers questions when asked.
SEC-registered investment adviser
A firm legally licensed and supervised by the U.S. SEC to give financial advice as a fiduciary — legally required to act in the client's best interest.
CRD number
A unique ID the securities industry uses to track every registered firm and individual; used here to trace Wing's registration history precisely.
AUM
Assets under management — the total dollar value of client money a firm actively manages or advises on. The standard measure of real scale in this industry.
Form ADV
The standard disclosure document every SEC-registered adviser must file publicly: ownership, fees, conflicts of interest, business details.
Fiduciary
Legally obligated to act in the client's best interest, not just give reasonable advice.
Diligence
The process of fact-checking a company's claims before an institution partners with, invests in, or pilots its product.
Category creation
Inventing a new name for a type of product instead of describing it in terms customers already understand — risky because it requires teaching the market from scratch.
Solicitor / referral disclosure
A legal requirement to state it publicly when a company pays (or gets paid) for referring clients to a financial adviser.
Positioning
How a company explains what it does and why it's different, in a way a customer immediately understands.
