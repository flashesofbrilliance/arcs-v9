01

What "honest" buys you

the point

A resume meets a background check and a reference call. So the bar was never impressive… it's true. Every line is graded by how checkable it is, and nothing gets written past its evidence. Which is also the rare part: most tools help you sound better; this one makes sure you can back it up. (Yes… it was run on its own landing page, too.)

● documented

Public and checkable… a repo, a commit count, a live link, a credential. Say it plainly.

● self-reported

Your number… plausible, consistent, not independently verified. Stated as yours, never past what your profile already shows.

● unverifiable

Tacit or subjective. It can frame the work; it never becomes a fact with a number. Flagged as a thing to be ready to defend.

02

How it works

six steps
1
READ
Read the posting. Your LinkedIn export is the source of truth for titles, dates, and certs… old resumes are just your own say-so.
2
GRADE
Give every claim its evidence and a checkability grade. One graded record; every draft is built from it.
3
STRESS-TEST
Flag what you couldn't defend: the overclaim, the one gig dressed as a specialty, the borrowed jargon, the NDA line. The flags become your prep.
4
ORDER
Lead with what the role actually wants, differentiator second. Experience newest-first; the framing by relevance.
5
BUILD
A print-clean PDF, a matching cover letter, and a plain ATS copy for the upload… then it checks the actual pixels, because page-count isn't a pass.
6
RED-TEAM
A mock hiring panel picks it apart. Every hit is checked against your evidence, and only the true ones change a word. Subtractive, never puffed.

05

How the honesty is enforced

four gates · open source

"Honest" is a promise anything can make. Here it is four checks that run before a page or resume ships, so the claim is mechanical, not a mood. Three of them are a plain grep or a measurement, so they cost nothing and never guess; the fourth asks a model to grade the judgment calls. This same suite runs on this page.

1
STYLE
A word-level lint that blocks the commit on the tired filler with almost no honest use on a resume, and on any em-dash. A grep decides it, so it is fast and it never argues.
2
HUMAN BASELINE
Measures two things machine prose gives away: sentence-length variety and how many lines actually name a tool, system, or number. Models flatten both; the gate reports when a surface has gone generic. Proxies for reading human, never an AI-detector score.
3
JUDGMENT
The tells a grep cannot call: forced either-or contrast, everything padded to three, the dramatic colon reveal, the uniform cadence. A model scores these against a public rubric. Advisory, because one deliberate contrast line can be the best sentence on the page.
4
FACTUAL
Every dollar, percent, or count on a page carries an evidence entry: a line in a real master, or a number you vouched for yourself. An invented figure has no entry, so it cannot ship. Style slop and fabricated numbers are different failures, and this catches the second one.

Stdlib-only Python, and wired into this repo's pre-commit. Each check ships with a self-test that proves it fails on a bad sample and passes on a good one, so the gate itself is checkable. Packaged for npm and brew next.
