02
Gap analysis

Four gaps, ranked by what they cost you

1 · Seven focus sectors, presented with equal weight, no stated wedge

Coherence

Responsible AI, HealthTech, Public Impact, Consumer Lifestyle, Future of Food, Future of Work, Future of Media — all seven get identical visual and narrative treatment on the homepage. But on our Mar 20 call, you named three sectors unprompted and with real conviction — authenticity/provenance, fashion tech, and AI-and-job-displacement/upskilling — as the ones "interesting to me at least at the jump." That's a materially narrower, sharper list than what the site currently shows a visitor. The site reads as "we cover everything adjacent to brand and mission"; the call revealed something closer to a real thesis.

2 · Case studies are indexed but not told

Credibility

Nine real, named engagements — Aescape, Twill, WelcomePAC, Fisher Wallace Labs, Cars & Bids, Pedal — exist as tags (category + role) with no visible narrative of what actually happened or what changed. For a studio selling "narrative clarity" as a core service, a case-study index with no narrative is the single page most likely to contradict the pitch on contact.

3 · The mentorship funnel already matches a pitch you haven't fully adopted

Cross-referenced against Mar 20 call

/scottwitt already runs a free-session-to-paid-tier structure — free 45-min First Session, then $225/30-min Office Hours. That's structurally the same mechanism proposed in the ARCS five-pager sent same-day after the Mar 20 call: stop giving away discovery calls, build a paid springboard. The site shows you'd already half-built it. Worth naming plainly: this isn't a pitch for something new, it's confirmation the model you were already running has legs.

4 · "Complex challenge. Accepted." carries no proof point next to it

Discoverability

The line appears twice mid-page with no example, case, or metric attached — a strong tagline sitting in isolation rather than anchored to one of the nine real engagements that could substantiate it directly.

03
Quick wins

Same thesis, more evidence of it

"I'm the guide who un-complicates. I specialize in the consequential and the complex."your own self-description — the exact sentence the case-study pages should be proving, not just asserting
  • Narrow the seven sectors to the three you named live. Authenticity/provenance, fashion tech, AI-and-displacement/upskilling — stated with real conviction on the Mar 20 call, absent as a distinct grouping on the homepage today.
  • Attach one paragraph of real narrative to three case studies, not nine. Twill (Chief Brand Officer, not just advisor) and Aescape (Fractional CBO) are the two with the most seniority attached — those are the ones a visitor most wants the actual story on.
  • State the mentorship funnel's own thesis on the homepage. "Free session earns trust, paid office hours monetize judgment" is already true of /scottwitt — it's just not named as a repeatable model anywhere else on the site.
  • Pair "Complex challenge. Accepted." with one specific example. A single sentence — which challenge, which client, what changed — turns the tagline from an assertion into a data point.

The delta in one line: today the site lists what Parlance has touched. The next version shows which three things it's actually built a repeatable method for.

Where the read and the record agree

Public record confirms the live-call read, not a new discovery.
