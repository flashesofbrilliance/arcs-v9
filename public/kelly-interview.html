SIGNAL — Competitive Intelligence System
Your team knows within hours when a competitor makes a meaningful move.
Event-driven pipeline. Deterministic evals. Role-based output channels. No scraping. No ToS risk. Fully replaceable of what Juniper's team already tried to build.
Two registers — same system
Executive
Sales walks into every demo with last week's battlecard, not last quarter's. Product sees what customers are asking competitors for. You get a three-bullet Monday brief on competitive position. When a competitor raises a round, ships a feature, or posts six ML jobs in a week — you find out that day.
Technical
Pollers watch ToS-safe sources on configurable intervals → events queue → Claude claude-sonnet-5 per event: classify · extract · score · route. Output channels: Slack digest, living battlecard doc, feature gap log, CRM enrichment webhook. Evals are deterministic: precision/recall on competitor classification, grounding check on summaries.
Architecture — five layers
01
Sources
Competitor press rooms, changelog pages, job posting pages (not profile data — that's the ToS fix), G2/Capterra reviews, SEC EDGAR filings, company social accounts, your own CRM deal notes mentioning competitor names.
02
Triggers
Configurable event watchers: new blog post → fires · 5+ AI/ML job postings in 30 days → fires · new funding round · new G2 review mentioning pricing · 8-K filing. Trigger = signal, not noise.
03
Processing
Claude claude-sonnet-5 per event. Four outputs per event: who (competitor) · what (signal type: product/pricing/hiring/funding/messaging) · relevance score 1–5 vs. Juniper's position · stakeholder tag (exec/sales/product).
04
Outputs
Exec: weekly Slack digest, 3 bullets, red/yellow/green on competitive posture. Sales: auto-updated battlecard (what changed in competitor messaging this week). Product: feature gap log extracted from competitor reviews. Hiring intel: alert when competitor signals new product direction via recruiting.
05
Evals
Deterministic, not vibes. Recall: did we catch event X within Y hours? Precision: is the competitor classification correct? Grounding: is the summary faithful to source? Weekly eval report. Thresholds trigger a human review flag — system doesn't overstate its own confidence.
The LinkedIn fix
Juniper's existing system was scraping profile data — personal work history, contact info. That's the ToS violation. Job posting pages are different. A company's own job listings are public marketing material. Signal quality for competitive intel from job postings is ~80% of what you get from profiles — hiring patterns reveal product direction, team build-out, and technical bets without touching personal data. The fix isn't a workaround. It's a cleaner architecture.
