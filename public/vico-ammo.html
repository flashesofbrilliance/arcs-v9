The deeper case

What the live demo showed, and the things worth naming directly

For Joe, on the VP of Engineering seat.
The one-line version

The strongest evidence here isn't a resume line, it's what Joe already watched happen live: a working LLM product, architected and shipped end to end, running the same kind of system this team is building. For a hands-on VP, that's the point: he can lead the build because he is already doing the build.

What Joe actually saw on the call

1

A live scoring system: an algorithm that screens incoming opportunities against weighted criteria, scores them, and reweights based on which calls turned out right or wrong. The same discipline as scoring a forecast: commit the call first, check it against the outcome after. He runs that discipline in public too, with dated predictions whose falsifier is written down first, then scored and corrected in the open (the calibration receipts).

2

A working automation pipeline: a tool that takes real evidence and produces a tailored, verified output, end to end, not a mockup.

3

A public status log: a live site tracking real ship velocity, day by day, since the project started. Not a claim about moving fast, a record of it.

The things worth naming directly

Title. Zach hasn't formally held a "VP/Director of Engineering" title. The scope has: leading engineering teams, owning delivery and governance for a $7B launch, recruiting a company's first CTO. It's a title gap, not a scope gap, and he'll say so first.

Stack. Python, React, and Postgres are weekly tools. GCP specifically is the one he'd ramp on: his cloud experience is on Vercel/Supabase and adjacent, not GCP. Nameable and fast to close.

RAG and fine-tuning in production. A local embedding service is built and running and the pgvector design is thought through correctly, but the persistent retrieval write-path isn't shipped, and his LLM work is agentic orchestration and evals rather than fine-tuning. The design is real; the at-scale production version is the gap, and it's the kind that closes fast inside a real system with a real team.

A better way to test it than a resume line

Rather than take the resume's word for the AI-native claim, watch the same live demo Joe already saw: a real, running product, not a rehearsed pitch. That's a faster, more honest signal than another round of describing it.

None of this is a reason not to talk. It's the reason to talk about the right things. Happy to go deeper on any of it, on a call or here.
