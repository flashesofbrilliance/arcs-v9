The deeper case

What the live demo showed, and the things worth naming directly

For Joe, on the VP of Engineering seat.
The one-line version

The clearest signal here isn't a resume line, it's what Joe already watched happen live: a working AI product (built on an LLM), designed and shipped end to end, running the same kind of system this team is building. For a hands-on VP, that is the point: he can lead the build because he is already doing the build.

What Joe actually saw on the call

1

A live scoring system: a tool that takes each new opportunity, rates it against a set of weighted criteria, and adjusts those weights based on which past calls turned out right or wrong. Same habit as scoring a forecast: make the call first, check it against the outcome after. He does this in public too, with dated predictions where he writes down in advance what would prove him wrong, then scores and corrects them in the open (the calibration receipts). A small sample so far, but filed where anyone can check them.

2

A working automation pipeline: a tool that takes real evidence and produces a finished, checked output, end to end, not a mockup.

3

A public status log: a live site tracking how much ships, day by day, since the project started. Not a claim about moving fast, a record of it.

The things worth naming directly

Title. Zach hasn't formally held a "VP/Director of Engineering" title. The scope has: leading engineering teams, owning delivery and governance for a $7B launch, recruiting a company's first CTO. It's a title gap, not a scope gap, and he'll say so first.

Stack. Python, React, and Postgres are weekly tools. GCP specifically is the one he would ramp on: his cloud experience is on Vercel and Supabase and next door, not GCP. Easy to name, fast to close.

Look-up and fine-tuning in production. The part that looks things up for the AI (RAG) is designed and partly built, a working embedding service and a sound database design. But the full always-on version is not shipped yet, and his AI work is orchestration and testing (evals) rather than fine-tuning a model. The design is real; the at-scale production version is the gap, and it is the kind that closes fast inside a real system with a real team.

A better way to test it than a resume line

Rather than take the resume's word for how AI-fluent he is, watch the same live demo Joe already saw: a real, running product. That is a faster, more honest signal than another round of describing it.

None of this is a reason not to talk. It's the reason to talk about the right things. Happy to go deeper on any of it, on a call or here.

Terms marked like this above show their plain meaning on hover or tap:
LLM
A large language model, the kind of AI behind tools like ChatGPT and Claude.
RAG
Retrieval-augmented generation: the AI pulls relevant facts from your own data before it answers, instead of guessing.
Embedding
Turning text into numbers so a computer can find the most relevant passages by meaning, not just keywords.
Evals
A fixed set of test questions with known answers, run against the AI to catch when a change makes it worse.
Fine-tuning
Further-training a model on your own examples so it specializes, instead of just prompting an off-the-shelf one.
GCP
Google Cloud Platform, Google's cloud hosting service. The stack VICO runs on.
