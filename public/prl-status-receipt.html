Why we show you a losing receipt

This is the first receipt this program has closed, and it closed against our own estimate. That is the point of the system. The receipt is only worth something if it stays honest when the news is bad: the miss gets measured, the cap absorbs it, and the lesson gets built into the next phase instead of padded into the next price. From here on, every phase opens with a written bet and closes with a page like this one. You never have to take a status report on trust; you can always read the last receipt.

Fine print · how the actual was measured
method, for the record
No hour log was kept during the phase, so the actual is reconstructed from work records: 7 distinct working days between Aug 10 and Aug 26 with 109 tracked changes on this project's files, plus untracked coordination time (vendor calls, CRM sessions, editor sessions). Three scenarios were computed: a conservative floor of about 28 hours (4 focused hours per working day), a central estimate of about 42 (6 per day), and a high case of about 56 (8 per day). Even the floor sits at the very top of the agreed 12 to 30 hour band, which is why the receipt closes as a miss rather than a judgment call. The mid-phase flag published here on Aug 18 called this early; nothing on this page changed after the fact. The 1.3 bet will cite this receipt at kickoff.
