The week my notes started filing themselves (the 90-second version)

A recruiter I didn't know messaged me on a Saturday about a company I'd never heard of.

99.9% of people archive that message. I ran a ten-minute check instead: does the sender exist, does the role exist somewhere they don't control, and one question no script can answer ("what's the story with the team?"). It passed. Honest answers pass. So do honest gaps, because "I don't know much" is harder to fake than a pitch.

Then the part that matters. The application took two minutes, run right before the call, because the hours had gone into building a reusable method, not a document. That's the AI unlock most people hold backwards: they speed up the task and re-prompt from scratch every time. The compounding move is to build the machine once and make every future task a two-minute run.

What happened next was a month of "procrastination":

  • My notes started filing themselves (the index rebuilds from the files' own headers; nothing can fall out of it)
  • The links between notes drew a pyramid on their own, and the most-cited idea in my whole archive turned out to be "cut, don't add"
  • A privacy gate refused 100% of exports on day one, correctly
  • An audit of my own month found my most expensive AI run bought a negative result, my failure log can commemorate but not calibrate, and my predictions consistently undershoot how warm people are

And my favorite: the automated check that keeps my public pages honest rejected the very commit that created my doctrine about drift, because that commit had itself caused drift. I built a spell-checker for my own honesty and its first catch was mine, in the sentence announcing it.

Three lessons I'm keeping: receipts or it didn't happen, because the easiest person to lie to is yourself. Some procrastination is your attention reading a window your calendar has no axis for. And your worst critic makes an excellent employee once you give it a job description.

The tool that started it all is free: resume-tailor.arcs.care. The full field notes, failures included, are one level deeper.
