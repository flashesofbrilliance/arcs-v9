Anterior + posterior

The 720° pressure test, pointed at the compass itself

Dope is a non-GAAP word. It earns a GAAP reading only through three consequences that each leave a custody trace in a separate table: sticky (the same person returns on a later day), curiosity-inducing (a follow-up names the tributary it came from), infectious (a second human arrives because of a shared brief). Model self-grades never count. Silence is a finding: a dashed cell is a tilde, not a zero.

First 360° · anterior (predict before you count)
  • EX-ANTE: name the tier you expect before running the query; the receipt scores the prediction, not the mood.
  • Cohort first: exclude the author, exclude provenance test, exclude anonymous rows. What is left is the only set that can be dope.
  • One trace, one table: sticky from Receipt dates, curiosity from parentTributaryIndex, infectious from shares. No column serves two traces.
  • Pre-register thresholds: 7 days for return, 72 hours after a nudge counts as pushed not pulled, 30 real seeds and 30 outcomes at S.
  • BAR: the ruin case is a dope-looking corpus made of one person's probes. Guard it structurally.
Second 360° · posterior (after the rows)
  • Receipts: what the tables actually hold, by cohort, with tildes on every inferred number.
  • Recoil: did the predicted tier land, or did the corpus stay in fog?
  • Orthogonal echo: two traces agreeing on the same receipts without sharing a column is the hardness metric.
  • KKL: is the window still open, or is the claim stale (a receipt older than its horizon with no outcome)?
  • Fun House: audit the auditor. The founder wrote most of the corpus; enthusiasm is not evidence.
Diamond rule: a receipt is S-tier only when it survives both sweeps and at least two of the three traces fire on it from distinct tables, inside a cohort of real-provenance decisions large enough for the Accountant to calibrate (30 closed). Today that cohort is empty. The ladder below says where the corpus sits meanwhile.
