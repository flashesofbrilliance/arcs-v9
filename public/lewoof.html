DTC (.com) Pilot — Decision Brief Revised · Aug 3

Channel moved from Amazon to Le Woof's own Shopify  ·  Prepared for Hila  ·  August 3, 2026

Moving to .com flips the recommendation. On Amazon the lead was a flat fee. On your own Shopify, lead with a 50/50 split of net sales, B&B funding acquisition from its half. It pays you more per unit than a flat fee ($11.70 vs $8), puts the paid-traffic risk on the party running paid traffic, and — because it's your store — a rev-share is no longer "opening your books to them."

The prize is now unambiguously yours: the storefront, customer emails, and reviews all live on lewoofbrand.com. The Amazon "who owns the ASIN" risk is gone — replaced by one hard line: B&B runs Petsie, so they never get your customer list. Keep a flat fee ($10–12) as the simple fallback.

What changes on .com vs Amazon

  • No 15% referral, no FBA — replaced by Shopify processing (~2.9%), DTC pick/pack, and shipping. Pie stays about the same size (~$3,336 vs $3,540).
  • Cash flow inverts — you're merchant of record; the $45 lands in your Stripe. "Flat fee where B&B keeps the rest" no longer maps cleanly; a split is the natural shape.
  • Traffic is bought, not found — no marketplace demand, so acquisition is the whole game. The operator running it should carry its risk.
  • Transparency flips your way — it's your dashboard, so B&B is the one trusting your numbers. The reason to avoid 50/50 on Amazon is gone.

Non-negotiables (.com)

  • Le Woof owns the storefront, all customer data, the email/SMS list, and reviews — B&B may not use, export, port or retain them, and never for Petsie. This is the asset.
  • Le Woof is merchant of record and controls pricing & checkout (if B&B ever collects, funds settle to you within 5 days with order-level reporting).
  • Consignment; you own inventory until sold; UCC-1 — unsold units revert to you.
  • Price consistency with Faire wholesale — .com can't undercut your wholesale accounts.
  • Use-restriction vs Petsie survives 3 yrs; pilot pricing is not a precedent — expansion reprices toward $22.50.

How to position it

"Starting on our own site is the right call — we each build something that's ours: your acquisition engine, our customers and brand. Cleanest shape is a 50/50 split of net sales, you fund the ads from your half so you're only ever acquiring profitably, and the storefront, customer list, and reviews stay with Le Woof. Because it's our Shopify, you get full dashboard visibility — no black box. If you'd rather a fixed number, we can do a flat per-unit instead."

When to walk

B&B wants the customer list / email data · won't fund acquisition · or wants to run the pilot on B&B's own domain instead of lewoofbrand.com. Any of those and Faire wholesale at ~$22.50 is the better home for the units.
Base case: $45 retail, 30% ad spend, $8 shipping absorbed, $4 landed cost, 240-unit consignment pilot. ARCS gauntlet: on_purpose=aligned · irreversibility=low · NCI ≈ 90. Confirm merchant-of-record, B&B's DTC acquisition capability, and customer-data ownership before signing.
