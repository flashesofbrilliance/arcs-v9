The press release, written as if August 22 already happened

Seattle, WA – August 22, 2026

theTrueAI's First Cohort Opens Full, Filled by Builders Who Wanted Proof Before They Wanted a Class

The four-week AI Product Builder Programme starts today with a waitlist longer than the seats – because it promised one shipped product, not another set of slides about prompting.

theTrueAI, founded by former Amazon Alexa AI product leader Jalaja Padma, today opened its first formal cohort of the AI Product Builder Programme – four weeks, live, ending with one real product shipped to real users. The programme was built on a simple premise: anyone can build something with AI now. Almost no one has been taught how to tell whether it's the right thing to build.

Most professionals using AI today are optimizing for speed – more output, faster iteration, another tool in the stack. Few have been taught the harder skill underneath it: judgment. theTrueAI's own research citations put a number on the cost of that gap – 95% of enterprise AI pilots deliver no measurable return (MIT NANDA), and 42% of failed startups built something nobody needed (CB Insights). Speed didn't cause those failures. Direction did.

The programme runs 40 hours over four live weeks, pairs every builder with weekly checkpoints and a Demo Day, and requires only a real problem and access to one frontier model – no engineering background needed. Past cohort participants have shipped a personalized health and fitness tracker, a stock-trading and portfolio tool, and a custom AI solution built for a working therapist's practice – three different people, three different domains, the same underlying discipline.

"We didn't set out to teach AI tools. We set out to teach the thing that was always hard, that AI just made more urgent: knowing what's worth building before you spend the hours and the tokens finding out the hard way." Jalaja Padma, Founder, theTrueAI Illustrative quote – written to match her stated positioning (her own LinkedIn: "AI adoption is mostly human work"), not something she has said to Zach directly

The programme costs $1,200, with a $500 rate for verified students and recent graduates, group pricing for teams and colleges, and a full money-back guarantee – attend the sessions, do the builds, and one email gets a full refund if it isn't the right fit.

"I'd used AI every day for a year and still couldn't tell if what I was building mattered. Four weeks in, I shipped something a real person actually uses. That's the part no tutorial gave me." Internal FAQ

The questions a real PR/FAQ review doesn't let you skip

This is the part of the exercise that matters most, and the part most pre-launch PR/FAQs skip. Every answer below is verified against the live site as of this scan, not aspirational.

Does the "Apply" button on the homepage actually let someone apply today?

No

The homepage "Apply" CTA and full pricing table read as if enrolment is open. Clicking through to /apply reveals "Enrolment isn't open yet" – it's an email waitlist, no pricing tier, no confirmation, no application. This is the single largest gap between the press release above and the site as it exists right now – and it sits exactly on the GTM/conversion problem raised in the original outreach.

Is the blog's "weekly insights" promise accurate?

No

The blog's signup CTA promises weekly insights; the three visible posts are dated Mar 14–27, 2026 – roughly four months stale as of this scan. Small on its own, same shape as the Apply gap: a stated cadence the site isn't keeping, in exactly the place a skeptical visitor is deciding whether to trust the bigger promise.

Is applicant data protected once enrolment actually opens?

Not yet configured

The live site sets no Content-Security-Policy, no X-Frame-Options/frame-ancestors, no x-content-type-options, and no referrer-policy. Low urgency today with no session state on the site, but worth fixing before the site starts handling real applications and payment intent.

What's the biggest unproven assumption in the press release above?

Open

That informal pilot enthusiasm from friends and family predicts paid-stranger conversion. Those are different audiences under different incentives. This isn't a criticism of the pilots – it's the actual open question worth testing before, not after, the cohort fills.

What could keep the August 22 cohort from filling?

Open

Not the offer – the pricing, guarantee, and instructor credibility are all genuinely strong. The risk is the funnel: a visitor who's ready to commit hits an email waitlist instead of a confirmation, at the exact moment of highest intent. Fixing that one gap is most of the GTM work originally asked about.
