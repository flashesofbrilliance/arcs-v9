02
Gap analysis

Three gaps, all pointed at the exact GTM problem you named

1 · "Apply" promises an application; the real experience is a waitlist

Conversion

This is the highest-leverage one, because it sits directly on your stated problem. A visitor reads the full pricing table, the guarantee, the FAQ, decides they're in, clicks "Apply" – and lands on an email capture with no pricing, no program detail, and no confirmation their pricing plan carried over. That's a real drop-off point, not just a copy nit: everything the homepage sold gets reset to zero at the exact moment of highest intent.

2 · The blog promises "weekly" and hasn't posted in ~4 months

Trust

Three good posts, then silence since late March. A stale "weekly insights" promise is a small thing on its own, but it's the same shape as the /apply gap – a stated cadence the site isn't actually keeping, right where a skeptical visitor is deciding whether to trust the newer, bigger promise (a $1,200 course from a pre-launch instructor).

3 · Contact runs through a personal Gmail address

Polish

Minor, but consistent with the other two: small signals of "not fully switched on yet" clustering in exactly the places (apply, ongoing content, contact) that a GTM-focused visitor evaluates trust through. None of these are hard to fix – that's the actual point.

03
GTM quick wins

Ideas, not a checklist – you know this space better than I do

  • Match the "Apply" button's promise to what happens after the click. Either rename it ("Join the Waitlist," "Get Notified") until enrolment truly opens, or open a real lightweight application now that at least confirms the pricing tier and next steps – right now the CTA text oversells the destination.
  • Either post weekly or stop promising weekly. Three strong posts is a fine start; the mismatch is the "weekly" claim, not the frequency itself. An honest cadence line ("occasional, no spam") costs nothing and removes a small trust gap.
  • Move off the personal Gmail before the August cohort. A one-line forwarding address (hello@thetrueai.ai) is a five-minute fix that removes the one visibly "pre-launch" signal in an otherwise polished contact flow.
  • The named third-party proof points (MIT, CB Insights, Pendo) are your strongest asset – lead harder with them. Most pre-launch courses assert their own unproven numbers; you're citing real external research instead. That's a genuine differentiator worth making even more prominent, not just supporting evidence lower on the page.

The delta in one line: the offer and the instructor credibility are real and well-built. The site currently promises a smoother, more-open experience than actually exists three clicks in – closing that gap is most of the GTM/conversion work you flagged.
