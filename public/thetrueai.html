02
Gap analysis

Three gaps. All pointed at the problem you named.

Apply promises an application. Apply delivers a waitlist.

Conversion

A visitor reads the pricing, the guarantee, the FAQ. Decides they're in. Clicks Apply. Lands on an email field. Everything the homepage sold resets to zero at the exact moment of highest intent. That's not a copy problem. That's the funnel breaking at its most expensive step.

Weekly, promised. Four months, delivered.

Trust

Small gap, same shape as the first one. A stated cadence the site isn't keeping, right where a skeptical visitor decides whether to trust the bigger promise – a $1,200 course, from a founder who hasn't launched yet.

Contact runs through a personal Gmail.

Polish

Minor. Same signal, third time. Not broken – just not fully switched on, in exactly the three places a GTM-minded visitor tests for trust: applying, staying in touch, reaching out.

03
GTM quick wins

Ideas, not a checklist

  • Make Apply mean apply. Rename the button until enrolment truly opens, or open a real lightweight application now. Right now the word oversells the click.
  • Post weekly, or say occasional. Three strong posts is a fine start. The mismatch is the word "weekly," not the pace itself.
  • Retire the Gmail before August. One forwarding address removes the last visibly-unlaunched signal on an otherwise polished flow.
  • Lead harder with MIT, CB Insights, Pendo. Most pre-launch courses invent their own numbers. You're citing real research instead. That's the differentiator – put it up front, not lower down.

One line for the delta: the offer is real. The site currently promises a smoother, more-open experience than it has, three clicks in. Closing that gap is most of the GTM work.
