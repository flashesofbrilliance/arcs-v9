00
First, the favor

One hygiene fix, no call required to act on it

No Content-Security-Policy or clickjacking protection on the live domain

Cheap fix

meetkiko.com's response headers set x-content-type-options: nosniff and a real referrer-policy, but there's no CSP and no X-Frame-Options / frame-ancestors – the page can currently be iframed by any third-party site. There's also a wildcard access-control-allow-origin: * on the HTML document itself, which usually belongs on an API route, not a rendered page – a sign the Cloudflare config got copied from somewhere else rather than tuned per-route. Low urgency today since the page holds no session state, but it's a ten-minute Cloudflare rules change. Happy to point at the exact header set if useful.

02
Gap analysis

Five gaps, ranked by what they cost you

1 · "AI negotiates and contracts with creators" is the highest-liability claim on the page

Risk

Automated negotiation and deal-execution with third parties is exactly where brand-safety and legal exposure concentrate for a platform like this. The "team reviews every AI interaction" line is reassuring, but it's asserted, not shown – no detail on where human review sits in the loop, what's binding without it, or what happens when it's wrong. For a company selling trust as its category, this is the one claim that most needs a visible, specific answer.

2 · No stated ICP, on a page selling three different buying decisions

Coherence

Sourcing, Intelligence, and Growth Partner are three different sales cycles and three different buyers – but nothing on the page tells a first-time visitor which one matches their size or stage. A DTC founder and a Fortune 500 CMO read the same homepage today with no default path. That's a real decision-architecture gap, not a copy nit.

3 · "$100M+ scaled" is unattributed

Credibility

No named brand, no case study, no logo wall visible anywhere in the rendered page. For a site whose entire thesis is "trust is the new distribution," the site's own trust signals are thin relative to the claim it's making about itself.

4 · Pricing without a path to actually pay it

Conversion

$200/mo and $100/mo read as anchors, but there's no signup form or checkout flow anywhere on the homepage – every CTA routes off-page. If Sourcing and Intelligence are meant to be genuinely self-serve, that's the highest-leverage build; if Growth Partner is actually the revenue engine, the self-serve pricing may be doing more marketing work than sales work, which is fine – but worth being deliberate about which one it is.

5 · The founder isn't on the site, and the story is unusually good

Narrative

Head of Product for Beyoncé at Parkwood. Before that, built an artist-to-fan texting product (SuperPhone) after reading about how a working musician stayed connected to fans directly. Before Kiko, five years running Shop Galaxy through institutional backing (Floodgate, Homebrew, Maveron, Snapchat, among others). That's a founder who has personally lived both sides of "trust as distribution" – once as the artist's team, once as the founder raising real capital. None of it is on meetkiko.com.

03
GTM quick wins

Same category insight, more evidence of it

"Distribution is the only moat."meetkiko.com, hero copy – the sentence the whole redesign should prove, not just state
  • The hero becomes a live feed, not a slogan. A ticking, lightly anonymized stream of the sourcing engine actually running – creators found, vetted, ranked, negotiated – the way the current creator-rolodex module hints at but doesn't sustain. The site becomes the product demo instead of a claim about one.
  • One redacted sample deliverable, gated by email. A real Viral Brief or buyer shortlist, anonymized. Proof, lead magnet, and product spec in one artifact – the same move that turns "trust me" into "watch it work."
  • A founder page that tells the real story in six sentences. Beyoncé/Parkwood → SuperPhone (artist-to-fan trust, built once already) → Shop Galaxy (institutional capital, five years, real exits from real backers) → Kiko. Studios and brands buy operators with provenance; this provenance happens to be unusually on-thesis.
  • Pricing shape wired to an actual path. "Monthly license · self-serve shortlist in 48 hours" if Sourcing is meant to be self-serve – or, if it isn't yet, a clear "book a call" frame instead of prices that don't connect to checkout.
  • A visible answer to the AI-negotiation question before anyone has to ask it. One sentence – what's human-reviewed, what's binding, where the line is – turns the highest-risk claim on the page into the strongest trust signal on the page. That's the "trust is the new distribution" thesis applied to Kiko's own site, not just its pitch.

The delta in one line: today the site asserts trust is the new distribution. The next site demonstrates it, on itself, first.

04
Where the machines might interlock

Kiko finds the right creators. ARCS makes sure the decisions built on top of that evidence hold up.

These are hypotheses, not a pitch – we haven't really talked shop yet, so take this as me thinking out loud rather than anything polished. The shape I'd guess at, based on the site alone: Kiko produces evidence (who to work with, what's working, what to spend) and a brand still has to decide what to do with it – budget commitment, campaign kill/continue calls, which creator relationships to deepen versus drop. That decision layer is the thing I've been building toward. A few places this might actually be real, worth kicking around next time we talk:

A · Kill/continue decisions on creator spend

Worth testing live

Viral Intelligence tells a brand what's working; Growth Partner runs the engine. Somewhere in between is a recurring decision – keep this creator, cut that one, double down here – made under incomplete information and internal disagreement (marketing wants reach, finance wants CAC, the founder wants brand fit). A decision receipt at that checkpoint – who argued what, what evidence was weighed, what the actual call was – is exactly the layer Kiko's data feeds and doesn't currently own.

B · The AI-negotiation trust gap, closed with a receipt instead of a claim

Directly addresses Gap #1 above

If "our team reviews every AI interaction" became a receipt-backed trail – who reviewed which negotiation, when, what changed – the highest-liability claim on the site turns into the strongest evidence of it. That's a narrow, concrete pilot rather than a platform rebuild.

C · Two founders, two forms of "trust is the new distribution"

Longer bet

You built one artist-to-fan trust product already (SuperPhone) before building one for brands (Kiko). My last decade has been on the decision-governance side of high-stakes calls – PE turnarounds, launching $7B of digital assets at Grayscale through regulatory chaos. If there's a real overlap it's probably not obvious from a website; better to find it in conversation than assert it here.
