First, the favor

One hygiene fix, no call required to act on it

No Content-Security-Policy or clickjacking protection on the live domain

Cheap fix

meetkiko.com's response headers set x-content-type-options: nosniff and a real referrer-policy, but there's no CSP and no X-Frame-Options / frame-ancestors – the page can currently be iframed by any third-party site. There's also a wildcard access-control-allow-origin: * on the HTML document itself, which usually belongs on an API route, not a rendered page – a sign the Cloudflare config got copied from somewhere else rather than tuned per-route. Low urgency today since the page holds no session state, but it's a ten-minute Cloudflare rules change. Happy to point at the exact header set if useful.

02
Gap analysis

Five gaps, ranked by what they cost you

1 · Your best trust proof is three clicks deep, and the homepage never points at it

Discoverability

/creator-sourcing has real, specific detail here – "our team reviews and approves every touchpoint and interaction, it's never autopilot," contracts routed through legal review with a 3–5 day turnaround, full audit logging on every negotiation. That's a genuinely credible answer to the exact question a wary buyer would ask about AI doing outreach and negotiation. But nothing on the homepage hints it exists – "TRUST IS THE NEW DISTRIBUTION" is the whole trust pitch up top, and a visitor who doesn't click through to Creator Sourcing never sees the proof behind it. (Could be deliberate segmentation rather than an oversight – worth just asking rather than me assuming.)

2 · No stated ICP, on a page selling three different buying decisions

Coherence

Sourcing, Intelligence, and Growth Partner are three different sales cycles and three different buyers – but nothing on the page tells a first-time visitor which one matches their size or stage. A DTC founder and a Fortune 500 CMO read the same homepage today with no default path. That's a real decision-architecture gap, not a copy nit.

3 · Real-looking metrics on /growth aren't labeled as anyone's, in particular

Credibility

The /growth page shows a live-looking Q1 2026 campaign log – 47 experiments, 200+ creators tested, +34% revenue growth, 4.2x ROAS – but it's not clear whether that's one client's real dashboard or an illustrative composite, and no named brand or logo appears anywhere on the site. Worth asking directly: is that a real account, and if so, is it shareable as a named case study? Right now the numbers are doing less work than they could.

4 · Pricing without a path to actually pay it

Conversion

$200/mo (confirmed, repeated across /compare) reads as an anchor, but there's no signup form or checkout flow anywhere on the homepage or /creator-sourcing – every CTA routes off-page. Viral Intelligence has no price live anywhere I could find. If Sourcing is meant to be genuinely self-serve, that's the highest-leverage build; if Growth Partner is actually the revenue engine, the self-serve pricing may be doing more marketing work than sales work, which is fine – but worth being deliberate about which one it is.

5 · The founder story exists and is strong – it's just orphaned on /growth

Discoverability

/growth has a real, first-person, signed bio – Beyoncé/Parkwood, the fashion resale marketplace backed by Snapchat and Floodgate, "creators are what drive commerce." It's a strong, on-thesis story. It's just not linked anywhere from the homepage, so most visitors never reach it – same shape as gap #1, a distribution problem on your own site about a company that sells distribution. (Same caveat as above: possibly deliberate, worth asking rather than assuming.)

GTM quick wins

Same category insight, more evidence of it

"Distribution is the only moat."your LinkedIn, Kiko Creators listing – the sentence the site itself should prove, not just the resume line
  • The hero becomes a live feed, not a slogan. A ticking, lightly anonymized stream of the sourcing engine actually running – creators found, vetted, ranked, negotiated – the way the current creator-rolodex module hints at but doesn't sustain. The site becomes the product demo instead of a claim about one.
  • One redacted sample deliverable, gated by email. A real Viral Brief or buyer shortlist, anonymized. Proof, lead magnet, and product spec in one artifact – the same move that turns "trust me" into "watch it work."
  • Link the /growth founder bio from the homepage. It already exists, in your own words – Beyoncé/Parkwood → Shop Galaxy (institutional capital, real backers) → Kiko. Studios and brands buy operators with provenance; this provenance is unusually on-thesis and currently one click further than anyone will bother to go.
  • Pricing shape wired to an actual path. $200/mo is real and confirmed across /compare, but never stated on the homepage or /creator-sourcing itself – "Monthly license · self-serve shortlist in 48 hours" would anchor the infrastructure claim right where people land.
  • Pull the Trust & Safety section from /creator-sourcing up to the homepage. "Never autopilot," legal review on every contract, full audit logging – that's the strongest answer to the AI-negotiation question you could ask for, and it's currently invisible to anyone who doesn't click through. That's the "trust is the new distribution" thesis applied to Kiko's own site, not just its pitch.

The delta in one line: today the site asserts trust is the new distribution. The next site demonstrates it, on itself, first.

04
Where the machines might interlock

Kiko finds the right creators. ARCS makes sure the decisions built on top of that evidence hold up.

These are hypotheses, not a pitch – we haven't really talked shop yet, so take this as me thinking out loud rather than anything polished. The shape I'd guess at, based on the site alone: Kiko produces evidence (who to work with, what's working, what to spend) and a brand still has to decide what to do with it – budget commitment, campaign kill/continue calls, which creator relationships to deepen versus drop. That decision layer is the thing I've been building toward. A few places this might actually be real, worth kicking around next time we talk:

A · Kill/continue decisions on creator spend

Worth testing live

Viral Intelligence tells a brand what's working; Growth Partner runs the engine. Somewhere in between is a recurring decision – keep this creator, cut that one, double down here – made under incomplete information and internal disagreement (marketing wants reach, finance wants CAC, the founder wants brand fit). A decision receipt at that checkpoint – who argued what, what evidence was weighed, what the actual call was – is exactly the layer Kiko's data feeds and doesn't currently own.

B · Kiko already logs the trail internally – the gap is turning it into proof the brand can see

Directly addresses Gap #1 above

You're not missing an audit trail – "every email, negotiation, and creator interaction is logged" is already a real claim on /creator-sourcing. The open question is who that log is actually for. If it's internal-only today, a receipt-shaped surface for the brand's side (why this creator, why this rate, who signed off) turns your existing operational discipline into an external trust artifact instead of an internal one. Narrow, concrete thing to compare notes on, not a platform rebuild.

C · Two founders, two forms of "trust is the new distribution"

Longer bet

You built one artist-to-fan trust product already (SuperPhone) before building one for brands (Kiko). My last decade has been on the decision-governance side of high-stakes calls – PE turnarounds, work on digital assets at Grayscale through regulatory chaos. (I'll give you the real numbers and specifics in person rather than assert a stat here that you can't check the way I just checked yours.) If there's a real overlap it's probably not obvious from a website; better to find it in conversation than assert it here.
