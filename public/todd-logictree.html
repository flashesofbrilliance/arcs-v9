The map
One greeting, three paths, one clean handoff

A visitor is sorted into the path that fits, asked only what a rep needs, then handed off as a single SugarCRM lead.

The branches
The three paths

Buyers are engineers, chemists, press operators, purchasing, and procurement. A real lead names a resin PRL sells. Vague inquiries are researchers.

Path A

Knows what they need

the common case · tag: ready-to-spec
  1. Which resin or spec are they after?
  2. Replacing something now? If yes, current resin.
  3. What's the application?
  4. Any requirements or specs (optional).

→ contact + location

Path B

Needs help finding one

application-first · tag: needs-help
  1. What are they making? (application)
  2. Material or performance requirements (temp, flex, color, regulatory).
  3. Currently using a resin to match or improve?

→ contact + location. Flagged for a human. The "recommend a formulation" AI is a later phase.

Path C

Something else

general question · tag: general (low priority)
  1. What can we point them to? (free text)
  2. Answer an FAQ inline if it matches, then offer to connect a human.
  3. Optionally capture contact and route as a low-priority lead.
Shared tail · all paths
Capture, then hand off
Contact + location
1
Name, email, phone (optional), company. Short and painless, since buyers usually already have a need and share readily.
2
Location for routing: geolocate by IP silently, or ask their state or region.
Handoff
3
Create a SugarCRM lead with every field + the branch tag (+ fit tag once defined), and email the notification.
4
Route to the rep by territory (to the sales manager for the MVP), and promise a reply within a few hours.

Persistence: partial leads are saved. A returning visitor resumes where they left off, so a half-finished form is not a lost lead.

ICP / fit gating · open, pending SM input

Sits right before handoff, after the core fields are captured. Not a hard gate: the bot never refuses to submit a lead. It's a soft tag that helps the sales manager triage what lands in his inbox, matching how he described qualification today (screening, not blocking).

Once the SM defines what "ideal" and "poor fit" look like (company size, industry, order volume, geography, or anything else he uses to prioritize), the lead gets tagged ideal, uncertain, or poor fit on the SugarCRM record. The tag never changes what the visitor sees or blocks the submission, it just tells the rep where to spend attention first.

Blocked until answered. Until the SM gives that criteria, no fit tag ships and every lead is treated the same as today.

Routing
MVP now, auto-route next

MVP

Every lead goes to SugarCRM plus an email to the sales manager. He assigns it to the rep by territory, which is his process today.

Target

Geolocate the visitor, look up the rep for that territory, and assign directly. Needs the territory map. The fit tag rides along on the same lead once defined, it does not change routing.

Resin recognition
The bot can now recognize a resin by name

Todd sent the Product Selection Guide, so this dependency is met. The bot can recognize a PRL resin the visitor types, understand the grade code, and even catch when someone names a competitor's resin instead.

What PRL sells

Product families

the prefixes the bot recognizes
  1. PC polycarbonate, ABS, ASA, PEI (Ultem class), PPX modified PPE (Noryl class), NY6 / NY66 nylon, TP PBT.
  2. Blends: PC/ABS, PC/PBT, ABS/PBT, PC/ASA, PPX/Nylon.
How grades are named

Nomenclature it can read

family + modifier + color + suffix
  1. Example: PC-FRUV2-7001-1 means polycarbonate, flame-retardant and UV-stabilized, black, with release.
  2. Modifiers (FR, glass, UV, FDA), a color-series number, and a finishing suffix. The bot can parse and confirm the code.
Competitor names

It catches resins PRL does not brand

from the guide's cross-reference
  1. When a visitor names a competitor grade (Lexan, Makrolon, Ultem, Noryl, Lustran, Valox, Zytel), the bot recognizes it as a real resin ask and flags it for the rep with the PRL equivalent.

Build order: family + competitor recognition first, full grade-code autocomplete as a fast-follow.

Open dependencies
Still needed to finalize

Todd's answers set the design. A few inputs from the sales manager or the product data sharpen it before the build.

  1. The product-development / application-discovery form (or a light version), to lock the exact fields: application, spec or properties, current resin, desired resin.
  2. The territory map (rep → states or regions), to turn on auto-routing instead of routing everything to the sales manager.
  3. ✓ The resin product list and nomenclature. Received. Todd sent the Product Selection Guide, so the bot can recognize a resin by name (see the section above).
  4. ICP / poor-fit criteria: what makes a prospect ideal versus a poor fit, company size, industry, order volume, or anything else the sales manager uses to prioritize. This gates the fit tag above.
  5. Optional: a handful of the most common questions the bot should answer up front (Path C).
