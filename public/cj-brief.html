Recommended behavioral tests
"Walk me through a decision you had to make with incomplete data. What did you capture afterward — and did anyone use it before the next similar decision?"
LOOK FOR → a closed loop. Not just "I documented it" — someone actually used it. If the answer ends at the decision itself, that's a signal.
"Describe a system you built — not a process you documented. What changed because of it, and how would you know if it stopped working?"
LOOK FOR → specificity about mechanism, not outcome. Anyone can claim impact. The person who built it knows how it breaks.
"Tell me about a time a GM or business leader pushed back on your framing of a problem. What happened?"
LOOK FOR → did they hold their ground with evidence, or did they fold? This role has to earn trust with Chase without having formal authority. How they navigate pushback is the whole job.
"Describe a campaign or initiative where the learning compounded into the next one. What specifically did you do to make that happen — not the org, you?"
LOOK FOR → personal agency, not org credit. "We had a good retrospective process" is not the answer. "I built the template and sent it before the next campaign kickoff" is.
"What's the difference between a dashboard and a decision instrument? Give me an example of each from your own work."
LOOK FOR → this is the role's core thesis. If they can't articulate the distinction cleanly — or if their "decision instrument" is just a fancy dashboard — misalignment on what this function is.
"You've been embedded for 30 days. You've found that two GMs are running nearly identical experiments and neither knows about the other. What do you do?"
LOOK FOR → do they go to their manager, go directly to the GMs, or build something that surfaces this automatically? The third answer is the job. The first is politics. The second is courage without infrastructure.
