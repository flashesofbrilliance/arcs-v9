Tradeoffs worth knowing about

Every choice here has a cost on the other side. These are the ones I think you should weigh rather than have me decide quietly.

Asking for a phone number
Requiring it means every lead saves cleanly and your reps can call straight away. It also adds one more question, and some visitors will drop out rather than answer it. Letting people skip it keeps the chat short, but then we fill a placeholder and your team sees a blank where a number should be.
Labeling chatbot leads
If we add a "Chatbot" source you will be able to show, later, how many of these turned into real business. If we use the existing "Web Site" label instead, nothing breaks, but the chatbot's leads blend in with everything else and that number is gone.
Testing in your live system
Without a separate practice environment, proving the connection works means writing one record into the real system. It would be clearly marked and deleted straight after. The alternative is switching it on for real customers without ever having seen it work end to end, which I would not recommend.
Using a connector in the middle
Sending leads through a connector rather than straight into SugarCRM costs a small monthly fee and one extra hop. In exchange, if something fails it retries automatically and leaves a visible record. A direct connection would be cheaper and would drop a failed lead silently, which is the one outcome this project exists to prevent.
Going live on the real site
The bot currently runs on a copy, so nothing customers see has changed. Moving it to prlresins.com is visible immediately. Worth picking a quiet window and having you and your sales manager watch the first day.

Phase 1 · How it works
How the chatbot will work

Based on what you told me, here is the conversation your visitors will have. It stays short so people actually finish it, and it hands your reps a clean, qualified lead.

1
It greets and sorts the visitor
The assistant pops up and asks what brings them in, then offers three quick paths. This screens out the tire-kickers before they reach a rep.
• They know the resin or spec they want
• They need help finding the right resin
• Something else, like a general question
2
It asks only what a rep needs
Depending on the path, it gathers the useful basics: the resin or spec, the application, what they use now, and any requirements. Then name, email, phone, company, and their location. Short and painless, since your buyers usually already have a need.
3
It hands your rep a clean lead
It creates a lead in SugarCRM, emails the notification, and routes it to the right rep by territory (to the sales manager for the first version). The customer hears that someone will reach out within a few hours.
No lost leads: if someone leaves halfway, the assistant remembers them. When they come back it picks up where they left off, so a half-finished form does not become a lost lead.
To finalize: a few inputs from your sales manager complete the picture (see the Discovery section above): the exact fields and what makes a prospect a good fit versus a poor one. (The territory map is in.) Everything else is ready to build whenever you give the go.

Build / Buy / Rent
How to get the chatbot: three ways, in plain English

There are three ways to put a chatbot on your site. Think of it like getting a delivery van for the business:

Build: custom from scratch
Like having a van built to order. Exactly what you want, but the most time, the most money, and you own every repair. Weeks of developer work.
Buy: own a platform license
Like buying the van outright. More control than renting, but a bigger upfront commitment and you're responsible for upkeep.
Rent: subscribe to a ready tool
Like leasing the van. A proven product for a low monthly fee, running in days, upgrades and fixes handled by the vendor. Best fit for the first version.

For the first version, renting is the clear winner. It's fastest to a live result, lowest cost, and nothing to maintain. The real work isn't the software; it's designing the conversation (what the bot asks and where each answer goes). We can always move to Build later if the bigger AI vision needs it.
