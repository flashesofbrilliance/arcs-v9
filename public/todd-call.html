Build / Buy / Rent
How to get the chatbot — three ways, in plain English

There are three ways to put a chatbot on your site. Think of it like getting a delivery van for the business:

Build — custom from scratch
Like having a van built to order. Exactly what you want, but the most time, the most money, and you own every repair. Weeks of developer work.
Buy — own a platform license
Like buying the van outright. More control than renting, but a bigger upfront commitment and you're responsible for upkeep.
Rent — subscribe to a ready tool
Like leasing the van. A proven product for a low monthly fee, running in days, upgrades and fixes handled by the vendor. Best fit for the first version.

For the first version, renting is the clear winner — fastest to a live result, lowest cost, and nothing to maintain. The real work isn't the software; it's designing the conversation (what the bot asks and where each answer goes). We can always move to Build later if the bigger AI vision needs it.

Recommendation — first version
Rent Tidio, install it on WordPress, and connect it directly to SugarCRM
Why Tidio: It installs on your WordPress site in about five minutes. The conversation is built with a drag-and-drop editor — so your team can tweak the questions later without a developer. It remembers returning visitors (which matters for a 6–12 month buying cycle), and it has built-in AI to handle questions the script doesn't cover. It starts around $29/month — the lowest cost and the fastest path to something live.

How leads reach SugarCRM: Tidio can send each new lead straight into SugarCRM automatically — no middle-man tool, no extra monthly fee, one less thing that can break. (If your SugarCRM setup doesn't allow that direct connection, the fallback is a $20/month connector — still cheap.)

Why not build it custom: A custom bot costs far more and takes weeks. The value isn't in the software — it's in designing the right conversation: what the bot asks, and where each answer goes. We nail that on a rented tool now, and can move to something custom later if the bigger AI vision calls for it.

Phase 1 · For Your Sales Manager
Questions to hand your sales manager

These decide what the chatbot asks a visitor and how a lead reaches him — about a 5–10 minute conversation. Copy them as plain text for your email, or just send him this page to refer back to.

What makes a lead worth a rep's time
  1. From the product-development / application-discovery form your reps already use, what are the 1–2 fields that matter most — the minimum you'd want captured for a lead to be worth acting on?
  2. Beyond name / email / phone, is application + resin currently in use the right core to capture, or is something else more telling?
  3. What makes a lead an obvious good fit vs. not worth pursuing? Any hard disqualifiers?
Who's actually asking
  1. Is the person inquiring almost always the engineer, or do you also hear from purchasing / others — and would they need different questions?
  2. How do you tell someone just researching from someone ready for a rep? (If there's a spec sheet or guide we can point researchers to, we'll route them there instead of to a rep.)
Questions & objections, up front
  1. What are the most common questions prospects ask before they'll share their info?
  2. What objections do you hear early, before they're comfortable giving contact details?
Routing & handoff
  1. How should a new lead be routed — by territory / region? What does the chatbot need to capture (e.g., location) so it lands with the right rep?
  2. When a lead reaches you, what format is easiest to act on fast — a SugarCRM lead, an email, both? Any fields you always want filled in?
Speed — optional, sets up a later win
  1. You aim to respond within ~4 hours — should the chatbot set that expectation with the prospect? And is it worth tracking win/loss against callback speed down the road?
The safety net: even if the data takes longer than expected, we still ship the chatbot ($1,500) from what you've already told me. We don't wait for perfect data to get something live and working.
