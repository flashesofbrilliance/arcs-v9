## II. Three inference modes, plus a fourth thing that is not a mode

The three **modes** are Peirce's, and they are inference *forms* (what relation holds between premises and
conclusion):

| mode | function | truth behavior |
|---|---|---|
| **Deduction** | guarantee | truth-preserving; unpacks only what the premises entail; adds no information |
| **Induction** | generalize | ampliative; sample → general; not truth-preserving, but *warranted* by the sample within stated `validityBounds` (ADR-033) |
| **Abduction** | propose | inference to the best explanation; generates new structure; defeasible by design |

The fourth thing is **not a fourth mode** — it is a *presentation-artifact*: **fluent chain-of-thought**
(the modern sense of "AI reasoning"). It can *perform* any of the three modes, or none, and its defect is
specific: it presents a conclusion **without a checkable warrant that the conclusion follows** — neither
deduction's entailment, nor induction's stated bounds, nor abduction's explicit defeasibility and
confidence interval. It reads as certain or insightful while holding none of the properties that would let
you check it. That absence-of-warrant, not "context," is what makes it untrustworthy. This is why an
external check is not optional decoration but the whole safeguard.
