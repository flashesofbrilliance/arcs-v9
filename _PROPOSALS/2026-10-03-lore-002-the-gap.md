# The Gap

*Lore, entry two. What people say, what they do, and the two opposite rules ARCS keeps about the
distance between them.*

> Bracketed `[ZACH: ...]` marks are yours. Every claim about the system cites a file. No
> counterpart is named: the recoil files this draws on describe real people, so only counts appear.

---

## The lede

Everyone tells you what they want. Fewer show you. The distance between the two is the most
honest signal in any room, and ARCS is built around it twice: once as a way to read other people,
and once as a promise never to read you too hard. Those are opposite rules. This is the story of
why both are true.

## I. The clock

The clearest version of this I ever lived is already on the record. Two recruiters called an hour
apart on the same Wednesday. I left the first call ten minutes early, because some part of me could
not get out of the room fast enough. I stayed on the second ten minutes late, because I did not
want to go. (NFTT, *With Life, and Without It*, `public/blog-nftt-take-home-trap.html`.)

I had not decided anything when I left early or stayed late. My body had. The clock knew before I
did which call had someone home. `[ZACH: optional second scene, a time the words and the clock
disagreed and the clock was right. Or wrong; that would be better.]`

## II. The economist's version

The idea has an older name. In 1938 the economist Paul Samuelson proposed that you stop asking
people what they prefer and watch what they choose: preference, revealed by behavior. Economists
called it revealed preference. Everyone else calls it "watch what they do, not what they say."

## III. Two rules that look like one

**Rule one, for reading others: weight the revealed.** Every recoil war room (the pre-call and
post-call workup ARCS builds for a conversation) carries a pair of fields: *Stated, watch* and
*Revealed, weight*. A counterpart who says "I need to loop in the team" is stated. One who stays
past the half hour and asks follow-up questions is revealed. The recoil canvas renders the banked
lesson as a watermark on every card: *"Revealed preference > stated preference."*
(`runtime/recoil/arcs_recoil_canvas.py`; 18 recoil files in `runtime/recoil/generated/` carry the
lens.)

**Rule two, for you: never overrule the revealed.** The first constitutional primitive,
NOTOMATION, says the tools may scaffold and sequence your thinking but must "never compress,
replace, or overrule revealed preference." Its worked example: if the compass reads GREEN on a job
offer and you say it feels wrong, the system records the gate as undefined, not GREEN. "Your lived
experience outranks the inference." (`_CANON/book-ii/part-ii-rom-axioms.md`.)

Read together they seem to collide. With others, ARCS reads the gap and acts on it. With you, ARCS
is forbidden to act on it. The reconciliation is in who holds the pen. Reading a counterpart's
behavior informs *your* decision; you are still the one deciding. Reading *your* behavior and then
deciding for you would make the machine the author of your preference, which is exactly the
replacement the exoskeleton (Lore, entry one) was built not to be.

The two rules are the same rule, seen from either side of the table: **behavior outranks words,
and only the person whose behavior it is gets to say what it means.**

## IV. The open question, now answered on the page

The NOTOMATION spec leaves one question open, in writing: when the operator's stated preference
contradicts their revealed behavior over time, should ARCS surface it, or stay silent until asked?
(`arcs-canon/50-governance/NOTOMATION-behavioral-spec.md`, line 53.)

Entry one already answered it, without meaning to. I state that ARCS should close loops. My revealed
behavior, measured on 2026-10-03, is 261 receipts filed and one real outcome closed. ARCS surfaced
the gap. It did not decide what the gap means.

That is the proposed answer: **surface the gap as an observation, never as a verdict.** Show the
two numbers side by side. Let the person whose numbers they are write the caption.
`[ZACH: accept this as the answer to the spec's open question, or overrule it. If accepted, it
becomes a candidate amendment to the NOTOMATION spec.]`

## V. The crack, and the falsifier

The honest weakness: a revealed read is not an observation. It is an interpretation of one.
"Stayed past thirty minutes" is a fact; "therefore interested" is a guess, and guesses carry the
reader's hopes. A rule that says "trust the revealed" can quietly become "trust my reading of
you," which is just a stated preference wearing a lab coat.

So the claim gets a test:

- **The claim:** in the recoil record, the revealed read predicts what actually happened better
  than the stated read did.
- **The test:** for every recoil file with both a stated and a revealed read *and* a post-call
  close, mark which read the outcome matched. Tally.
- **What would prove it wrong:** if the stated read matches as often as the revealed read, or more,
  the banked lesson is downgraded from law to rule of thumb (LORE verdict `old-wives-tale`), and the
  watermark comes off the canvas.
- **Status:** not yet run. 18 files carry the lens; how many have a usable close has not been
  counted carefully. Until the tally exists, this story is told, not cited.
