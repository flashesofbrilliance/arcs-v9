The unattended door

ARCS can schedule checks, and it can schedule a model's judgment. It cannot schedule a person's judgment. A step that needs a person gets done only when it falls due at a moment someone is already acting, and the largest case in the backlog is a single unclaimed row.

Why

Months of mechanizing protocols (gates, receipts, detectors) were meant to make ARCS close its own loops. This measures which loops actually close, and why the rest do not.

What

A mining pass over the backlog (571 rows in _QUEUE/induced-backlog.jsonl), full git history, the decision-receipt ledger (the record filed for each merge or publish) and 19 pinned artifacts, attacked by the operator panel and cut to a short list of actions.

Why now

About 30 PRs merged in the three days after the last handoff, and the one row that gates 106 held rows has sat unclaimed for 11 days.

How

  1. Before: facts mined in the shell on 11-15, each with the command that re-derives it.
  2. During: three passes of adversarial review on 11-15 broke at least ten claims; each repair is kept in view.
  3. After: Zach rules on the actions below; nothing here is wired yet.
