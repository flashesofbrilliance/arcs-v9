Honest proposals

A public skill that turns a client's ask or an RFP into one engagement record, and renders the reply, the proposal page, a plain-language agreement and a client page block from it, with checks that refuse to send a number without a source, a quote where a range belongs, or a price list before the paid step.

Markdown source: _PROPOSALS/2026-11-08-honest-proposals-design.md

Why

Small proposals go out as three or four hand-typed documents that drift apart the first time a date or price changes, and they tend to oversell. The two engagements done by hand showed the fix is one record that every document renders from, plus a few rules a check enforces before send.

What

A Claude Code skill, honest-proposals. The sibling of the honest resume skill: that one refuses to claim what it cannot defend about a person; this one refuses to promise what it cannot defend about a piece of work.

Why now

The engine is mid-flight. The renderer is 536 lines of stdlib Python and only its self-test names a client. The rendered agreement is still v2 text marked superseded by rev 27, so that path needs rework anyway. No client deadline drives this.

How

Before (now): this design. During (after your go): v0.1 built privately in arcs-v9 on a fictional example. After: a public repo only on a second go, and a "it generalizes" claim only after a third engagement passes the bar in section 7.

How to read: sections 1 to 3 are what it is and who it is for. Section 4 is the core. Sections 5 to 7 are reuse, first release and the bar to generalize. 8 is the home. 9 is what I need from you.

1. What it is

One record in, five things out, four gates before send.

client ask (email, call notes, RFP)
   |
   v
engagement record (one JSON; every value has a source and an append-only history)
   |
   +--> reply (email text)
   +--> proposal page (five altitudes, exec to surgical)
   +--> agreement + Exhibit A (plain language, draft for a lawyer)
   +--> client page block (rendered between markers in an existing page)
   +--> ex-ante receipt (what I expect the client to do, with odds and their basis)

gates: record validator | leak and voice lint | pre-send gauntlet | counsel flag

It does not send, publish or price for you. It refuses to mark the agreement sendable until a lawyer review date is in the record, and refuses to render a client-facing output that fails a gate.

2. Who it is for

  • First: a solo consultant or fractional operator writing a small fixed-fee proposal to a private company that asked for help in an email or on a call. A paid first step, then later steps not yet scoped. This is the only type v0.1 supports.
  • Later: formal RFP responses (with rfp-engine supplying the cited asks), agencies with a procurement counterpart, multi-party agreements.
  • Not for: anyone who wants a persuasive pitch generator. It makes a proposal less impressive and more defensible on purpose.

3. What stays private

Zach's terms, rates, payment rules, state of law, voice and receipt ledger live in a private overlay skill (honest-proposals-zach), the same split as resume-tailor and resume-tailor-zach. The public method ships one fictional worked example. Its figures, step count and dates are invented rather than swapped out of a real engagement, because the shape alone (three steps, a five-day first step, a particular fee) can identify a client.

4. The honesty rules, and how each is enforced

Code means the validator or a lint refuses the output. Gate means a review must pass before send. Human means the skill asks and records the answer but cannot check it.

#RuleEnforced byTodayMy view
1Every number cites a source or is labeled judgmentCode: every stored field needs a source; judgment is a source kind. Add a lint that fails any figure in rendered text that did not come from a cite (rfp-engine's "figures are quoted, never computed", turned on our own output).partlyKeep. The literal-figure lint is the cheap gap closer.
2Ranges with written assumptions, never a quoteCode: phases after the paid step use a money_range field and link an assumption with a test that would decide it.noKeep, refined: the paid first step is a fixed fee. That is the price of finding out, not a quote for the job. A range on a five-day step reads as evasive.
3Reliance disclaimer instead of a sharing banCode: the agreement template carries the clause; a lint fails on ban phrases ("may not share", "do not forward").partlyKeep. The ban backfired in review: the client has to show the work to people who were not in the room.
4No task-level bid sheet before the paid stepCode: leak check. A per-engagement ceiling plus a pattern check for per-task hour or rate tables, on every client-facing output while the step is unpaid.one pageKeep, generalize: ceiling and paid flag move into the record. This is what stops the proposal being shopped to other bidders.
5"Opinion, not legal or compliance advice", "as is", a lawyer reads it onceCode for the wording. Human for the lawyer: counsel_review {date, by_role} must be set before the agreement renders as sendable.wordingKeep. "Once" means once per template version. The public README says the templates follow one US state's habits.
6Ex-ante receipt, scored after the replyGate: ex_ante {expect, p, basis, date} before any send, close after the reply. Public: local JSONL. Overlay: the decision_receipt row.noKeep. Without it there is no way to learn whether honest proposals win or lose more often.
7Gauntlet pass before anything is sentGate: counterpart-gauntlet with a five-seat proposal preset (founder, finance, counsel, skeptic, busy reader). Public: the same seats as a checklist prompt.yesKeep. It caught a narrowed office-hours term and the sharing ban on one engagement (measured, n=1).
8What is not proven yet is said out loudCode: not_proven[] must be non-empty and renders as its own section.noKeep. An empty list is itself a red flag, so it is an error.

Two I would add, both half built

#RuleEnforced by
9A change after sending is shown, never silentCode, already built: once last_sent is set, a changed cited value prints "changed DATE, was X" in every output.
10Plain language, first person singular, no em-dashes, no invented urgencyCode: em-dash and voice lint (the page_gate.py --counterpart check). Human: why_now must name a real trigger.

One I would leave out: "price last". It suits video and calls, but a written proposal reader often scrolls to the price first, and hiding it reads as a sales move.

6. The smallest first release (v0.1)

One engagement type: a fixed-fee paid first step for a private client who asked by email or call, with later steps shown as ranges.

In

  • SKILL.md: intake questions, fill the record, render, lint, gauntlet checklist, ex-ante, stop before send.
  • Schema v1 and the validator.
  • Four outputs: reply, proposal page, agreement, Exhibit A.
  • Four lints: literal figures, sharing-ban phrases, leak ceiling and bid-sheet pattern, voice and em-dash.
  • The counsel flag and the ex-ante requirement.
  • One fictional worked example, and a self-test that fails on a stale output and passes on a fresh one.

Out, each a later backlog row

  • Client page tabs and progress tracking.
  • RFP intake through rfp-engine.
  • PDF output, hosting, passcode gates.
  • Supabase receipts (overlay only).
  • Any second engagement type.

Size, judgment: one to two working sessions, since the renderer and most checks exist and the new work is schema additions, four lints and the example. Basis: the 588 lines already written and this week's build pace. Not measured.

7. What a third engagement must prove before generalizing

Written down now so the count is not bent later. Thresholds are judgment.

  1. Different shape. A different client type or a formal RFP, not another paid-diagnostic-then-build.
  2. Cold use. Run end to end by a session that did not build the skill, from SKILL.md alone.
  3. No hand edits. Zero edits to rendered outputs; every change goes through the record.
  4. Small code change. Renderer changes under about 50 lines and at most three new record keys. More means the schema was fitted to the first client.
  5. Gates earn their keep. Each gate's catches are counted and published, zero included.
  6. The ex-ante is scored. The client replied and the receipt says whether the prediction held.

Pass: move to a plugin with the second engagement type. Fail on 4: fix the schema, not the templates.

8. Where it lives

Recommendation: a new public repo, honest-proposals, as a Claude Code skill, built first inside arcs-v9. Not a skill inside rfp-engine.

The reason is rfp-engine's own contract: requests to draft proposal language or decide whether to bid are refused before any model call, enforced in code. That refusal is what makes it trustworthy as a reader of solicitations. A drafting skill in the same repo would break that rule or sit beside it as an exception, and both weaken what it is known for. They should be neighbours: rfp-engine reads the RFP and returns cited asks; honest-proposals takes cited asks and writes an offer. A README link each way, and later an intake adapter, joins them without merging their promises.

A plugin is premature: there is one skill with one engagement type. Revisit at the third instance.

Not proven yet

  • That honest proposals win more often, or lose less painfully, than ordinary ones. There is no outcome data; rule 6 exists to start collecting it.
  • That the schema fits a second engagement type. Only one engagement used the code.
  • That the agreement template is sound outside the one state it was drafted for.
  • That the fictional example is far enough from a real client that nobody can tell. It needs a skeptic read before it goes public.
